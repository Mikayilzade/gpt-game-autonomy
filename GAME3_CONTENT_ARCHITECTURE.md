This file is the canonical Phase-5 content specification for Borrowed Collision. It defines campaign shape, teaching order, authored data contracts, thematic families, complexity ceilings, solvability/validation, anti-repetition rules, demo scope, mastery/remix policy and the hard 1.0 content ceiling. It does not add a new impact property, transform family, receiver family, combat system, free-angle physics or inventory layer.

When content convenience conflicts with `GAME3_MECHANICAL_ARCHITECTURE.md`, the mechanical architecture wins unless Phase 4 is explicitly reopened.

---

# 1. Content contract

Borrowed Collision must become deeper because the same small consequence-transfer grammar creates different causal problems, not because every case introduces another force type or bespoke gimmick.

All shippable 1.0 content follows these rules:

1. **Portable impacts remain direction + magnitude + lineage only.** No elemental, rarity, durability or special-effect token families.
2. **The world that created and receives an impact matters.** Detached arrow-to-socket matching is invalid mature content.
3. **Cases are authored deterministic causal systems.** They may use multiple valid solutions, but every shipped case has known validated completion evidence.
4. **Difficulty rises through causal distance and state interaction.** More transforms, bodies, donor regeneration classes, receiver aftermath, self-launch, moving windows and chain consequences are preferred over new primitives.
5. **No reflex capture.** Harvest outputs remain stable pickups; moving interactions use canonical movement-step windows.
6. **No one-off collision scripts.** Every body/device instance maps to Phase-4 data-driven semantics and R1–R8 families.
7. **Strong is never the universal answer.** Mature content must repeatedly make WEAK/MEDIUM choices materially useful.
8. **Undo is learning, not score.** Mastery evaluates final causal structure/state, not raw experiment history.
9. **Theme cannot rewrite physics.** Visual nouns may change; canonical body, donor, receiver and transform semantics do not.
10. **Content must remain readable on controller/Deck.** Dense case design cannot assume hover, tiny free-space trajectories or four simultaneous mini-rooms.

# 2. 1.0 campaign shape

## 2.1 Frozen planning target

**34 authored main campaign cases** is the Phase-5 canonical target.

Allowed pre-freeze production adjustment: **32–36** only if whole-game simulation/playtest pacing later proves the exact count should move. The default campaign plan and progression graph assume 34.

Target first clear remains roughly **5–8 hours**. The game should not need 60–100 micro-puzzles to justify value.

## 2.2 Five-act structure

### Act I — Stored Consequence, C01–C07
Purpose: prove that a real collision becomes a portable resource and make direction/magnitude readable without vector language.

Ceilings:
- 1 room or tightly connected single-screen packet;
- 2–4 active bodies;
- 1–2 donor sources;
- 1 portable impact in ordinary use, inventory capacity still mechanically 2;
- 1 transform family visible at a time;
- 1–2 required predicates;
- chain depth target <=2 collision events after spend;
- no moving receivers;
- no CHAIN_GENERATED donor requirement before C07;
- no 3-token capacity.

### Act II — Scarcity and Suitability, C08–C14
Purpose: make provenance, magnitude windows, regeneration and receiver aftermath matter.

Ceilings:
- 1–2 rooms;
- 3–6 active bodies;
- 2–3 donors;
- 2-token inventory becomes normal;
- quarter-turn/reverse/mirror/damper all may appear after teaching;
- 2–3 required predicates;
- chain depth target <=4;
- first moving receiver late in act;
- RESETTABLE and CYCLIC_WEAK donors introduced;
- self-launch becomes normal rather than novelty.

### Act III — Consequence Chains, C15–C21
Purpose: make one spent impact alter future donor/receiver topology and create secondary harvestable consequences.

Ceilings:
- 2–3 connected rooms;
- 4–8 active bodies;
- 3–4 donors;
- 2 held impacts, occasional third world pickup pressure but no 3-slot inventory yet;
- 3–4 required predicates/invariants total;
- chain depth target <=7;
- moving receivers and CHAIN_GENERATED donors normal;
- multiple donor regeneration classes may coexist;
- first proven-dead-end cases allowed, always recoverable by Undo/Restart.

### Act IV — Causal Routing, C22–C28
Purpose: force source selection, transform topology, self/cargo allocation and regeneration logic to interact across rooms.

Ceilings:
- 2–4 rooms, never an open traversal world;
- 6–10 active bodies;
- 3–5 donors;
- baseline 2-slot inventory, selected cases may unlock/use 3-slot capacity only from C26 onward;
- 3–5 required predicates/invariants;
- chain depth target <=10, hard authored validation <=16;
- up to 3 moving receivers/windows in one case if states remain inspectable;
- no more than 2 meaningful transform devices in one required chain unless the transformation itself is the case's central lesson.

### Act V — Borrowed Collision Department, C29–C34
Purpose: demonstrate mastery with the existing grammar rather than introduce a final mechanic.

Ceilings:
- 3–5 compact connected rooms;
- 7–12 active bodies;
- 4–6 donor sources;
- 3-slot inventory permitted only where it creates a real ordering problem; 2 remains preferred;
- 4–6 required predicates/invariants maximum;
- typical required causal chain <=12 collision/transform/spend material events;
- hard chain ceiling remains Phase-4 24 movement steps; authored known solutions should normally stay materially below it;
- all donor classes, moving receivers, self-launch and R1–R8 may recombine;
- no new transform, token type or receiver family.

C34 is a synthesis case, not a boss with bespoke rules.

# 3. Teaching / introduction order

The campaign teaches the complete primitive grammar early enough that later progression can rely on composition.

## C01 — Collision becomes portable
One STANDARD donor collision emits a WEAK impact pickup. Player carries it to R1 Ordinary Mover.

Lesson: `the crash happened there; its consequence can be used here`.

## C02 — Direction identity
Two receivers make direction matter. No converter yet.

Lesson: arrow orientation is a physical property of the stored hit, not an aim reticle.

## C03 — Quarter-turn converter
Introduce R4 quarter-turn socket.

Lesson: direction changes only through visible fixed machinery.

## C04 — Magnitude bands
WEAK/MEDIUM contrast with R3 Band-Window Mechanism. Stronger is not yet shown as harmful; first establish category consistency.

## C05 — Fragility / strong can be wrong
R2 Fragile Receiver makes STRONG undesirable while MEDIUM is correct.

Lesson: magnitude is suitability, not power score.

## C06 — Reverse / mirror
Introduce reverse; mirror may be introduced here or C07 depending readability. Never introduce both mirror axes in the same first teaching beat.

## C07 — Secondary collision
Spend one impact on R7 Chain-Producing Body and harvest a genuinely new downstream collision lineage.

Lesson: a borrowed consequence can create another real consequence.

## C08 — Two-token ordering
Inventory capacity 2 becomes explicit and useful.

## C09 — Exhaustible provenance
EXHAUSTIBLE donor. Player sees an apparently useful early spend that consumes a source needed later.

## C10 — RESETTABLE donor
Visible meaningful reset action; reset changes route/world state rather than acting as a free ammo button.

## C11 — Damper
Introduce R5. STRONG -> MEDIUM solves a fragile/window requirement.

## C12 — Self-launch
R6 becomes a core receiver; player is part of the same impact grammar.

## C13 — CYCLIC_WEAK donor
Renewable weak source introduced specifically to contrast scarcity classes. It may never be escalated into infinite strong generation through a converter loop.

## C14 — Moving receiver
First canonical receive-window case with Pause/Step available. No reflex requirement.

## C15 onward — no new primitive grammar
From C15, all content must derive novelty from composition: source choice, regeneration, magnitude suitability, transform topology, chain generation, moving state, self/cargo allocation, inventory pressure and causal compression.

## 3.1 Mirror teaching lock
If both horizontal and vertical mirror exist in 1.0 content, they share one `MIRROR` family and are introduced through explicit visible axes. The player is never expected to infer mirror orientation from icon color or room art.

- `fixed_orientation_or_axis`

## 4.8 `ObjectiveContent`
Fields:
- `objective_id`
- `family_id` from Phase-4 objective registry
- `subject_ids[]`
- `target_ids[]`
- `predicate_parameters`
- `evaluation_timing`
- `required`
- `player_visible_text_token`
- `status_explanation_token`

## 4.9 `KnownSolutionFixture`
Stores validation evidence, never an in-game answer oracle:
- exact content/rules version
- initial canonical state hash
- ordered semantic commands
- expected major lineage events
- expected completion predicates
- expected final hash or equivalent canonical state assertions
- `solution_character_tags[]`

At least one baseline fixture is mandatory for every main case and remix.

# 5. Thematic presentation families

Themes exist to create memorable physical nouns while preserving exact mechanics.

## T1 — Freight Annex
Visual nouns: carts, crates, shutters, loading arms, rails, counterweights.
Best for: Ordinary Movers, Band Windows, fragile cargo, exhaustible impacts.

## T2 — Clockwork Works
Visual nouns: punch mechanisms, latches, flywheels, gates, rotating socket housings.
Best for: converters, dampers, RESETTABLE sources, moving receive windows.

## T3 — Salvage Yard
Visual nouns: suspended scrap, compactors, bumpers, reclaim rails, broken machinery.
Best for: strong/weak tradeoffs, CHAIN_GENERATED donors, breakage, causal reuse.

## T4 — Transit Test Hall
Visual nouns: test sleds, passenger pods, launch rails, safety doors, moving platforms.
Best for: self-launch, moving receivers, multi-room transport, late causal routing.

### Theme rule
A themed `glass courier pod` and `ceramic actuator` may both present R2 fragility, but the rules remain the same. Theme-specific exception scripts are forbidden.

Four themes are enough for 1.0. A fifth theme may replace one before freeze, not simply increase art burden.

# 6. Objective / invariant distribution

Phase 4 defined ten objective/mastery predicate families. Phase 5 groups their use into content roles rather than inventing new predicates.

## Early emphasis — C01–C07
- move/activate target body/device;
- preserve one receiver/body state;
- use a required collision lineage concept where tutorial needs it;
- simple final-position/activation predicates.

Maximum required clauses: **2**.

## Act II — C08–C14
Add:
- preserve donor availability;
- satisfy magnitude/safe-band conditions;
- maintain non-broken state;
- complete self/cargo positioning;
- bounded moving-window outcome.

Maximum required clauses: **3**.

## Act III — C15–C21
Add:
- chain consequence requirements;
- multi-room final-state conjunctions;
- explicit protected invariants that make local solutions incomplete;
- source/receiver state interactions.

Maximum required clauses/invariants: **4**.

## Act IV — C22–C28
- 3–5 total required clauses/invariants;
- at least one provenance/world-state condition in every mature case;
- no more than two simultaneously moving required targets unless the case is specifically about moving-window scheduling.

## Act V — C29–C34
Maximum: **6** player-facing required clauses/invariants, preferably grouped into 3–4 coherent requirements. Content must not turn the case rail into a debug checklist.

### Invariant philosophy
Protected invariants exist to make locally good impacts incomplete. They must be visible before the relevant decision and explainable after breakage. Ordinary experimentation may break them temporarily and recover through Undo/continued play unless the case becomes a proven dead end.

# 7. Multi-room and state-density ceilings

These are hard authoring guardrails for 1.0 unless explicitly reopened before freeze.

## 7.1 Rooms
- C01–C07: max 1 primary room plus tiny transition alcove.
- C08–C14: max 2 rooms.
- C15–C21: max 3 rooms.
- C22–C28: max 4 rooms.
- C29–C34: max 5 compact rooms.

A `room` is a causal/traversal unit, not a decorative camera zone. Splitting one huge room into five scene nodes does not bypass the ceiling.

## 7.2 Active bodies
- early target <=4;
- mid target <=8;
- absolute 1.0 case ceiling **12 active canonical bodies**.

Decorative moving props do not enter the domain simulation.

## 7.3 Donors
Absolute simultaneous donor-source ceiling: **6**.

No more than **2 RESETTABLE donors** in one case unless reset interaction is the declared dominant reasoning transformation.

No more than **1 CYCLIC_WEAK source** in ordinary campaign cases.

## 7.4 Portable pickups
World + inventory simultaneously visible portable impacts: target <=4, hard ceiling **6**. A case that floods the room with stored arrows violates the product identity and UI readability.

## 7.5 Receivers
Target <=6 mechanically relevant receivers in ordinary mature cases; hard ceiling **9**, excluding transform devices.

## 7.6 Transform devices
Hard ceiling **5** physical transform devices in a case. More than 3 in one required solution chain requires explicit content-review justification.

## 7.7 Chain depth
Authoring metric `material_chain_depth` counts causal material events, not movement-animation frames.
- early expected <=4;
- mid <=8;
- late expected <=12;
- >12 requires adversarial clarity review;
- no known main-campaign solution may rely on reaching the Phase-4 hard 24-step safety ceiling.

## 7.8 Required moving windows
No case may require more than **3 moving receive-window interactions** for baseline completion. Only one of them may demand ordered interaction across multiple rooms in ordinary campaign content.

# 8. Solvability and deterministic validation

Every shippable case passes static, dynamic and adversarial validation.

## 8.1 Static structural validation
Reject content with:
- duplicate stable IDs;
- missing body/lane/boundary/reference IDs;
- body on invalid initial node;
- unsupported mechanical family composition;
- receiver family outside R1–R8;
- transform outside four canonical families;
- donor regeneration class outside four canonical classes;
- malformed RESETTABLE/CYCLIC contracts;
- collision boundary with undefined simultaneous grouping;
- receiver window tied to presentation/interpolation time;
- inventory capacity >3;
- active body/donor/receiver/room/transform counts above Phase-5 ceilings;
- objective reference to nonexistent state;
- chain-authority graph with an explicit reachable loop.

## 8.2 Initial-state validation
Every case declares intentionally:
- which baseline goals begin false/true;
- which invariants begin true;
- which donors are initially available/spent/resettable;
- which world pickups exist;
- whether any body begins moving;
- whether case begins already dead-ended (normally forbidden outside a teaching demonstration).

Accidental already-complete cases fail validation.

## 8.3 Known solution proof
Every main/remix case stores at least one exact-version known baseline solution fixture. Fixture must demonstrate:
- every committed command legal;
- deterministic replay;
- required lineage uniqueness;
- expected donor regeneration;
- no hidden mid-transaction input;
- no use of illegal free-angle behavior;
- final objective/invariant truth.

Late cases should store at least **two materially distinct baseline solutions** where reasonably feasible. This is a content quality target, not a universal mathematical requirement.

## 8.4 Canonical replay
Replaying the same fixture from exact initial content/rules version must reproduce:
- emitted impact directions/bands;
- lineage ancestry;
- receiver/breakage outcomes;
- objective transitions;
- final canonical state/hash.

Presentation speed, input device and locale may not change fixture results.

# 9. Automated exploit / quality checks

## 9.1 Lineage uniqueness
Validator/test harness asserts:
- one harvest output max per collision lineage;
- transforms preserve source lineage;
- spent impact cannot reappear as same identity;
- Undo restores branch state rather than duplicating both branches.

## 9.2 Donor regeneration loop detection
Build-time bounded search flags:
- RESETTABLE donor whose reset has zero meaningful state/route cost;
- any reachable loop generating net additional STRONG impacts without consuming or changing a bounded world resource/state;
- CYCLIC_WEAK path that converter/chain composition escalates into renewable MEDIUM/STRONG output.

Flagged cases require manual review or fail automatically where the net-positive loop is proven.

## 9.3 Strong-force dominance
For representative/mature known and solver-found solutions, record the meaningful-choice bands used.

A case is automatically flagged if:
- every known solution simply uses the strongest available impact for all relevant receivers;
- weaker bands exist but never create a strategic advantage;
- STRONG behaves as a generic key rather than a situational consequence.

Across C15–C34, no consecutive **three-case window** may have STRONG as the principal successful band choice in all three.

## 9.4 Detached token-permutation test
Each mature case receives internal `provenance_relevance_note` and is reviewed under the counterfactual:

> If impacts were handed to the player at case start with their directions/bands unchanged, and donor world state were removed, would the primary challenge remain essentially the same?

If **YES**, the case fails mature-content review unless it is an explicit early tutorial about token identity/transforms.

From C15 onward, every case must depend materially on at least one of:
- donor exhaustion/reset state;
- source generation sequence;
- receiver aftermath;
- chain-generated consequence;
- self/cargo allocation;
- multi-room transport caused by world topology;
- moving receiver timing state;
- protected invariant altered by source/spend world consequences.

## 9.5 Ambiguous multi-contact
Static + replay validation rejects any authored state where the same canonical step can produce multiple mechanically plausible collision groupings without an explicit simultaneous relation/group rule.

No case may rely on scene order, body ID iteration or animation placement to decide which impact exists.

## 9.6 Chain-loop invalidity
Reachable deterministic loops that continue motion indefinitely or until the hard chain safety ceiling are invalid shippable content unless the loop is guaranteed to settle through a finite canonical state transition before the ceiling. The hard ceiling is crash protection, not a puzzle timer.

# 10. Anti-repetition reasoning transformations

Every case receives one dominant reasoning-transformation tag and optional secondary tags from this frozen Phase-5 list:

1. `SOURCE_SELECTION` — choose which collision/donor to preserve/create.
2. `DIRECTION_TOPOLOGY` — use fixed transforms/routes rather than free rotation.
3. `MAGNITUDE_SUITABILITY` — weak/medium/strong tradeoffs, fragility/window/overshoot.
4. `REGENERATION_ECONOMY` — exhaustible/resettable/cyclic donor availability.
5. `CHAIN_CREATION` — engineer a secondary collision whose new lineage is the key resource.
6. `SELF_VS_WORLD_ALLOCATION` — spend on player traversal versus cargo/environment.
7. `MOVING_WINDOW_TIMING` — canonical movement-step synchronization without twitch timing.
8. `INVENTORY_ORDERING` — 2/3-token capacity and pickup/spend ordering.
9. `PROTECTED_STATE_TRADEOFF` — local progress threatens a required invariant/world state.
10. `CAUSAL_COMPRESSION` — one engineered consequence replaces several obvious actions/donors.

### Campaign diversity rules
- C01–C14 teaching cases may repeat a tag when introducing vocabulary.
- From C15 onward, no consecutive 3-case window may share the same dominant tag on all three.
- From C15 onward, every 5-case window must contain at least **3 distinct dominant tags**.
- No 4-case window from C22 onward may contain only direction/magnitude matching tags (`DIRECTION_TOPOLOGY`, `MAGNITUDE_SUITABILITY`) without a provenance/world-state tag.
- Act V must include at least one dominant case each for `CHAIN_CREATION`, `REGENERATION_ECONOMY` or `PROTECTED_STATE_TRADEOFF`, `SELF_VS_WORLD_ALLOCATION`, and `CAUSAL_COMPRESSION`.

A changed room theme or reversed arrow direction does not count as a different reasoning transformation.

# 13. Mastery architecture

Mastery must reward understanding of causal structure, not punishment for experimentation.

Base mastery families:

## M1 — Causal Compression
Finish with a specified final causal footprint, e.g. use no more than N distinct donor generations or create a named useful chain instead of consuming multiple obvious sources.

This is **not** raw button press/edit count.

## M2 — Preservation
Complete while preserving a visible optional world condition:
- donor remains unspent;
- fragile receiver/body remains intact;
- optional cargo route remains valid;
- resettable mechanism remains in safe state.

## M3 — Resource Discipline
Complete while ending with a transparent bounded resource condition, e.g. preserve one specific scarce source or avoid consuming STRONG when a weaker chain is sufficient.

## M4 — Stable Causality
Complete through an authored bounded moving-window/chain outcome with an optional stricter final state, never by waiting through arbitrary cycles.

### Mastery rules
- baseline ending never requires mastery;
- Undo/Redo count never disqualifies mastery;
- elapsed time is not a default mastery metric;
- input device/accessibility options never invalidate mastery merely because they are used;
- every mastery instance needs internal `mastery_distinction_note` explaining what different insight it rewards;
- no case should carry more than **2** mastery contracts in campaign; remixes may use up to 3.

# 14. Remix / optional content

Working 1.0 target: **10 remix/mastery cases**, within Product Thesis 8–12 range.

Use two packs of five:

## Pack A — Reclaimed Impacts, RM01–RM05
Unlock around completion of C14 / Act II.
Uses early substrates with changed donor regeneration, receiver aftermath or source ordering. No moving-window/late complexity requirement.

## Pack B — Second Consequences, RM06–RM10
Unlock after C34 baseline clear.
Expert cases may use all frozen grammar within hard ceilings.

### Remix rule
Every remix declares:
- `source_substrate_id`
- `changed_inputs[]`
- `changed_causal_dependency`
- `dominant_reasoning_tag`
- `why_source_solution_no_longer_transfers`

A remix fails if it merely:
- reverses arrow directions;
- tightens a magnitude threshold;
- moves a donor one room over;
- increases body count;
- reduces mastery count;
while preserving the same causal insight.

No daily rotation, procedural endless remix or time-limited content is required.

# 16. Content production risks

## C5-R1 — Detached arrow matching: HIGH
Mitigation: provenance counterfactual test mandatory from C15; every mature case needs source/receiver world-state dependency.

## C5-R2 — Validation/QA state explosion: HIGH

## C5-R8 — Theme repetition: MEDIUM
Mitigation: four presentation families rotate physical nouns while reasoning tags prevent cosmetic relabel from counting as variety.
