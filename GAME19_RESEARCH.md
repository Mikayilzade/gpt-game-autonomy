## 1. September 2026 market read
Fresh research was used because discovery conditions, recent releases and demo competition materially affect concept selection.

Current signals:
- Steam discovery is exceptionally crowded. June 2026 Next Fest reporting ranged from roughly 4,200 to nearly 5,000 tracked demos, while GamesRadar reported 8,682 demo listings visible during the event depending on counting/filtering method. The exact count is less important than the direction: discovery pressure is extreme.
- SakujoData's June-2026 analysis reported a highly concentrated outcome: the top ~4% of games captured nearly 70% of modeled wishlist gains. A merely competent concept is therefore insufficient; Game #019 needs a hook that reads before genre explanation.
- Recent 2026 indie coverage remains dense with cozy/life simulation, horror, roguelike hybrids, survival/crafting, action, narrative exploration and genre mashups.
- Puzzle products still have room when the interaction itself is distinctive and demonstrable. Blue Prince remains a strong modern reference for systemic puzzle identity, while Order of the Sinking Star shows that highly authored puzzle depth can still attract attention.
- Demo performance matters more than passive trailer novelty. Current festival analysis suggests played demos correlate strongly with wishlist gains, strengthening the factory rule that the central surprise should become playable within minutes.
- Co-op, dark-fantasy action and extraction-style products can generate visibility, but networking, content and balance burden remain disproportionate to this factory's small-team/implementation-handoff constraints.

Fresh sources checked on 2026-09-06:

- PC Gamer, “The best indie games on PC” (updated 2026-09-04).

Research conclusion:
**Do not optimize for a trendy genre label. Optimize for a mechanically legible 10-second proof, a 20–35 minute demo with a real second-order discovery, bounded production and enough systemic recombination for several hours.**

## 4. Broad clean-slate field — 26 concepts

### C01 — ROOM TONE
A tiny building emits visible “activity noise” through walls. You place only absorbers, resonators and vents; occupants are static public rule nodes. Goal is not silence but to sculpt which rooms hear which combinations.
Depth: interference by path/material category, not wave physics.
10-second proof: one wall panel makes the nursery stop hearing the boiler while the studio hears it more.
Risk: could drift toward #004 if hidden audio geometry becomes deduction. Must remain fully visible transmission construction.

### C02 — THE THIRD COPY
A document desk has only carbon-paper-like copy layers. Stamping/writing one sheet deterministically marks selected sheets beneath depending on stack order and separator types. Build several different final documents from a tiny shared operation sequence.
Depth: fan-out, masking and stack order.
Demo: one stamp creates three different visible marks across a layered stack.
Risk: flat-paper manipulation can collide with #009 if folding/geometry appears.

### C03 — BORROWED FOOTPRINT
Characters cannot move objects; they can only step into each other's footprints. Every step leaves a temporary tile of a public movement type (jump, slide, climb), and another character may consume it once.
Depth: route construction through disposable affordances.
Demo: first character crosses uselessly, second uses the trail as a movement language.
Risk: generic pathfinding if footprint types do not create conceptual constraints.

### C04 — WHAT THE WALL KEEPS
A wall remembers only the last thing touching each of its sides: heat, wetness, pressure or scent. You arrange brief contact sequences so stored surface states later affect passing objects.
Depth: two-sided surface memory and sequencing.
Demo: press a cold tray to one side; later the opposite side condenses moisture onto a seed packet.
Risk: may resemble persistent-state portfolio concepts unless identity stays specifically boundary/surface storage.

### C05 — TABLE FOR NONE
An empty banquet is served to a sequence of automatic table mechanisms, not people. Plates, lazy susans, cloth folds and serving arms obey local placement rules. You set only the initial place settings and watch courses redistribute.
Depth: local mechanical propagation with embodied comedy.
Demo: one missing plate causes three serving arms to reroute dishes.
Risk: adjacency puzzle can collapse toward killed #018 runner-up ONE MORE CHAIR; must differ by mechanism propagation, no guest etiquette.

### C06 — PUBLIC SECRET
Every object has two fully visible meanings depending on which public signboard currently faces the room: “museum,” “workshop,” “kitchen,” etc. The player rotates a few room-context boards; unchanged objects gain different legal functions under that context.
Depth: contextual affordance grammar.
Demo: same glass case is DISPLAY in museum context, HEAT_SHIELD in workshop context.
Risk: semantic relabeling collision with #002/#010. Likely kill unless rules are physicalized strongly.

### C07 — DRY RUN
A miniature water system has no continuous fluid. Pipes hold discrete “wet/dry” states; running a pump marks a connected path wet, and later operations react differently to already-wet vs dry segments.
Depth: path history, priming and one-way valves.
Demo: first pump run seems wasteful but primes exactly the route required by the second.
Risk: generic network-flow puzzle / portfolio persistence overlap.

### C08 — IN THE MARGIN
You place annotations only in margins around a central diagram. Each annotation projects a simple bounded transformation inward (rotate symbols in row, invert one category, mirror a region). Notes can overlap; objective is a final diagram.
Depth: composition from outside the object.
Demo: four notes around a map transform different regions without touching the center.
Risk: abstract puzzle presentation.

### C09 — NIGHT SHIFT SWITCHBOARD
A switchboard operator cannot connect endpoints directly. Every patch cable changes the role of the two sockets it leaves unused next to it. Build a small communication pattern where connectivity and local side-effects coexist.
Depth: placement externalities, not generic graph routing.
Demo: making one call possible turns neighboring ports from INPUT to OUTPUT.
Risk: may feel like another network puzzle if visual metaphor is weak.

### C10 — HOUSEPLANT WEATHER STATION
You do not care for plants. Instead plants are fixed sensors/actuators: fern opens when humid, succulent stores heat, ivy shades neighbors. You reposition only a few blinds/trays/fans between discrete “weather pulses” to produce a requested sensor signature.
Depth: coupled environment logic.
Demo: one blind cools one pot and indirectly opens humidity for another.
Risk: can look like cozy gardening; must sell as compact climate logic.

### C11 — LAST PERSON OUT
Each room has a public “close-down” rule performed by the final person leaving it: turn off light, lock cabinet, push chair, carry cup, etc. You choose departure order, not actions. Goal is a final building state.
Depth: ordering through end-of-occupancy triggers.
Demo: changing who exits the kitchen last alters three later room states.
Risk: NPC behavior opacity; rules must be tiny and explicit.

### C12 — HALF-LIFE OF A SIGN
Signs physically fade by one discrete layer whenever their rule is obeyed. A strong sign may command several actions before disappearing; weaker signs expose a different instruction beneath.
Depth: consumable rule layers.
Demo: “KEEP LEFT” is obeyed twice, peels away, revealing “STOP.”
Risk: could become program-step manipulation like #011 if too abstract.

### C13 — SMALL CLAIMS COURT
Objects occupy contested spaces; each has public precedence clauses such as “book yields to cup, cup yields to lamp.” You place one new object and the room resolves all pairwise claims simultaneously to stable positions.
Depth: partial-order displacement and nonlocal cascades.
Demo: adding a mug ejects a book, which displaces a key, while a lamp stays.
Risk: property/relational semantics may feel too abstract; avoid bureaucracy fiction despite title.

### C14 — WRONG-SIZE ELEVATOR
An elevator does not move between floors; instead its interior scale changes among three discrete sizes. Objects/doorways remain fixed in world space, so changing cabin scale changes which cargo arrangements fit and which exit can be reached.
Depth: scale-relative packing and access.
Demo: shrink cabin around a large crate so a small hatch aligns with its top.
Risk: geometry implementation risk; must use discrete authored states, not free physics.

### C15 — THE RECEIPT IS THE MAP
A shop prints a receipt whose line spacing, subtotal dividers and item order physically become a later navigation map. You choose purchase/order grouping to author the map, then traverse it.
Depth: one system's output is another system's topology without changing topology in play.
Demo: combining two cheap items moves a receipt divider, opening a route in the second scene.
Risk: can become gimmicky two-game structure; first and second halves must stay tightly coupled.

### C16 — DOORFRAME
You cannot move doors or rooms. You insert one of several frame inserts that redefine how objects crossing a doorway are transformed: rotate, mirror orientation, swap front/back tag, duplicate only a marker.
Depth: reusable boundary transforms and route composition.
Demo: carry one asymmetric object through two differently framed doors to satisfy orientation.
Risk: property transfer / topology collision if frames alter object properties too broadly.

### C17 — THE GOOD SIDE
Every object has two public sides but only one may face a process at a time. Processes do not modify the object; they modify whichever side is exposed. You can flip only whole trays containing several objects.
Depth: coupled orientation and selective exposure.
Demo: one tray flip protects finished faces while exposing unfinished ones.
Risk: strong collision with #015 masking/exposure. Kill.

### C18 — QUIET HOURS
Machines may run only when the sum of their public nuisance categories heard by each neighbor is below a threshold. You schedule no clock time; you choose concurrent machine groups. Walls/doors are fixed.
Depth: subset composition over overlapping local constraints.
Demo: three individually legal machines become illegal together in one apartment, but four can run if grouped differently.
Risk: may become dry bin-packing.

### C19 — TAKE A NUMBER, LEAVE A SHAPE
A dispenser outputs numbered physical tickets whose cut shape depends on which template is installed. Downstream machines care independently about number and shape. You choose a few template swaps while ticket numbers advance automatically.
Depth: two orthogonal identities generated by one sequence.
Demo: ticket 7 must be triangle for one gate but later ticket 9 rectangle for another.
Risk: queue/number imagery collides with #017 unless no agents/diagnosis; theme should likely change.

### C20 — COLD STORAGE
Crates occupy discrete thermal bands in a freezer. Opening a compartment does not advance time; it swaps a bounded heat token between neighboring compartments. Goods have public irreversible thresholds. Goal is to redistribute thermal exposure without “cooling everything.”
Depth: token redistribution plus threshold sequencing.
Demo: warming one safe crate gives exactly enough cold to save two fragile ones.
Risk: conserved-resource collision with #005 if modeled as a strict global conservation puzzle.

### C21 — ONE-SIDED CONVERSATION
A set of simple mechanical callers each speaks only if the previous audible message matches a public category; you can place acoustic tubes that route one message to one recipient. No hidden dialogue or AI.
Depth: causal message routing and conditional activation.
Demo: “RED” spoken to one caller makes it answer “CIRCLE,” which wakes another.
Risk: graph routing / audio theme collision; keep visible token language.

### C22 — OUT OF ORDER
A repair bench displays four intact machines, but you are allowed to remove parts only, never add them. Removed parts become physical blockers/templates/tools for later machines, while every repaired machine must still meet a reduced target function.
Depth: subtraction as planning without fabrication cuts.
Demo: remove a fan guard from machine A; it becomes the gauge needed to calibrate B.
Risk: collision with #016 by “removed part becomes tool.” Likely kill.

### C23 — POSTMARK
Mail pieces pass through a fixed sequence of public stamping stations. A stamp's meaning depends on which other stamp already exists nearby on the envelope, producing a small visual grammar of compound marks.
Depth: order-sensitive compositional marking.
Demo: blue stamp alone means HOLD; next to red it becomes RELEASE.
Risk: can resemble generic transformation/programming puzzle; needs tactile presentation.

### C24 — THE ROOM BORROWS A FLOOR
Rooms have fixed geometry but each may temporarily borrow one of three floor materials stored in adjacent closets: ice, carpet, grate. The material changes how already-moving autonomous objects complete their current movement rule.
Depth: environment-mediated motion, discrete not physics-heavy.
Demo: same rolling cart stops, slides or drops depending on borrowed floor.
Risk: material-property transfer collision with #003 if treated as object property; floor must stay environmental/contextual.

### C25 — WINDOW OF OPPORTUNITY
A compact greenhouse/workshop has sliding shutters with cutout shapes. You do not move the light; you move shutters between fixed layers so only selected process markers receive a public “exposed” token on each discrete pulse.
Depth: layered aperture composition.
Demo: two harmless shutters overlap into a precise three-site exposure pattern.
Risk: shadow/masking collision with #014/#015. Kill.

### C26 — AFTER THE BELL
A school/workshop bell does not mark time. Ringing it commits all currently staged objects to their next public role: desks become barriers, carts become stations, trays become carriers. You rearrange a few objects between bells; roles advance cyclically but object history does not rewind.
Depth: synchronized role-phase transformation.
Demo: one bell turns your blocking cart into the only delivery platform.
Risk: cyclic phase can drift toward #011 if presented as program timing; keep embodied role conversion.

## 6. Round-A retained field — 12 equal-format briefs

### A1 — WRONG-SIZE ELEVATOR (C14)
Hook: resize the elevator cabin, not the cargo, so the same fixed shaft connects to different-sized exits and packing arrangements.
Core interaction: choose one of three discrete cabin scales, reposition a few cargo pieces in socketed slots, commit.
Depth source: relative scale changes access, fit, adjacency and which cargo faces align with fixed hatches.
10-second proof: cabin shrinks; a crate that filled the floor now becomes a platform beneath a tiny side hatch.
Primary failure hypothesis: discrete scale states may still create expensive visual/geometry edge cases or read as arbitrary.

### A2 — LAST PERSON OUT (C11)
Hook: you choose who leaves each room last; the last person automatically performs that room's public close-down rule.
Core interaction: route/sequence departures through a tiny building with explicit room-trigger cards.
Depth source: exit order, carried objects and persistent room state compose across rooms.
10-second proof: swapping two departure paths makes one person lock a cabinet while another carries the needed cup onward.
Primary failure hypothesis: too much behavioral bookkeeping or apparent NPC arbitrariness.

### A3 — THE THIRD COPY (C02)
Hook: stack paper, carbon layers and separators so one physical stamp writes different required documents at once.
Core interaction: reorder a bounded stack, place separators/masks, apply one of a few shared marks.
Depth source: fan-out, layer selectivity and operation order.
10-second proof: one stamp hits top sheet strongly, second as outline, third not at all because of a separator.
Primary failure hypothesis: niche/abstract and visually too flat; collision if it drifts into folding.

### A4 — ROOM TONE (C01)
Hook: sculpt who hears what in a tiny building by placing visible acoustic materials, not by turning sources off.
Core interaction: socket absorbers/resonators/vents into wall positions; resolve transmission categories.
Depth source: overlapping propagation paths, selective frequency/category transmission and conflicting room goals.
10-second proof: one resonator makes the studio hear the boiler rhythm while the bedroom becomes quiet.
Primary failure hypothesis: audio accessibility burden or collision with HEARWALL if geometry becomes hidden.

### A5 — BORROWED FOOTPRINT (C03)
Hook: one character lays down disposable movement affordances that another character can consume.
Core interaction: plan short routes for 2–3 characters; each traversal writes a visible one-use footprint type.
Depth source: sacrificial route writing, consumption order and different character movement vocabularies.
10-second proof: character A takes a pointless detour; B then chain-jumps over the footprints to an otherwise unreachable ledge.
Primary failure hypothesis: route-search dominates the novel affordance layer.

### A6 — IN THE MARGIN (C08)
Hook: solve a central diagram without touching it; every move is an annotation placed around its edge.
Core interaction: place a small set of margin notes whose visible projection regions transform central symbols.
Depth source: overlapping transforms, commutativity/noncommutativity and external composition.
10-second proof: four annotations around a diagram visibly rotate/invert different slices into one solution.
Primary failure hypothesis: excellent puzzle but weak embodied/store fantasy.

### A7 — THE RECEIPT IS THE MAP (C15)
Hook: what you buy and how the receipt is grouped literally authors the map you must traverse next.
Core interaction: choose item order/bundle separators under shopping constraints, then use the printed layout as a navigation topology.
Depth source: dual-purpose choices where accounting structure becomes spatial structure.
10-second proof: cashier prints receipt; dividers unfold into walls/bridges of the next scene.
Primary failure hypothesis: feels like two disconnected minigames or requires expensive bespoke transitions/content.

### A8 — QUIET HOURS (C18)
Hook: run as much of a tiny building as possible without any neighbor hearing an illegal combination of nuisance categories.
Core interaction: choose simultaneous machine groups; each machine has visible sound categories and each resident hears a fixed local subset.
Depth source: overlapping local constraints, mutually compatible bundles and temporary objective priorities.
10-second proof: adding a “quiet” washing machine makes a legal group illegal because one apartment already hears vibration + water.
Primary failure hypothesis: reduces to dry subset/bin-packing with little expressive fantasy.

### A9 — HALF-LIFE OF A SIGN (C12)
Hook: every time a rule is obeyed, that physical sign wears away and reveals the next rule underneath.
Core interaction: route simple actors/objects through a space while choosing which sign layers to consume now versus preserve.
Depth source: consumable rules, reveal order and using a current instruction to engineer the future rule surface.
10-second proof: two carts obey KEEP LEFT; paint flakes off and STOP is revealed exactly for the third.
Primary failure hypothesis: can become a thin sequencing puzzle or resemble program mutation.

### A10 — POSTMARK (C23)
Hook: stamps combine into compound meanings, so the same mark changes function depending on what is already on the envelope.
Core interaction: route envelopes through a tiny set of stamping stations and choose order/adjacency.
Depth source: compositional visual grammar and cross-envelope resource/order constraints.
10-second proof: blue stamp means HOLD alone, but placing it beside red visibly merges them into RELEASE.
Primary failure hypothesis: arbitrary symbol grammar or programming-puzzle niche.

### A11 — AFTER THE BELL (C26)
Hook: every bell instantly advances all staged objects into their next physical role; you arrange the room for what they are about to become.
Core interaction: reposition a few multi-role props between synchronized bell commits.
Depth source: anticipating future affordances, coupled state changes and deliberate temporary “wrong” uses.
10-second proof: bell rings; benches become barriers, a cart becomes a serving station, and the route you prepared suddenly works.
Primary failure hypothesis: role cycles become hard to remember or mechanically resemble cyclic scheduling.

### A12 — WHAT THE WALL KEEPS (C04)
Hook: walls store the last physical condition that touched each side, turning boundaries into reusable one-step memory.
Core interaction: press/move simple sources or objects against authored wall sockets, then exploit retained surface state from the other side.
Depth source: two-sided memory, overwrite order and delayed cross-room causality.
10-second proof: cool one wall face; later condensation appears on the opposite face and wets a paper mechanism.
Primary failure hypothesis: state bookkeeping or weak market legibility; must not become forensic “what happened before” deduction.

## 7. Round-A scoring gates
Use equal destructive criteria rather than enthusiasm:
1. one-sentence hook clarity;
2. 10-second trailer/GIF legibility;
3. minute-to-minute agency;
4. hour-5 depth from the same grammar;
5. demo strength within 20–35 minutes;
6. dominant-strategy / brute-force resistance;
7. rule/tutorial burden;
8. technical risk;
9. bespoke asset/content burden;
10. store-category distinction in 2026;
11. portfolio collision risk;
12. likelihood the implementation team can build from a bounded deterministic spec.

Do not reward “novel in description” if the actual play is generic routing, packing or enumeration.
