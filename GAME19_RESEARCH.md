# GAME #019 — OPPORTUNITY DISCOVERY

Date: 2026-09-07
Status: ACTIVE CANON — Phase 1
Design complete: NO

## 1. Clean-slate boundary
Game #019 is a new design slot. Games #001–#018 are exclusion/portfolio history only. None of their mechanics, fiction, titles, content structures, or product assumptions are inherited here.

Hard collision exclusions from the portfolio: living-cargo cascades; bureaucracy/reality editing; property transfer; audio-hidden geometry; tension redistribution; topology rewiring; remembered transformed shape; destructive key vectors; folding/imposition; moving luggage label permutations; cyclic program deletion; negative-space topology; tamper witnesses; shadow classification; masking/spray histories; irreversible fabrication/byproduct tools; diagnostic queue choice; movable local-time zones.

## 2. September 2026 market read
Fresh research was used because Phase 1 depends on current competition and player expectations.

### Signals
- Puzzle competition is unusually visible in 2026. `Order of the Sinking Star` is a major pure-puzzle entrant with four combining mechanic families and reportedly 1,000+ handcrafted puzzles. Competing on raw handcrafted puzzle count is therefore a bad small-team strategy.
- `Blue Prince` remains a strong recent proof that puzzle players will engage deeply with a systemic mystery structure rather than a conventional linear level list; its room-drafting/mansion identity is nevertheless a collision space to avoid rather than imitate.
- June 2026 Next Fest coverage shows both highly authored thinky games and hybrid/cozy/presentation-led puzzle demos. A legible demo hook still matters because discovery is crowded; one source described roughly 4,300 demos in the event.
- Thinky coverage highlights compact games with unusual rule discovery, including AntiMatcher and Colorbound, and explicitly values demos that teach a small vocabulary then reveal deeper interactions. This supports rule-density over content-volume.
- `What's the Password?` launched May 28, 2026 at $7.99 and is explicitly praised on its Steam page for puzzle density without padding. This is evidence that compact puzzle products can credibly occupy a lower premium tier rather than pretending to be 20-hour games.
- September indie coverage remains crowded with roguelites, co-op, puzzle-platformers and eccentric premise-first games. A generic roguelite deckbuilder, cozy assembly game, Sokoban variant, automation builder, or co-op physics puzzler would enter a noisy lane.
- Current indie success continues to reward immediately explainable objects/situations (e.g. slot machine, keyboard-worm, wooden diorama), suggesting the best #019 candidates should have a screenshot/GIF-readable physical metaphor rather than depend on lore.

### Opportunity thesis
Do not fight 2026 puzzle competition with level count, expensive art, or another generic grid-pusher. Seek a compact premium PC-first game with:
1. one physical or diagrammatic object readable in seconds;
2. a small deterministic verb set;
3. second-order interactions that generate depth;
4. a 20–40 minute demo that proves the full idea rather than merely teasing story;
5. moderate authored-content burden, ideally with validator-assisted construction;
6. no multiplayer/networking dependency;
7. no deckbuilding/roguelite wrapper unless the mechanic absolutely requires it.

## 3. Saturation / scope kills before ideation
Reject by default:
- generic Sokoban + one gimmick;
- Balatro-like score roguelite/deckbuilder;
- cozy object-assembly/diorama puzzle whose differentiation is mostly art;
- first-person mystery mansion / knowledge-metroidvania;
- co-op communication/physics puzzle;
- factory automation or colony sim requiring large systemic/content scope;
- narrative detective game requiring large writing/voice/scene burden;
- precision platformer whose puzzle claim depends on execution skill;
- 3D perspective/shadow puzzle because the portfolio already occupies adjacent visual-spatial proof territory.

## 4. Broad candidate field
These are deliberately independent seeds, not canon mechanics.

### A — WRONG ADDRESS
A compact routing puzzle where every parcel bears several destination descriptors, but the player can physically rotate/cover portions of a shared address stencil so the same printed object is interpreted by different sorting machines.
- Hook: change what machines can read, not the parcel.
- Minute-to-minute: inspect descriptors, position occluders/stencils, run sort, diagnose misroutes.
- Depth source: several readers use different fields/order/visibility rules; one intervention affects many parcels.
- Demo proof: one screen of parcels visibly diverging through readers after a tiny stencil change.
- Burden: low–moderate; mostly 2D data-driven puzzles.
- Failure hypothesis: may feel like spreadsheet constraint solving unless routing is strongly visual.

### B — COUNTERWEIGHT OFFICE
A hanging mobile of trays, documents and counterweights must satisfy several local balance/height clearances at once; moving one object propagates physical equilibrium through the hierarchy.
- Hook: solve bureaucracy by balancing the literal organizational chart.
- Minute-to-minute: move weighted objects among hooks, observe deterministic re-leveling, satisfy target clearances/order.
- Depth source: nested levers couple distant consequences; discrete hook positions make states enumerable.
- Demo proof: a single drag causes the whole branching mobile to settle into a surprising new geometry.
- Burden: moderate; needs polished deterministic pseudo-physics/readability, not continuous simulation.
- Failure hypothesis: equilibrium math may become calculation-heavy rather than intuitive.

### C — PROOFREADER'S CUT
A strip of symbols passes under fixed reading windows. The player may insert a tiny number of spacing blanks; every insertion shifts all downstream alignments, making different windows read valid or invalid tokens.
- Hook: solve many readers by adding spaces to one shared line.
- Minute-to-minute: inspect windows, insert/remove allowed blanks, preview shifted alignment, commit.
- Depth source: global downstream displacement plus heterogeneous window widths/pattern predicates.
- Demo proof: one blank simultaneously fixes one reader and breaks two later readers.
- Burden: low; validator-friendly 2D presentation.
- Failure hypothesis: too abstract/textual and visually weak.

### D — TIDE TABLE
A miniature harbor contains boats tethered to fixed-height docks. Player chooses a short sequence of discrete water levels; boats float vertically, ropes tighten/slacken, bridges become passable, and cargo can transfer only at matching heights.
- Hook: program the tide, not the boats.
- Minute-to-minute: choose next tide height, observe global vertical state, transfer cargo when interfaces align.
- Depth source: shared global control, limited tide changes, boat draft/rope/bridge constraints, order-dependent transfers.
- Demo proof: harbor transforms coherently as one tide slider moves everything.
- Burden: moderate 2.5D/2D; deterministic discrete states.
- Failure hypothesis: can collapse into simple scheduling if cargo interactions lack second-order consequences.

### E — LOST CARBON
A stack of old multipart carbon-copy forms is misaligned. Pressure marks pass through selected layers depending on backing sheets and cutouts; player rearranges the stack so each layer receives exactly the required marks.
- Hook: one typed mark creates different evidence through a stack.
- Minute-to-minute: reorder sheets/backings, align cutouts, apply a small set of strikes, inspect resulting copies.
- Depth source: transmission depth, blockers, reused strike coordinates, layer order.
- Demo proof: exploded stack view showing one keystroke propagating through some layers but not others.
- Burden: low–moderate; crisp 2D animation.
- Failure hypothesis: could resemble prior masking/history concepts if persistence becomes central; must stay about simultaneous transmission, not temporal witness history.

### F — SWITCHBOARD ETIQUETTE
An old telephone switchboard has callers with incompatible conversation rules. A plug connects two endpoints, but plugging/unplugging also changes which shared line lamps and ring circuits are active. Goal is to complete required conversations without forbidden overhearing/ringing states.
- Hook: every connection rewires both communication and signaling.
- Minute-to-minute: place/remove cords, read lamps, trigger calls, complete finite conversation requirements.
- Depth source: shared circuits, limited cords, endpoint etiquette predicates, sequencing.
- Demo proof: satisfying cord-plugging interaction plus a lamp cascade.
- Burden: moderate; strong tactile UI, limited art.
- Failure hypothesis: graph-routing familiarity may make it feel conventional.

### G — DRYING RACK
Printed sheets hang on a rack. Ink is wet for a known number of turns; folding/contact transfers wet marks to touching faces. Player wants exact final markings while using unavoidable contact as a tool.
- Hook: wet ink copies itself when surfaces touch.
- Minute-to-minute: hang/move/fold sheets, advance drying, predict contact transfer.
- Depth source: deterministic drying timers, mirrored transfer, multi-sheet contacts, limited rack slots.
- Demo proof: peel two sheets apart and reveal a useful mirrored transfer.
- Burden: moderate; 2D/2.5D state visualization.
- Failure hypothesis: temporal surface-history may collide too closely with Fresh Coat/Binder's Imposition unless kept materially distinct.

### H — ONE BAD RULER
A workshop's measuring jigs are all referenced to a shared movable zero stop. Parts are cut only at fixed ruler marks; shifting the zero changes every available absolute cut at once. Produce a set of lengths with limited stock and cuts.
- Hook: you cannot move the ruler marks—only where zero begins.
- Minute-to-minute: choose stock, shift shared zero, make allowed cuts, allocate pieces.
- Depth source: global calibration, kerf/waste, shared settings across batches.
- Demo proof: moving one stop changes all highlighted legal cut positions.
- Burden: low.
- Failure hypothesis: arithmetic optimization could overpower puzzle intuition and fabrication theme is too near Game #016.

### I — ELEVATOR INSPECTOR
A building has elevators whose floor labels can be temporarily covered with inspection plates. Elevators obey physical floor order, while passengers request visible labels and transfer based on what they believe the floor is. Re-label strategically to route a finite crowd.
- Hook: the lift knows where it is; passengers only know what the sign says.
- Minute-to-minute: place limited plates, dispatch lifts, watch boarding/transfers, restore labels.
- Depth source: two simultaneous coordinate systems—physical floor vs public label—plus crowd dependencies.
- Demo proof: two elevators open at the same apparent floor label in different physical places.
- Burden: moderate.
- Failure hypothesis: dangerously adjacent to False Map Department's reality/bureaucracy manipulation and Luggage Carousel's public predicates; likely portfolio kill.

### J — PRESS PASS
A mechanical platen press applies one rigid pattern to several objects placed beneath it. Objects may be flipped/rotated and stacked; raised areas on upper objects prevent pressure from reaching lower ones. Goal is exact embossed/debossed marks on every object.
- Hook: one press stroke must solve an entire stack.
- Minute-to-minute: arrange parts, orient, stack, choose plate, press, inspect.
- Depth source: occlusion/contact, orientation, shared irreversible stroke budget.
- Demo proof: dramatic press/reveal animation.
- Burden: moderate.
- Failure hypothesis: masking/stacking adjacency to Games #009/#015 is too high unless tournament proves a clearly different mental model.

### K — NIGHT SHIFT WINDOWS
A building façade has rooms with blinds, internal doors and one moving maintenance light outside. Each tenant has a required sequence of light/dark observations, but the player controls only blinds/doors and the light's route.
- Hook: choreograph what each window sees from one shared moving light.
- Minute-to-minute: set apertures, choose light stops, observe illumination propagation.
- Depth source: shared source, occlusion graph, sequence constraints, limited changes.
- Demo proof: façade lights into a readable pattern as the maintenance lamp moves.
- Burden: moderate.
- Failure hypothesis: shadow/light adjacency to Negative Casting and temporal milestone adjacency to Local Time; portfolio kill likely.

### L — RETURN TO SENDER
A pneumatic-tube network uses capsules whose physical orientation determines which branch sensor reads their notch. Stations may flip, rotate, or pass capsules, but each station has a fixed action; player configures only a few station settings before launching a batch.
- Hook: program a sorting machine by exploiting the orientation history of the objects moving through it.
- Minute-to-minute: configure finite station settings, launch test capsule/batch, inspect routes, revise.
- Depth source: object orientation as compact state carried through a network; same station behaves differently by incoming state.
- Demo proof: transparent tubes + capsule rotations make causality visible in seconds.
- Burden: moderate; deterministic simulation, low bespoke art.
- Failure hypothesis: routing-network surface is familiar and can drift toward Luggage Carousel unless orientation transformation is the true puzzle core.

### M — RECEIPT TEAR
A long receipt contains repeated symbols along both edges. Player may tear it only along a few perforations, then overlap resulting strips; scanners judge only exposed edge sequences. Goal is to make several valid coupons from one receipt without changing printed symbols.
- Hook: solve codes by tearing and overlapping one continuous receipt.
- Minute-to-minute: choose tears, slide/overlap strips, feed exposed edges to scanners.
- Depth source: resource partitioning, occlusion, shared substrings, reversible layout after irreversible partition.
- Demo proof: one tear turns a useless long receipt into two combinable code fragments.
- Burden: low.
- Failure hypothesis: physical masking/occlusion portfolio adjacency; could feel like a paper puzzle rather than a videogame.

### N — RESONANCE CLERK
A rack of tuning forks is struck by a single pulse. Forks with matching harmonic relations trigger neighboring latches after deterministic delays. Player chooses only fork positions and dampers; goal is a target latch firing order.
- Hook: arrange resonances so one strike performs a whole mechanical sequence.
- Minute-to-minute: place forks/dampers, strike once, watch pulse cascade, diagnose order.
- Depth source: harmonic compatibility + delay + adjacency; one-shot causal machine.
- Demo proof: audible/visual cascade from one satisfying strike.
- Burden: moderate, with audio accessibility needing visual parity.
- Failure hypothesis: can become opaque unless every resonance relation is visibly inspectable; audio identity risks HEARWALL adjacency despite different mechanics.

## 7. Round-A priors
No winner is selected in Phase 1. Initial hypotheses for destructive tournament:
- **D TIDE TABLE** currently has the cleanest combination of GIF-readable global transformation, modest scope and a distinct portfolio identity, but must prove it is not merely a scheduling puzzle.
- **B COUNTERWEIGHT OFFICE** has the strongest single-screen physical metaphor, but needs a discrete equilibrium model players can reason about without doing algebra.
- **F SWITCHBOARD ETIQUETTE** has tactile appeal and systemic sequencing potential, but must demonstrate novelty beyond ordinary graph routing.
- **A WRONG ADDRESS** is cheap and systemic, but presentation is the biggest danger.
- **L RETURN TO SENDER** is highly legible in motion, but must survive direct comparison with the factory's earlier routing/sorting game.
- **C/E/M** are useful low-scope challengers; each has a clearer weakness that the tournament should actively try to falsify.

## 8. Tournament protocol for next run
Phase 2 should not brainstorm more concepts unless all eight fail. Run equal destructive tests on A/B/C/D/E/F/L/M:
1. one-sentence stranger comprehension;
2. exact first 10 minutes;
3. distinct decision types available by hour 3 and hour 10;
4. minimum mechanic vocabulary needed for depth;
5. dominant-strategy / brute-force vulnerability;
6. level-generation/validation feasibility;
7. content-count burden and likely repetition;
8. controller/Steam Deck readability;
9. screenshot/GIF/trailer proof;
10. demo stopping point and conversion argument;
11. portfolio collision test;
12. technical/art/audio risk;
13. strongest reason the game gets boring;
14. strongest reason a buyer says "I've played this already."

Score only after writing the destructive case for every candidate. Kill aggressively. Retain 2–4 finalists for a deeper semifinal; do not lock a product thesis until Phase 2 identifies a winner.
