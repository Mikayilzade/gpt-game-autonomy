# 2. FINALIST A — BORROWED COLLISION

## 2.1 Frozen one-sentence Steam pitch

**Steal the force from one crash and spend it somewhere else: harvest collisions as portable impulses, rotate them through physical sockets, and use yesterday's impact to move carts, doors, cargo — or yourself.**

## 2.2 10-second muted trailer beat

0–2 s: crate drops and slams a bumper.
2–4 s: impact freezes into a chunky EAST / MEDIUM arrow-token that pops visibly from the collision.
4–6 s: player carries it across the room and snaps it into a cart.
6–8 s: cart launches through a gate and collides with a second object.
8–10 s: that second collision creates a weaker upward impulse; player grabs it and self-launchs toward the exit.

The full product thesis is visible without text: **a consequence is harvested, transported, and reused**.

## 2.3 Exact first 20 minutes

### Minute 0–3 — consequence becomes object
One authored collision happens in front of the player. The impact emits a visible weak impulse token. The player carries it five steps and stamps it into a stationary cart. Cart moves; switch opens.

No inventory explanation beyond `This is the crash you just captured`.

### Minute 3–6 — direction is physical, not mathematical
A medium SOUTH token cannot solve an EAST receiver directly. Player sees a fixed quarter-turn socket in the room, passes the token through it, and gets EAST medium. No free vector rotation exists.

### Minute 6–9 — stronger is not better
A strong impulse visibly cracks a fragile receiver. Undo/reset is immediate. Medium succeeds. Strength bands become categories, not numbers.

### Minute 9–12 — the player is also a receiver
Player spends a weak impulse on a self-launch socket to cross a gap. This prevents the game from becoming detached arrow bookkeeping: impulse is traversal as well as object logic.

### Minute 12–15 — donor scarcity
Two useful donor collisions exist. Spending the strong one on an ordinary door leaves no safe solution for a heavy mechanism later. Player learns that **where an impulse came from and whether that donor can be recreated matters**.

### Minute 15–20 — first synthesis
One compact room contains:
- 2 donor collisions;
- 1 quarter-turn socket;
- 1 fragile receiver;
- 1 cart;
- 1 self-launch;
- 2 valid solutions.

The player must decide source, direction transform, magnitude suitability, order, and whether to create a secondary collision. Completion should feel like causal reuse rather than vector matching.

## 2.4 First two hours

### 0:00–0:20
Core loop above.

### 0:20–0:45
Introduce:
- two-token capacity;
- reverse/mirror sockets;
- weak/medium/strong receiver windows;
- causal lineage inspection;
- first room where one collision can generate a new useful donor event.

### 0:45–1:15
First multi-room packet:
- carry impulse between spaces;
- source exhaustion;
- one resettable weak donor;
- one exhaustible strong donor;
- environmental consequence after spending a token;
- optional efficiency target based on final causal route, never raw Undo count.

### 1:15–2:00
First genuine mastery layer:
- chained collision creates an impulse that replaces two obvious donors;
- player must choose whether to use impulse on self or cargo;
- one receiver can be damaged by excessive strength;
- 2–3 alternate valid solutions are accepted;
- final room proves the game cannot be solved as a detached token permutation because the donor world-state and receiver consequences alter future options.

By hour 2 the player should understand the whole **primitive vocabulary**, while later depth comes from recombination rather than a stream of new token types.

## 2.5 Minimum shippable scope

Working minimum viable commercial design target:
- PC/Steam first;
- premium single-player;
- 30–36 authored main cases grouped into compact districts/labs/areas;
- 8–12 optional remix/mastery cases only if they genuinely change causal structure;
- 8 directions × 3 magnitude bands, no numeric vector UI;
- 4 direction-transform families maximum: quarter-turn, reverse, mirror, damper;
- 6–8 receiver/donor behavior families maximum;
- player self-launch supported as core grammar;
- 2-token baseline inventory, 3-token absolute late-game ceiling;
- 4–7 hour first-clear minimum target; 5–8 hours preferred after pacing proof;
- no open world, combat campaign, procedural infinite mode, multiplayer, physics sandbox, free-angle aiming, or realistic rigid-body chaos required for 1.0.

The game must still feel complete if shipped near the lower count; it cannot rely on 100 tiny rooms to compensate for shallow transformations.

## 2.6 Content-production method

Content should be authored from a deterministic **body/receiver graph** rather than hand-tuned free physics.

Each case declares:
- stable bodies and authored collision boundaries;
- donor events and regeneration class;
- resulting impulse token band/direction;
- receiver compatibility and safe/unsafe magnitude bands;
- converter topology;
- player traversal graph/self-launch receivers;
- causal objectives/invariants;
- known valid solution paths/final states;
- optional mastery contract;
- transformation tags used for anti-repetition validation.

Content families should rotate dominant reasoning between:
1. source selection;
2. direction conversion;
3. magnitude suitability;
4. donor preservation/exhaustion;
5. causal chain creation;
6. self/cargo allocation;
7. multi-room transport;
8. causal compression/elegance.

## 2.7 Validation-tooling burden

Required early tooling:
- stable-ID/reference validation;
- deterministic collision-table replay;
- token provenance/duplication checks;
- donor regeneration-cycle detection;
- receiver-band legality checks;
- known solution fixture runner;
- bounded state search for accidental max-force dominance/trivial paths where feasible;
- canonical state hash and replay fixtures;
- anti-repetition report over reasoning-transformation tags.

Burden: **High but bounded and directly useful**. It is mostly deterministic state validation rather than continuous-physics debugging.

## 2.8 One-week prototype plan

### Day 1
Build deterministic 2D body state, 8 directions × 3 bands, one authored collision table, one capture source, canonical replay/hash.

### Day 2
Implement token harvest/spend, visible arrow/band display, one cart receiver, reset/Undo.

### Day 3
Add quarter-turn/reverse sockets, fragile receiver, strong/medium distinction, causal lineage.

### Day 4
Add player self-launch and secondary collision generation. Confirm no frame-timing dependence.

### Day 5
Build BC01–BC05 from the existing falsification packet plus one two-token ordering case.

### Day 6
Build BC06–BC08 mature cases; run deterministic replay hundreds of times; try automated maximum-force / donor-factory exploits.

### Day 7
Naive playtest with verbal prediction before actions. Measure misunderstanding, blind permutation rate, strongest-force dominance, and whether players describe tokens as `stored crashes` rather than generic arrows/ammo.

### Binary end-of-week decision
**PASS only if all are true after one tuning pass:**
- repeated identical runs produce identical collision/token results;
- no ordinary solution requires unsnapped aim or reflex timing;
- <=20% ordinary failures are primarily direction/magnitude UI misunderstanding;
- <50% mature solutions use the strongest available impulse at every meaningful choice;
- <35% successful mature actions are blind token permutations;
- majority of testers can explain a secondary collision chain causally without numeric vectors;
- player description centers on reusing collision consequences, not generic physics puzzles.

Otherwise kill or radically reopen before large design investment.

## 2.9 Likely negative Steam sentiment

Most likely review:
> “Amazing idea for an hour, then it turns into carrying colored arrows to matching sockets.”

Second likely review:
> “It pretends to be physics, but everything is quantized and gamey.”

Design response for later phases:
- do not add finer vector resolution to look more physical;
- make source world-state, receiver aftermath, chaining, self-launch and scarcity matter increasingly;
- presentation should intentionally communicate a stylized causal-physics world, not promise a sandbox simulator.

## 2.10 Perceived-value / price hypothesis

If the game proves 5–8 hours of genuinely distinct causal challenges, strong demo conversion, controller/Deck quality and polished visual feedback, working list-price hypothesis: **$14.99–$19.99**, likely center around **$14.99–$17.99** rather than $19.99 unless production polish and replay value land unusually strongly.

The price must be rechecked near release; this is not canon.

## 2.11 Implementation schedule risk

Relative small-team risk:
- core deterministic prototype: Medium;
- UX/cause readability: High;
- content tools: High;
- authored content: Medium–High;
- QA: High;
- bespoke art: Low–Medium;
- animation/feel dependency: Medium;
- catastrophic unknown subsystem: **none beyond whether the central metaphor is intuitive**.

This is a favorable risk profile because the existential question is cheap to test before content production.

**FINALIST A VERDICT: WINNER.**

# 3. FINALIST B — SINGLE-PIXEL KINGDOM

## 3.1 Frozen one-sentence Steam pitch

**You are one pixel carrying one borrowed property at a time: steal SOLID, HOT, HEAVY or ALIVE from the world, physically route it through spaces that the property itself changes, and deposit it somewhere else before your return path disappears.**

## 3.2 10-second muted trailer beat
Pixel touches solid block -> block ghosts out while pixel gains SOLID icon -> pixel crosses transparent bridge -> deposits SOLID -> bridge appears -> donor path behind vanishes -> pixel grabs HOT and detours around coolant.

Strong clip, but it requires the viewer to understand donor absence + carried state, so it is slightly less immediate than Borrowed Collision.

## 3.3 Exact first 20 minutes
0–4: borrow SOLID and deposit it in ghost bridge.
4–7: learn donor loses SOLID and route behind disappears.
7–10: SOLID blocks mesh route, forcing deposit before passage.
10–13: HOT melts one gate but coolant strips HOT.
13–16: HEAVY activates pressure but prevents lift route.
16–20: first two-state relay with one consequential cache receiver and two valid orderings.

## 3.4 First two hours
By hour 2 introduce 4–5 of six total state families, one-slot logistics, receiver-as-cache consequences, donor absence, moving receiver, route restoration, and multi-room relay. Do not introduce a seventh property later simply to create content.

## 3.5 Minimum shippable scope
- 30–40 authored route/state cases;
- exactly six property families maximum;
- 3–5 room mature networks;
- no remote property transfer;
- 4–6 hour first-clear minimum, 5–7 preferred;
- minimalist but deliberate visual identity;
- no combat/meta progression required.

## 3.6 Content / validation burden
Content is tile/node based and technically cheaper than the other finalists. Validation must prove:
- route reachability under each carried state;
- donor-absence effects;
- no universal first state;
- no neutral-cache inventory trivialization;
- at least one known solution;
- route dependence cannot be removed from the property-transfer problem.

Technical burden: lowest finalist. Content/readability burden: high.

## 3.7 One-week prototype
Day 1 movement/state carrier; Day 2 donor absence; Day 3 SOLID/HOT interference; Day 4 HEAVY/CONDUCTIVE; Day 5 SP01–SP05; Day 6 SP06–SP08 + bounded solver probes; Day 7 naive tests and direct comparison prompt against generic property-swapping.

### Binary decision
Pass only if:
- state readability confusion <25% ordinary failures;
- no state starts >50% mature solutions;
- testers consistently reason about the **route while carrying**, not only donor->receiver mapping;
- the minimalist presentation is perceived as intentional rather than unfinished;
- at least two mature cases remain interesting after their property nouns are abstracted to A/B/C.

## 3.8 Likely negative Steam sentiment
> “It’s a clever property-swapping puzzle dressed up as a one-pixel gimmick.”

This is unusually dangerous because `LeiLei` now gives the market a direct property-transfer comparison. The candidate can answer only by making embodied route logistics unmistakably central.

## 3.9 Price/value hypothesis
Likely **$9.99–$14.99** unless content volume/presentation grows substantially. The extreme minimalist aesthetic makes a $19.99 value story harder without either unusually deep content or exceptional audiovisual identity.

## 3.10 Implementation schedule risk
Core code Low–Medium | UI/readability High | content High | QA Medium–High | art Low | platform/input Low.

Excellent production efficiency, but product risk is higher than implementation risk.

**FINALIST B VERDICT: SECOND.** Best scope/identity ratio, but Borrowed Collision owns the stronger market-facing verb and stronger causal spectacle.

# 4. FINALIST C — BUREAU OF LOST WEIGHT

## 4.1 Frozen one-sentence Steam pitch

**Every kilogram has to live somewhere: peel mass certificates off objects and redistribute a conserved total, making one thing light only by making something else dangerously heavy.**

## 4.2 10-second muted trailer beat
Safe loses 100 kg certificate -> floats upward; balloon receives it -> crashes through weak floor -> falling debris lands on pressure plate -> door opens.

Very readable, but generic `make heavy/light` is familiar enough that conservation must be explicitly visible in trailer UI.

## 4.3 Exact first 20 minutes
0–4 transfer mass from crate to pressure plate.
4–7 buoyancy: balloon sinks when receiving mass.
7–10 shelf breaks from overloaded mass sink.
10–14 transport/carry capacity.
14–17 inertia: heavier cart produces useful momentum.
17–20 synthesis where mass must be distributed across 4 objects with at least two valid allocations.

## 4.4 First two hours
Introduce support capacity, buoyancy, inertia, pressure, transport limits, counterweights, temporary transfer station, and return-mass requirement. Avoid free-chaotic physics; all meaningful results use discrete deterministic tables/bands.

## 4.5 Minimum shippable scope
- 28–36 authored physics cases;
- 5–7 discrete mass certificate sizes/bands maximum;
- 6–8 physical response families;
- 5–7 hour target;
- no unrestricted sandbox, free throwing, or timing-sensitive rigid-body puzzles.

## 4.6 Content / validation burden
Highest of the finalists. Every case needs:
- conservation validation;
- support/load graph;
- mass-sink exploit analysis;
- alternate-solution search;
- deterministic response verification;
- no timing-dependent completion;
- known valid distributions.

The alternate-solution space expands quickly because several objects can host each certificate.

## 4.7 One-week prototype
Day 1 discrete mass transfer; Day 2 support/break thresholds; Day 3 buoyancy; Day 4 inertia/counterweight; Day 5 first six cases; Day 6 mature eight-object case + solver search; Day 7 naive tests and mass-sink exploit test.

### Binary decision
Pass only if:
- no single sink strategy solves >40% mature cases;
- simplified deterministic physics is intuitive to >=4/5 testers after tutorial;
- ordinary completion never requires precise collision timing;
- players discuss where mass should live rather than merely which object should be light;
- alternate solutions remain testable without bespoke prohibition rules.

## 4.8 Likely negative Steam sentiment
> “The conservation gimmick sounds deeper than it is; every puzzle is make X lighter and dump the kilos into Y.”

## 4.9 Price/value hypothesis
Potentially **$14.99–$19.99** if presentation is strong and case variety proves real. The physical comedy can support perceived value better than extreme minimalism, but QA/content burden is higher.

## 4.10 Implementation schedule risk
Core state Medium | deterministic physics High | UI Medium | content High | solver/QA Very High | art Medium.

The product is good, but more engineering/QA effort buys less market differentiation than Borrowed Collision.

**FINALIST C VERDICT: THIRD.**

The numeric difference is not the sole reason for selection. The decisive distinction is **risk asymmetry**:

- Borrowed Collision's existential question is `does portable collision consequence feel intuitive rather than like vector tokens?` This is cheaply falsifiable, and if it works, the hook is both novel and visible.
- Single-Pixel Kingdom is easiest to build, but its differentiation is conditional on route logistics successfully overpowering an already occupied generic property-transfer comparison.
- Bureau of Lost Weight has excellent systemic depth but inherits familiar mass-manipulation imagery and the largest alternate-solution/physics-validation burden.

# 6. Final selection

## WINNER — G3C15 BORROWED COLLISION

Selection reasons:
1. **The repeated verb is genuinely unusual:** harvest a completed physical consequence and reuse it elsewhere.
2. **The hook survives silence.** Collision -> token -> transport -> new movement reads instantly in a short clip.
3. **It creates second-order depth without feature sprawl.** Source exhaustion, magnitude suitability, direction topology, secondary collisions, self-launch and receiver aftermath all derive from the same small grammar.
4. **Its closest analogues validate physics/action interest without occupying its core noun+verb.**
5. **The existential risk is cheap to test.** Primitive arrows, blocks and deterministic tables are enough to discover whether the metaphor works.
6. **The implementation risk is high but bounded.** It does not need realistic physics, free-angle aiming, networking, huge art, voice acting or AI.
7. **It has a plausible premium-value story.** A 5–8 hour polished systemic campaign can sit comfortably in the current compact premium puzzle market if content transformation remains strong.
8. **It is portfolio-independent.** It does not reuse Organism Cargo's logistics thesis or False Map Department's representation-authority thesis.

## Rejected finalists preservation

`Single-Pixel Kingdom` and `Bureau of Lost Weight` remain documented as rejected Game #003 finalists. They must not be silently merged into Borrowed Collision. They may be reconsidered only in a future numbered factory cycle with fresh evidence.
