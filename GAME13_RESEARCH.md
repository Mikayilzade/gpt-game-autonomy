## Clean-slate / portfolio exclusion boundary
Games #001–#012 are portfolio history only, not canon. Game #013 must not rebuild: living-cargo cascades; bureaucracy/reality editing; transferable collision/property rules; audio-hidden geometry; conserved network tension; destructive adjacency rewiring; observation/persistent remembered forms; destructive key-vector editing; fold/nest/trim imposition; conveyor label permutation; cyclic-program deletion/phase alignment; or solid placement judged by empty-space topology.

Broader exclusion: avoid merely reskinning those games' signature state transforms even when theme differs.

## Fresh September 2026 market read
Steam evidence sampled on 2026-09-01 shows compact puzzle releases still viable but discoverability is unforgiving. EVERDAWN (Apr 2, $9.99) packages tile placement as relaxing procedural score play and has strong early sentiment; The Grid. (Jun 23, $7.99) sells four minimalist logic/word games but has no visible review traction; Gridlock (Apr 22, F2P) uses a daily shared block puzzle; Fugaz (Jul 7, $5.99) couples a single legible musical interaction with knowledge-gated exploration and a demo; Kubo (Aug 14 listing) uses route placement across a cube; Puzzle ATLAS (Apr 29, $1.99) promises 60 handcrafted puzzles with changing mechanics but has no visible review traction. Steam's current upcoming page is dominated by large genre products, making a compact game dependent on an instantly demonstrable hook rather than generic 'logic puzzle' positioning.

Research conclusions:
- Risky/saturated: generic tile placement, Sokoban-with-a-twist, route/connect-the-nodes, cozy decoration + scoring, daily block puzzles, generic automation/factory, generic card roguelite/deckbuilder, portal/light-shadow spatial puzzlers, 'many unrelated puzzles' collections.
- Opportunity: one unusual physical/logical verb that is visually self-explanatory; deterministic small state spaces; consequences visible in one screenshot/GIF; authored cases from a reusable vocabulary; tactile document/object metaphors without becoming bureaucracy (#002 collision); asymmetric constraints where the player changes interpretation/order/ownership rather than simply moving pieces.
- Demo expectation: expose the true hook immediately and prove escalation with the same vocabulary; a weak tutorial-only demo is unacceptable.
- Scope traps: 3D physics, systemic NPC simulation, large narrative/exploration worlds, networking, procedural content requiring opaque solver quality, huge art sets, text-heavy localization dependence.

## Scoring model
Eight dimensions, 1–5 each (40 max): H hook clarity; D same-vocabulary depth; S scope safety; V visual causal legibility; M market differentiation; T deterministic/testable implementation; C content efficiency; E demo strength. Scores are comparative hypotheses for tournament entry, not proof.

## Retained Phase-2 field (12)

### A — Carbon Copy (37)
Failure risk: transfer rules could become matrix bookkeeping; tournament must prove players can reason spatially from visible layers rather than enumerate combinations.

### B — Margin Call (37)
Failure risk: repeated cropping may collapse to finding four independent thresholds. Must prove edge choices couple globally and do not resemble generic submatrix selection.

### C — Pallet Proof (36)
Failure risk: multi-view silhouette puzzles are familiar. Must prove labels/occlusion create a new reasoning object without requiring expensive 3D content.

### D — Seal Break (37)
Failure risk: could become set-cover in costume. Need destructive evidence states, order dependence and legible causality without arithmetic enumeration.

### E — Overprint (37)
Failure risk: overlap masks can collapse into boolean grid algebra. Must demonstrate human-readable visual deductions and enough families without one-off ink rules.

### F — Shared Fuse (36)
Failure risk: sequence search could dominate. Must keep circuit state visually obvious and distinguish from conventional circuit puzzles and #005 conserved-load identity.

### G — Counterweight Clerk (36)
Failure risk: physics-looking presentation may hide a discrete arithmetic balance problem. Tournament must reject if solving becomes equations rather than spatial causal reasoning.

### H — Wax Witness (37)
Failure risk: occlusion/overlap may be too similar to Overprint mechanically. Must prove deformation/ridge persistence yields a distinct vocabulary or merge/kill it.

### I — Cut Here (39)
Failure risk: strongest hook, but must avoid #009 fold/trim portfolio collision. The essential novelty must be one shared cut transformed through independently oriented flat layers, not book imposition or folding.

### J — Blind Staple (38)
Failure risk: stack permutation factorial growth can encourage brute force. Must find deduction-rich cases and a visual model where penetration/binding is immediately understandable.

### K — Transfer Window (39)
Failure risk: sliding perforated strips can reduce to modular alignment arithmetic. Must prove conservation and repeated transfers create qualitative deductions, not offset enumeration.

### L — Dry Run (37)
Failure risk: cellular propagation could resemble many flood-fill puzzles and, if wet material blocks later flow, can approach rejected Ink Bleed lineage from #012's tournament history. Tournament must enforce independence and kill on conceptual collision.
