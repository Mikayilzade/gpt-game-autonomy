# 2. Field expansion — seeds G5C41–G5C60

The expansion deliberately targets territories underrepresented in Run 1: direct action without combat, social/crowd systems, non-audio sensory mechanics, floor-level micro-management and formats not defined primarily as isolated puzzle rooms.

## G5C41 — Crowd Umbrella
**Verb:** open/close and angle one oversized umbrella while walking through a deterministic pedestrian stream. The umbrella physically widens the player's footprint and splits/merges crowd lanes.

Visible consequence: open it at a bottleneck and a stream bifurcates around you, opening one lane while clogging another.

Depth: pedestrian lane formation + player path + timed service crossings. Risk: crowd simulation/readability and NPC art burden.

## G5C42 — Queue Rope
**Verb:** carry one retractable queue rope and snap it between authored posts; nearby agents immediately choose legal queue lanes according to visible destination icons.

Depth: one divider changes multiple waiting times and physical access routes. Risk: management abstraction and indirectness.

## G5C43 — Foreman Baton
**Verb:** hand one visible baton to a worker; nearby workers copy that worker's current task for exactly one short cycle.

Depth: local imitation + task phase + player movement. Risk: worker-AI explanation and accidental automation/factory identity.

## G5C44 — Witness Spotlight
**Verb:** push/rotate one audience spotlight; certain stage actors/mechanisms perform only while visibly `on stage` in the beam.

Depth: selectively advance one routine while freezing others through performance logic, without a timeline UI. Risk: close family to time gating if not socially legible.

## G5C45 — Thermal Footprint
**Verb:** walk/run to lay a short-lived visible heat trail; heat-sensitive tiles, wax, springs and sensors react only while enough recent trail passes near them.

Visible consequence: loop around a frozen hinge, heating it open while the earlier trail cools behind you.

Depth: route shape + trail lifetime + movement speed + spatial sequence. Risk: may become path-drawing/timing rather than systemic action.

## G5C46 — Frost Relay
**Verb:** press one reusable cold pad onto a marked surface; the newly frozen surface hardens/slows while the previous frozen target begins thawing immediately.

Depth: one-active-state conservation + material response. Risk: too close structurally to Frame Pin.

## G5C47 — Dust Blower
**Verb:** aim one short gust from a handheld blower. A finite visible dust layer moves between authored cells, revealing airflow/lasers and piling into small ramps or occluders.

Depth: conserved dust + visibility + traversal. Risk: particle simulation and unclear state.

## G5C48 — Vibration Peg
**Verb:** hammer one peg in a connected beam structure; a visible vibration pulse propagates through joints and moves/loosens attached objects.

Depth: structural path + resonance state. Risk: collision/impulse ancestry too close to Borrowed Collision unless kept non-physical/discrete.

## G5C49 — Magnet Halo
**Verb:** rotate a marked magnetic arc around the player; only objects inside the arc are attracted/repelled while the player keeps moving.

Depth: body orientation + object state + route. Risk: familiar magnet puzzle and free-physics QA.

## G5C50 — Elastic Hook
**Verb:** attach one elastic strap between two authored hooks; player movement stretches it and deterministically moves linked devices.

Depth: player route doubles as actuator input. Risk: overlaps Tension Budget while adding physics complexity.

## G5C51 — Camera Keeps It Real

Depth: physical framing + concurrent routines + traversal. Risk: meta premise and time-control ancestry.

## G5C53 — Escalator Reversal
**Verb:** flip exactly one reversible escalator/walkway while physically riding a multi-level public space; people and movable objects change route with it.

Depth: vertical crowd flow + one-active reversal + player's own movement. Risk: content may be mainly pathfinding.

## G5C54 — Parade Standard
**Verb:** carry one large directional standard; the nearest marching group aligns with its facing at the next intersection, while farther groups continue existing routes.

Depth: embodied crowd choreography. Risk: routing/logistics identity and many agents.

## G5C55 — Smell Fan

# 3. Ten Run-1 candidates — six mature kernels each

The kernels deliberately use the smallest plausible rule vocabulary. If a candidate needs a new mechanic merely to produce six adult problems, that is treated as evidence against it.

## G5C01 — Frame Pin
Rule core: one moving object/mechanism can be pinned at its exact current phase; pinning another releases the old target.

Kernels:
1. **Crusher Shelf** — pin a crusher halfway down, climb it, then pin a rotor and allow crusher to resume behind you.
2. **Elevator Hand-off** — freeze elevator at a non-floor height to bridge a gap; switching pin later reactivates it and changes the return path.
3. **Sweep Cover** — pin a rotating shield to block a hazard arc, but release it to let a conveyor carry an item through.
4. **Conveyor Catch** — pin one carrier at a precise place so it becomes temporary support; releasing sends cargo into a switch.
5. **Paired Sweepers** — exact phase choice matters because only one of two periodic arms can be frozen at once.
6. **Return Cascade** — the pose used to enter becomes dangerous when the old mechanism resumes after a later pin.

Attack result:
- 10-second clip: **strong**.
- 20-minute demo: **easy**.
- controller: **good** if target selection is snapped.
- one-week graybox: **very cheap**.
- hour-5 risk: repeated `wait for good phase → freeze` can dominate.
- market pressure: high; object time-freeze is already explicit in Chrono/Temporal Objects and many prototypes.

**Verdict: SURVIVES CONDITIONALLY.** Identity must be `one frozen pose becomes live physical infrastructure and every new pin releases the old one`, not generic time manipulation. It needs Run-3 proof that mature play is more than timing the correct phase.

## G5C02 — Tension Budget
Rule core: one movable anchor redistributes a fixed discrete tension budget among connected cables; tension levels visibly change several mechanisms at once.

Kernels:
1. **Lift / Gate Trade** — tighten lift cable enough to rise, simultaneously slackening a gate that drops shut.
2. **Sag Bridge** — move anchor so one bridge sags into reach while a counterweighted obstacle lifts elsewhere.
3. **Three-Load Junction** — one anchor position creates three visible tension bands; correct state is not max/min but a split.
4. **Counterweight Corridor** — traverse while moving anchor through two snap positions so a ceiling weight and floor platform trade height.
5. **Moving Winch** — a deterministic winch changes one cable's base pull; player must re-place anchor after the state change.
6. **Return Inversion** — objective removes one load, so the same anchor position redistributes tension differently on extraction.

Attack result:
- 10-second clip: **strong** if cables animate simultaneously.
- demo: **strong**; teach one cable then multi-load consequence.
- controller: **good** with authored anchor rail/sockets.
- graybox: **cheap** if values are discrete and no rope physics is authoritative.
- hour-5 risk: could become arithmetic/graph reading if cable routes are too abstract.
- market pressure: targeted search found rope/tether traversal products but no clear match for conserved tension allocation as the core puzzle verb.

**Verdict: SURVIVES STRONGLY.** Biggest requirement is a discrete, physically readable model; soft-body cable simulation is explicitly unnecessary and undesirable.

2. moving fan shadow elevator;
3. two silhouettes overlap into one long path;
4. shadow wall blocks a hazard while removing a route;

## G5C09 — Rain Router
Rule core: move/rotate one awning/gutter to redirect one visible stream of rainfall among mutually exclusive destinations.

Kernels:
1. turbine versus plant bridge;
2. water cools one machine while disabling an electrical crossing;
3. fill a counterweight bucket, then reroute before overflow;
4. wash debris from one channel, exposing a different downstream branch;
5. moving cart catches diverted rain and carries water to another node;

Attack result:

## G5C10 — Pressure Line
Rule core: carry one seal/valve between authored pipe junctions. Sealing one branch redistributes discrete pressure to visible pistons/doors/launchers in the physical room.

Kernels:
1. piston stair versus closed door;
2. one seal overpressures a launcher while starving a lift;
3. two parallel branches create a non-obvious alternate path;
4. stored reservoir discharges only after seal relocation;
5. a moving machine changes demand, altering which branch reaches threshold;
6. objective opens a bypass, invalidating the entry configuration.

Attack result:
- clip: medium-strong if the room moves visibly; weak if dominated by pipe lines/gauges.
- demo: viable.
- controller: good with authored junctions.
- graybox: very cheap with integer pressure bands.
- hour-5 depth: credible, but risks becoming `pipe puzzle plus avatar`.
- market pressure: `ComPressure` already owns deep steam-pressure/valve computation; many small pressure/pipe puzzles exist. Distinction must be **portable one-seal live traversal**, not building networks.

**Verdict: SURVIVES CONDITIONALLY / LOWER TIER.** Run 3 should kill it unless embodied movement materially changes the decisions compared with a network editor.

2. teach A to carry an object through B's work zone;
3. two routines exchange an item at a timed point;
4. teach one agent to act as moving cover;
5. overwrite one routine while old agent keeps running until release;
6. final three-agent choreography.

Attack result:
- clip: strong.
- demo: strong.
- controller: good.
- graybox: moderate.
- hour-5 risk: choreography/route memorization.
- **nearest-neighbor pressure: fatal enough.** `The Last Clockwinder` is explicitly about recording embodied short routines that autonomous copies repeat, and current robot-loop/programming products heavily occupy adjacent space.

## G5C17 — Door Memory
Rule core: a special doorway visibly remembers one allowed property of the last eligible object that crossed; the next eligible object crossing inherits that remembered property and updates the memory.

Minimum candidate property vocabulary for testing: **HEAVY/LIGHT** first; a second binary family only if needed later.

Kernels:
1. **Heavy Plate** — send heavy crate through, then lightweight cart inherits HEAVY and can press a plate.
2. **Carry Escape** — next object receiving LIGHT becomes movable but door memory changes, affecting return.
3. **Two-Door Sequence** — two memory doors with different current states require ordering rather than global property swap.
4. **Passenger Problem** — player can cross only after staging which object should receive the next property.
5. **Memory Poison** — solving the first objective leaves a bad remembered state that must be corrected before extraction.
6. **Single Property / Multiple Consequences** — weight changes plate activation, lift load and pushability in one room without adding another property family.

Attack result:
- clip: good if doorway visibly `loads` and next object visibly changes.
- demo: moderate; rule needs one clean tutorial beat.
- controller: excellent.
- graybox: extremely cheap.
- hour-5 risk: property vocabulary inflation and arbitrary magic-door perception.
- neighbor pressure: property transfer/swap exists (`Prop Swap`), but targeted search did not surface this sequential doorway-memory rule.

**Verdict: SURVIVES STRONGLY.** Run 3 must prove that a mostly-one-property campaign can scale; if depth requires many arbitrary properties, kill it.

## G5C21 — Broken Rule Workshop
Rule core: a small machine runs automatically; player can remove/swap exactly one physical rule/connection chip, run the machine immediately, observe causal behavior, then revise.

Kernels:
1. wrong direction chip makes sorter send both items left;
2. swapped order chip makes press fire before clamp closes;
3. one delay chip causes two otherwise-correct subsystems to collide in schedule;
4. one inverse condition makes safety gate open on the wrong sensor state;
5. machine has two visible symptoms but only one upstream faulty rule;
6. final machine can be repaired in two legal ways, one meeting a bonus efficiency target.

Attack result:
- clip: medium-strong if machine motion dominates; weak if chip board dominates screen.
- demo: strong because `change one thing → run → watch result` is naturally iterative.
- controller: good if chips are chunky physical sockets, not wiring UI.
- graybox: cheap.
- hour-5 risk: bespoke machine authoring and editor/homework feeling.
- nearest-neighbor pressure: engineering/automation puzzle games are common, but exact single-rule causal diagnosis remains less crowded than free building/programming.

**Verdict: SURVIVES.** It must remain a **diagnosis game**, not become a circuit constructor. Run 3 should test whether reusable machine primitives can produce enough distinct failures without bespoke scripting.

## G5C25 — Sunpatch Garden
Rule core: move one reflector so a single bright sun patch relocates among nearby ecological consumers; reactions happen in compressed seconds, not minutes.

Kernels:
1. grow/retract one vine bridge by moving sunlight;
2. bloom a flower to redirect pollinators, then shade it to reopen a path;
3. warm a basking creature so it moves off a pressure point while another plant loses light;
4. dry a wet crossing while a shade-loving plant closes elsewhere;
5. one reflector angle illuminates two weak consumers versus one strong consumer;
6. objective changes one canopy, creating a different return-time sun route.

Attack result:
- clip: very strong/cozy.
- controller: excellent on rail/snap reflector.
- demo: strong.
- graybox: cheap if ecology is discrete.
- hour-5 risk: consumer list can become content-heavy and `wait for growth` can poison pacing.
- market pressure: simple `mirror light to flower` is old/familiar; candidate only survives if **one scarce sun patch creates simultaneous competing ecological consequences**.

**Verdict: SURVIVES CONDITIONALLY.** All important reactions must resolve in a few seconds; no gardening timer or farm loop is allowed to rescue depth.

## G5C37 — Zero-G Tool Orbit
Rule core: in compact zero-g rooms, throwing/releasing one tool near an authored anchor places it into one of a few predictable visible orbit tracks until caught/re-thrown. The player continues moving while the tool repeatedly traverses that path.

Kernels:
1. **Orbit Switch** — wrench loops through a switch once per cycle while player crosses a door it holds open briefly.
2. **Alternating Hit** — choose inner/outer orbit so tool hits A but misses B each cycle.
3. **Moving Player Catch** — launch tool, traverse independently, intercept it at another point and re-throw to a second anchor.
4. **Orbit Shield** — tool repeatedly intercepts a periodic hazard on one arc while player uses the opposite side.
5. **Anchor Hand-off** — catch from orbit A and release into orbit B without returning to start.
6. **Return Inversion** — objective changes one obstacle; the formerly-safe orbit now strikes the wrong target, requiring a new track.

Attack result:
- clip: **excellent**; persistent orbit is visually legible.
- demo: strong if orbit entry is snapped/previewed.
- controller: plausible only if launch uses authored tracks, not real orbital aiming.
- graybox: cheap with discrete circular/elliptical rails; expensive if real gravity is used.
- hour-5 risk: could collapse into `throw tool at switch` unless catching/repositioning while moving remains central.
- market pressure: orbital mechanics games exist, including a 2026 action/sim `Orbital Salvager`; those generally focus on spacecraft/planetary flight. The candidate remains distinct only by rejecting realistic orbital simulation and using a persistent hand-tool as a second moving actor inside tiny action-puzzle spaces.

**Verdict: SURVIVES STRONGLY.** Prototype must prove the orbit is predictable enough to feel intentional rather than physics fiddling.

### Hard lessons from the six-kernel test
1. **Small vocabulary matters more than pretty premise.** Rain Router needs too many different water consumers to keep escalating.
2. **Exact precedent kills otherwise excellent clips.** Shadow Scaffold is a clear example.
3. **Embodiedness is not enough by itself.** Routine Possession remains too close to recorded-loop automation even if the player directly possesses the worker.
4. **Discrete physics is a major opportunity.** Tension Budget and Tool Orbit both become far more plausible when presented as authored deterministic state spaces instead of simulation toys.
5. **Sequential state can create depth cheaply.** Door Memory remains interesting with a tiny interaction vocabulary because the next object depends on the previous crossing.
6. **UI/editor drift is still a major threat.** Broken Rule Workshop and Pressure Line only survive if the player spends most time watching/inhabiting a live machine, not drawing graphs.

# 5. New-seed immediate pressure

## G5C41 Crowd Umbrella
A quick market check found crowd simulation/puzzle products (`Crowd Simulator`) but no obvious game centered on physically opening/angling an umbrella to split deterministic pedestrian lanes. Interestingly, real pedestrian-flow research has specifically measured how umbrellas alter lane formation, supporting physical plausibility of the visual effect without requiring realistic simulation.

Why alive:
- instantly readable 10-second clip;
- direct movement remains central;
- social/crowd territory gives portfolio distance;
- could use 20–40 stylized agents with deterministic lane cells rather than free social-force physics.

Primary kill risks:
- crowd behavior may feel noisy/unpredictable;
- art/animation burden much higher than abstract puzzle candidates;
- depth may collapse into `open umbrella at bottleneck`.

**Run-2 status: ADVANCE TO RUN-3 ATTACK, not yet a finalist.**

## G5C45 Thermal Footprint
Targeted search did not surface a clear exact puzzle precedent for movement itself laying a short-lived thermal trail that changes local mechanisms. The premise has a good physical causal loop and nearly zero bespoke UI.

Why alive:
- repeated action is ordinary traversal itself;
- muted clip can show a glowing trail warming mechanisms in sequence;
- cheap grid/region graybox;
- controller-native.

Primary kill risks:
- path drawing can become rote;
- decay timing may make execution rather than reasoning dominate;
- mature depth may require too many arbitrary heat-reactive objects.

**Run-2 status: ADVANCE TO RUN-3 ATTACK, not yet a finalist.**
