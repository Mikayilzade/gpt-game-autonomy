## Round A method
All A1–A12 were attacked under the same 13 dimensions frozen in GAME18_RESEARCH.md: 10-second hook, minute agency, hour-5/10 depth, tutorial burden, deterministic specifiability, asset/content burden, technical risk, demo strength, screenshot/GIF identity, saturation/derivative risk, accessibility/controller viability, bookkeeping/brute-force risk, and portfolio collision.

Scores use 1–5 where 5 is favorable. Total is /65. Score is not an automatic winner: a fatal structural flaw can kill a high scorer.

## A1 — LOCAL TIME — 54/65 — SURVIVES
Scores H5 A4 D5 T3 S4 C4 R4 Demo5 V5 Sat5 Acc4 Book3 Coll5.

Attack: The hook is unusually legible: move time instead of waiting for it. It can produce strong diorama GIFs without combat or huge content. Hour-10 depth plausibly comes from overlapping local-time zones, process durations, transfer moments, limited clock moves and simultaneous schedules. The danger is semantic paradox: what happens when a process enters/leaves a zone, whether elapsed progress rewinds, and whether moving a clock can undo completed events. If those are not reduced to discrete public state transitions, every puzzle becomes rules-lawyering.

Survival condition: no retrocausality, no continuous clocks, and no object ever loses already-earned progress merely because its local displayed time changes. Round B must prove a tiny rule grammar can support late mastery without adding bespoke schedule exceptions.

## A2 — UNFINISHED SENTENCES — 51/65 — SURVIVES
Scores H5 A4 D5 T2 S5 C5 R4 Demo5 V4 Sat3 Acc4 Book2 Coll5.

Attack: The blank-noun command is a strong visual programming hook and cheap to produce. Depth from dataflow and aliasing is real. The severe risk is audience narrowing: if the optimal mental model becomes variable binding, pointers and execution traces, the product becomes programmer homework. Brute-force routing can also replace understanding if the state space is small enough to trial-and-error.

Survival condition: commands must read as physical sentences, not code syntax; the first 15 minutes must work without programming vocabulary; late puzzles need visible causal grouping rather than long execution traces.

## A3 — THE ROOM AFTER YOU — 50/65 — SURVIVES
Scores H5 A4 D4 T3 S3 C3 R3 Demo5 V5 Sat5 Acc5 Book3 Coll4.

Attack: Excellent human-readable fantasy and trailer moment. The room visibly becoming a message/tool for the next visitor is emotionally stronger than abstract logic. But every new visitor priority or object affordance risks becoming bespoke authored AI. If the player must memorize that “Mara moves lamps but Jo opens drawers,” content cost and opacity explode. Hour-10 depth is uncertain unless a very small public behavior grammar recombines strongly.

Survival condition: Round B must demonstrate a reusable visitor grammar with at most a handful of verbs/priorities and a representative midgame puzzle that is understandable from visible affordances rather than character-specific trivia.

## A4 — KEEP THE CHANGE — 45/65 — KILL
Scores H4 A4 D4 T4 S5 C5 R5 Demo4 V3 Sat4 Acc5 Book1 Coll5.

Destructive result: Mechanically clean, cheap and deterministic, but its strongest states are still denomination search. Transforming coins adds state but does not reliably change the player's felt activity from arithmetic planning. A trailer can explain the mutation, yet screenshots are weak and hour-5 mastery risks becoming wallet-state bookkeeping. Making transformations more theatrical would add rules rather than solve the core perception.

KILL REASON: high probability of feeling like arithmetic homework/bookkeeping despite good implementation economics.

## A5 — BORROWED GRAVITY — 48/65 — SURVIVES WITH TECHNICAL RED FLAG
Scores H5 A4 D5 T3 S3 C4 R1 Demo5 V5 Sat5 Acc4 Book4 Coll5.

Attack: One of the field's best GIF hooks and strong second-order depth: relational gravity can create chains where reference objects themselves move. But continuous physics would be a production trap and destroy deterministic explainability. Even discrete movement has hard questions around cycles (A down=B, B down=A), simultaneous support changes, rotations, collision order and camera readability.

Survival condition: Round B must specify a discrete socket/pose model and total resolution order. If the concept requires general rigid-body physics to feel correct, kill it immediately.

## A6 — TIDE TABLE FOR TWO — 50/65 — SURVIVES
Scores H5 A4 D4 T4 S4 C4 R3 Demo5 V5 Sat4 Acc5 Book3 Coll4.

Attack: Phase-changing affordance is visually strong and intuitive. Shared tide across two terrain profiles creates useful coupling. Risk: “raft becomes bridge/dam/weight” can turn into a list of handcrafted exceptions, and water puzzles are common enough that generic rising-water gating is not a hook. Depth must arise from a small state table and cross-shore timing, not bespoke object powers.

Survival condition: define a tiny universal affordance grammar based on water level/contact/support; prove midgame cross-shore dependency without adding new object classes every chapter.

## A7 — SECOND-HAND WEATHER — 48/65 — KILL
Scores H5 A4 D4 T3 S3 C3 R2 Demo5 V5 Sat4 Acc4 Book2 Coll4.

Destructive result: Capturing and moving weather is a good fantasy, but the proposed depth source combines conserved parcels, pressure side effects, ecology, timing and neighboring relationships. Either those systems are simplified until play becomes resource shuffling, or retained until prediction/UI burden resembles a small simulation game. It also risks drifting toward #001-style ecological cascades and #005-style conservation interactions even if the fiction differs.

KILL REASON: depth requires too much simulation/state explanation, while the simplified version collapses into resource logistics.

## A8 — ONE MORE CHAIR — 52/65 — SURVIVES
Scores H5 A5 D4 T4 S5 C4 R5 Demo5 V5 Sat5 Acc5 Book3 Coll4.

Attack: Strong embodied comedy and extremely cheap core production. Empty seats as the player's main tool is memorable. Local etiquette rules can create chain reactions. The danger is that after the joke lands it becomes a circular adjacency puzzle with dish tokens, and late difficulty may simply add guests/courses. It needs persistent consequences between courses and multiple local rule interactions that remain visually readable.

Survival condition: Round B must prove a midgame state whose interesting decision is not reducible to “insert chair at the only broken adjacency,” plus late mastery that does not rely on table size inflation.

## A9 — AFTERIMAGE DELIVERY — 53/65 — SURVIVES
Scores H5 A5 D5 T4 S5 C4 R4 Demo5 V5 Sat4 Acc5 Book3 Coll3.

Attack: “First route writes infrastructure for the next” is an excellent action-to-state loop and supports deliberate inefficiency. Deterministic route traces are easy to explain and demo. Collision risk exists with route/grid puzzle conventions and, abstractly, persistent state-writing from prior games, but the identity is movement history as temporary street infrastructure rather than topology editing or remembered object form. Main depth risk is that arrows become simple one-way tiles and later puzzles become pathfinding brute force.

Survival condition: Round B must demonstrate interactions beyond mere gate-open arrows—decay timing, conflicting traces, multiple vehicle rules—while keeping a small grammar and avoiding grid-search bookkeeping.

## A10 — HOUSE OF DRAFTS — 46/65 — KILL
Scores H4 A4 D4 T3 S4 C4 R4 Demo5 V5 Sat3 Acc4 Book2 Coll4.

Destructive result: Cutaway airflow is visually appealing, but the underlying interaction is still network-flow routing unless pressure rules are made spatially richer. Adding smoke/seeds/sound token types increases content vocabulary rather than changing the player's central reasoning. Long-term challenge likely becomes tracing arrows through a graph, with UI doing substantial calculation for the player.

KILL REASON: attractive skin over a familiar network-flow grammar; insufficiently distinct hour-10 activity.

## A11 — FALSE BOTTOM — 47/65 — KILL
Scores H5 A4 D4 T2 S3 C2 R2 Demo5 V5 Sat5 Acc4 Book3 Coll5.

Destructive result: Closing containers changing internal depth is novel, but the player needs to understand hidden layers, nesting, pressure transfer and retrieval order simultaneously. Making this readable likely requires bespoke cutaways/animations per container form. Keeping containers standardized improves clarity but erodes the fantasy and leaves a state-stack puzzle. Content production is therefore inverted: visual clarity costs scale with puzzle variety.

KILL REASON: high bespoke spatial-presentation burden and tutorial cost for depth that can collapse into stack-state bookkeeping.

## A12 — COMMON GROUND — 46/65 — KILL
Scores H4 A4 D5 T3 S5 C5 R5 Demo3 V2 Sat5 Acc5 Book2 Coll5.

Destructive result: Hypergraph construction is genuinely deep and cheap, but the embodied fiction does not hide the abstraction. A screenshot of neighborhoods and amenities is unlikely to communicate the surprising rule; a GIF mostly shows relationship overlays changing. To make it marketable would require characters/social simulation feedback that risks falsely promising a city/social sim and raises asset/content burden.

KILL REASON: strong abstract system but weak visual/demo identity relative to discovery requirements.

The factory prefers candidates whose central interaction is already specifiable at design scale.
