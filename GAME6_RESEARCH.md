# 1. Market frame checked 2026-08-29

The purpose of market research here is not to chase a genre mechanically. It is to understand what a small-team game must beat to earn attention.

## 1.1 Steam attention is brutally congested
A July/August 2026 analysis reported that Steam release growth is increasingly dominated by AI-flagged titles and that the market remains extremely hit-driven. Whatever the exact methodology, the design implication is robust: Game #006 cannot rely on “competent indie game” as its pitch. It needs an immediately legible repeated action and visible consequence.

Reference:
- https://www.gamesradar.com/games/steam-study-of-over-53-000-games-finds-60-90-percent-of-the-growth-in-monthly-releases-on-valves-store-is-from-games-using-ai-and-almost-none-of-them-make-money/

## 1.2 Automation/management demand is attractive but full-scale factory scope is dangerous
Genre-sales estimates continue to place factory/automation unusually high relative to puzzle, but contemporary Steam already has many 2026 automation products competing on belts, factories, logistics, combat, open worlds and 3D spectacle. `Pistonica`, `Warfactory`, `OrderlyWorld`, `Belts of Iron`, `ASEMA`, `Genterra`, `Botlings` and others reinforce that a conventional factory game would create an unnecessary production and differentiation burden.

Implication: if Game #006 uses automation at all, prefer **one unusual local transformation that naturally composes into a small machine**, not an open-ended factory economy.

## 1.3 Minimal conveyor/logic puzzle space is also occupied
`ZAVOD: Conveyor Logic` released 2026-07-24 and `ProBelt` released 2026-04-26. Classic signal/laser/logic routing also has substantial precedent (`Sokosignal`, `Prelogate`, `Reroute`). A Game #006 idea whose main screenshot is simply belts, gates, lasers or arrows needs a much stronger noun+verb distinction.

## 1.4 Time clones / repeated-path ghosts are crowded
`echo`, `Repeat After Me` and the 2026 `Echo Loop` all use variants of copies replaying player behavior. Game #006 should not spend its novelty budget on “your past self repeats what you did” unless the repeated action is transformed so strongly that the clone is no longer the core hook.

## 1.5 Folding/topology is attractive but increasingly visible
`Folding Dungeon` (2026) directly folds/connects map space, `Compress(space)` already sells space folding, and `Crease` uses literal paper folding. Plain “fold the level” is therefore not whitespace.

## 1.6 Hands-on repair/cozy maintenance has demand, but generic repair shop is occupied
`Piece by Piece` launched 2026-03-11 with a Very Positive reception at the time checked, while `ElectroFix Simulator` shows that a broad repair/store simulator can also become undifferentiated and content-heavy. A repair-adjacent candidate should focus on one strange systemic diagnosis/maintenance rule, not a catalogue of minigames or an economic shop sim.

## 1.7 Short physical-system spectacle remains commercially legible
Recent viral/co-op results are not directly reproducible, but they reinforce that an absurd, visible 5–15 second event can carry discovery better than an abstract design thesis. Game #006 should score trailer/GIF readability explicitly even if it remains single-player.

# 2. Game #006 portfolio exclusions

To preserve portfolio diversity, Phase 1 rejects concepts whose center is too close to earlier games:

- no organism/cargo logistics identity;
- no map/representation-authority bureaucracy identity;
- no portable collision consequence / impulse harvesting;
- no hearing/listening-as-primary interface identity;
- no conserved rope/tension distribution identity;
- no generic property stealing/transfer, which was already heavily examined during prior factory cycles;
- no conventional Sokoban as the primary value proposition;
- no full factory/colony/survival scope merely because those genres have stronger median economics.

---

# 3. Scoring frame for discovery

Every candidate is screened on 1–5 for:

1. **5–15 second repeated action** — can the player do the signature thing constantly rather than once per level?
2. **Visible consequence** — can a mute clip communicate cause -> effect?
3. **Second-order depth** — can the same rule create interactions rather than requiring feature accumulation?
4. **Controller viability** — no precision mouse-only authoring as the baseline.
5. **Graybox falsifiability** — can the existential question be tested with primitives in days, not months?
6. **Small-team scope** — low bespoke asset/dialogue/networking burden.
7. **Market distinction** — the core noun+verb is not already obviously occupied.
8. **Hour-5/10 transformation** — mature play changes reasoning, not merely adds bigger layouts.
9. **Demo strength** — 15–25 minutes can show the real hook rather than only tutorial setup.
10. **Portfolio independence** — meaningfully different from Games #001–#005.

A Phase-1 survivor needs no fatal weakness and usually >=40/50 provisional score. Scores are hypotheses to attack in Phase 2, not evidence of fun.

# 4. Broad candidate field — 36 concepts

## G6C01 — STITCHSPACE
**Repeated action:** grab one exposed room edge and stitch it directly to another edge; objects/people can then cross the new seam.

The important distinction from generic folding is that geometry does not fold or overlap. The player rewires **topological adjacency**: north wall of Room A can become the doorway into east wall of Room C. Existing objects preserve orientation rules, and seams are scarce/physical.

Depth vectors: one-way seams, orientation, objects crossing while seam is moved, loops, sealed regions, multi-room causal effects.

Primary risk: looks like another space-folding puzzle unless seams behave visibly unlike folds/portals.

**Provisional: 46/50 — SURVIVES.**

## G6C02 — LAST CONTACT
**Repeated action:** touch two mechanisms together; the receiving mechanism adopts a small deterministic behavior based on **what physically touched it last**, then that contact state alters its next operation.

Example: a piston last contacted by rubber becomes a soft stop; last contacted by metal becomes a hard striker; last contacted by ice becomes a sliding transfer. The state is not remotely stolen; it is a short physical “contact memory.”

Depth comes from sequencing physical contacts through one machine rather than carrying inventory.

Risk: may collapse into generic property transfer if donor objects become mere labels.

**41/50 — SURVIVES WITH STRICT contact-memory differentiation.**

## G6C03 — SIGNAL IN FLIGHT
**Repeated action:** release a visible command pulse into a tiny machine, then physically reroute/intercept the pulse **while it is travelling** before it reaches actuators.

Unlike static logic-gate puzzles, commands are objects with travel time and order. Two pulses can cross, queue, overtake only through authored devices, or intentionally arrive together/apart. Pause/Step can keep it reasoning-first.

Risk: signal-routing market is crowded; must prove “editing commands in flight” changes play materially.

**44/50 — SURVIVES.**

## G6C04 — HEATPRINT
**Repeated action:** move/press a hot tool or body against surfaces, leaving a temporary thermal footprint that changes how later mechanisms interact with that exact region.

Not “carry HOT property.” Heat diffuses in a tiny deterministic cellular field: warm zones soften wax, bend bimetal strips, change traction, reveal paths, and can merge/cancel with coolant prints.

Risk: simulation/readability may become field-management dashboard; diffusion must stay discrete and visual.

**43/50 — SURVIVES.**

## G6C05 — THE SHORT CIRCUIT
**Repeated action:** deliberately create a temporary electrical short between two exposed contacts; the overload knocks specific devices into fallback states before a fuse isolates the fault.

The puzzle is not connecting a correct circuit but weaponizing **incorrect electrical topology** for bounded side effects: drop a latch, reset a motor, heat a strip, force a relay state.

Risk: if represented as ordinary nodes/wires, it is yet another circuit puzzle.

**40/50 — SURVIVES narrowly.**

## G6C06 — FLOOR PLAN SURGERY
**Repeated action:** slide an entire wall segment along a fixed track while occupants/machines remain active, dynamically reallocating the same physical floor area between rooms.

A moved wall changes containment, routes, pressure zones, ownership/permissions and machine reach. No free room building; only a few wall carriages.

Risk: pressure/containment could drift toward Tension Budget-like engineering; art/camera for moving rooms needs care.

**45/50 — SURVIVES.**

## G6C07 — CHECKPOINT MATERIAL
**Repeated action:** freeze the current state of one small mechanism as its **reference state**; later the mechanism measures only deviation from that captured reference and reacts to the difference.

Examples: a scale considers current load relative to captured baseline; a door reacts to “two objects more than checkpoint”; a slider treats checkpoint position as zero. Player moves the zero point, not numeric values.

Risk: could become abstract calibration homework. Visual physical reference markers must make it tactile.

**42/50 — SURVIVES.**

## G6C08 — COMMON SHAFT
**Repeated action:** clutch/unclutch machines onto one shared rotating shaft. Anything attached must share one angular speed/direction, so adding one useful machine physically changes every other attached machine.

No belts or factory expansion; one compact machine room and a handful of devices. The player constantly couples/decouples loads to use collateral effects.

Risk: adjacent to conserved-resource engineering and may visually resemble factory belts/gears.

**47/50 — SURVIVES / current leader.**

## G6C09 — VACUUM SHAPE
**Repeated action:** open/close local vents so flexible objects collapse or inflate into discrete stable shapes; their geometry becomes platforms, seals, pushers and containers.

Risk: pressure-system identity could create expensive soft-body expectation; must use authored discrete shape states, not simulation.

**41/50 — SURVIVES narrowly.**

## G6C10 — WRONG-SIZED WORLD
**Repeated action:** place one physical calibration object into a machine that then treats that object's size as the local unit; nearby mechanisms snap to multiples/fractions of that reference.

This changes spacing/fit without scaling the whole world. A “unit” is embodied by a chosen object.

Risk: likely hard to teach and may read as arbitrary math puzzle.

**38/50 — CUT.**

## G6C11 — MISALIGNED ASSEMBLY
Repeatedly insert parts intentionally one notch “wrong”; misalignment causes useful second-order motion/clearance elsewhere. Interesting but risks bespoke machine content and 3D fiddliness.

**36/50 — CUT.**

## G6C12 — DOOR GRAMMAR
Build a physical condition sentence from a small set of plates. Too close to symbolic logic/Baba-like rule authoring and can become detached UI.

**35/50 — CUT.**

## G6C13 — ECHO DEBT
Every action repeats N turns later as a world event. Time/replay precedent is crowded; likely becomes scheduling spreadsheet.

**34/50 — CUT.**

## G6C14 — SHADOW BRIDGE
Move lights to turn shadows into collision geometry. Visually excellent, but shadow-as-platform/geometry is established enough that deeper distinction would require feature invention.

**37/50 — CUT / reserve inspiration only.**

## G6C15 — FRICTION PAINTER
Paint low/high friction regions. Directly readable but mouse/touch authoring bias, continuous-physics QA, and close relation to draw-physics products make it weak for this factory.

**32/50 — CUT.**

## G6C16 — ONE-WAY BLUEPRINT
A machine executes a short visible command strip and the player may replace only the last executed command. Clever but strongly programming-puzzle coded and low trailer spectacle.

**36/50 — CUT.**

## G6C17 — ROOM THAT REMEMBERS
Room replays the player's last route as physical ghost motion. Time-clone market collision triggers immediate cut.

**29/50 — CUT.**

## G6C18 — MAGNETIC RECEIPT
Every magnet interaction leaves polarity “receipt” usable later. This quickly becomes Borrowed Collision/property-history adjacent.

**33/50 — CUT.**

## G6C19 — ONE BROKEN GEAR
Repair one machine by deliberately moving a single missing tooth around the gear train. Visually cute, but likely one-dimensional after several puzzles.

**35/50 — CUT.**

## G6C20 — SIDE EFFECT LAB
Each machine action has one desired output and one deterministic waste/side effect that must be reused. Too generic as a thesis and could become conventional automation.

**34/50 — CUT.**

## G6C21 — ROLLING ORIGIN
Move the coordinate origin of a grid so directional rules reinterpret positions. Abstract, weak physical fantasy.

**30/50 — CUT.**

## G6C22 — TEMPORARY HINGE
Attach a hinge between any two compatible rigid pieces, use resulting motion, then recover it. Strong toy feel but freeform rigid-body/contact QA and construction UI are expensive.

**37/50 — CUT / possible future mutation.**

## G6C23 — ONE-WAY AIRLOCK
Change which side of a room counts as inside/outside. Representation-heavy and risks overlap with False Map Department's authority/representation territory.

**31/50 — CUT.**

## G6C24 — CONTACT QUEUE
Objects line up and transmit a command only when physically touching; rearrange moving queues. Too close to signal routing and conventional conveyor logic without stronger noun.

**34/50 — CUT.**

## G6C25 — PHASED FLOOR
Every tile alternates between two physical states when crossed. Familiar toggle-puzzle structure; low market distinction.

**31/50 — CUT.**

## G6C26 — MACHINE SCAR
Break/overstress a mechanism and the resulting visible deformation becomes a permanent new constraint/tool for the rest of the case. Strong consequence but hard to produce systematic depth without bespoke break states.

## G6C27 — COUNTERWEIGHT LANGUAGE
Use weights to encode conditions. Too near prior tension/weight concept space.

**25/50 — CUT.**

## G6C28 — SLIDING OWNERSHIP

**24/50 — CUT.**

## G6C29 — COLD WELD
Touch surfaces while cold to temporarily fuse them into one rigid body; warm them to separate. Good clip, but it is essentially attach/detach construction plus temperature and would invite free-physics complexity.

**38/50 — CUT TO RESERVE.**

## G6C30 — SHARED BRAKE
Several moving devices share one brake actuator; engaging it freezes all currently coupled motion, and the player changes coupling topology to exploit simultaneous stops.

Compact and legible, but as a full game it currently looks like a narrower version of Common Shaft.

**39/50 — MERGED AS ATTACK CASE for G6C08, not separate survivor.**

## G6C31 — RESIDUAL CHARGE
Machines keep the difference between requested and actually delivered energy as a visible residual charge that affects the next activation. Risks numerical bookkeeping and ordinary energy-routing tropes.

## G6C33 — NEGATIVE SPACE PRESS
A press removes a shape from one soft material; only the remaining negative space is usable as tool/route. Strong visual but likely bespoke shape-content heavy and weak controller handling.

**37/50 — CUT.**

## G6C34 — REFERENCE OBJECT
Calibrate machines by physically showing them an example object; they thereafter react to relative comparison (larger/smaller/heavier/faster than reference). Broader but less elegant version of Checkpoint Material.

## G6C36 — SERVICE WINDOW
A machine can be opened only during its normal operating cycle, exposing moving internal components that can be repositioned while the outer machine keeps running in deterministic steps. Great maintenance fantasy, but existential UI/readability burden is high.

# 6. Required destructive tests for Phase 2

Every survivor must face the same tests rather than receiving bespoke excuses.

## A. Exact 15-minute demo
Write minute-by-minute onboarding through first synthesis. If the actual hook does not appear in the first 2–3 minutes, penalize heavily.

## B. 10-second mute test
Specify one clip with no explanatory text. If a viewer cannot roughly infer what the player caused, the concept's marketing cost rises.

## C. 24-situation / transformation test
Produce at least 24 situations but group them into **genuine reasoning transformations**. Twenty-four skins of the same operation do not count.

## D. Dominant-strategy attack
Identify the most likely boring strategy and write the smallest global counter-rule. If the repair requires many level-specific exceptions, cut.

## E. Controller grammar
Specify all ordinary repeated interactions without precision pointer dependency or virtual cursor as a requirement.

## F. Production burden
Score art, animation, UI, simulation, content authoring, validation, alternate-solution QA and presentation/feel independently.

## G. Hour-3 / hour-10 mutation
Explain how mature play changes without adding a large new vocabulary. If only board size/number of devices grows, cut.

## H. Exact market analogue check
Search the refined noun+verb, not only the genre. A direct occupied analogue can kill or force mutation.

## I. Negative-review test
Write the most credible negative Steam review after 2–5 hours and the smallest repair. If the repair changes the product identity, cut.

## J. One-week graybox binary gate
Define what can be prototyped in one week and a PASS/KILL threshold measurable without production art.

# 7. Initial Phase-2 hypotheses

These are **not locks**; they exist so the next run attacks concrete assumptions.

## Common Shaft
Potential strongest product because a single shared physical variable creates collateral effects without a detached resource counter. A 10-second clip can show clutch A -> everything slows/reverses/stalls -> clutch B out -> machine wakes. The danger is that it is merely Tension Budget wearing gears or a tiny factory power network. Phase 2 must prove its reasoning is about **mechanical synchronization/coupling**, not budget allocation.

## Stitchspace
Potential strongest scope/visual identity. A seam can transform navigation, orientation and causal adjacency with tiny assets. The danger is current space-folding visibility. Phase 2 must prove that continuously rewiring adjacency while world processes persist is not just portals/folds under another verb.

## Floor Plan Surgery
Promising physical management fantasy: move one wall and several systems change because the room itself changed. The danger is custom room semantics. It needs a tiny universal vocabulary such as containment, reach and route—not dozens of room-specific rules.

## Signal in Flight
A good bridge between puzzle and automation: machine commands are temporal moving objects. The danger is looking exactly like another laser/signal router. It needs order/interception/collision between pulses to be the actual game rather than presentation.

## Heatprint
Potentially tactile and beautiful. It survives only if thermal state can be read without numbers and diffusion is a deterministic small grammar rather than continuous simulation.

## Checkpoint Material
Potentially very novel: player moves the **reference point** rather than the measured value. It needs a physical fantasy stronger than “calibration puzzle” and must avoid numeric interfaces.

## Service Window
Best repair/maintenance fantasy of the field. It survives only if a small reusable machine grammar exists; unique minigame-per-machine production is a kill condition.

## Last Contact
Can create causal sequencing with almost no UI, but generic property transfer is an explicit kill threat. Contact memory must be short, physical, and about the receiver's next operation rather than detachable traits.

## Vacuum Shape
Strong visible transformations and controller-friendly vents. It must use discrete authored shape states so players do not expect soft-body physics.

## The Short Circuit
Deliberately causing faults is a good inversion. It needs visible electrical consequences and failure topology rather than logic-gate wiring. If the core screenshot is wires/nodes with active/inactive colors, cut.
