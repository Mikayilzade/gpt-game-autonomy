# 1. Run-2 research purpose

Run 1 found a useful opportunity cluster around transferable/conserved properties, semantic manipulation, one-object worlds, and action performed inside system editing rather than after it. Run 2 attacks those ideas against actual current/recent Steam products rather than rewarding novelty on paper.

The core question is not whether a candidate has *any* precedent. Almost every useful mechanic does. The question is whether a Steam shopper can summarize the candidate as the same noun + same verb + same fantasy as an existing product.

A candidate is downgraded when:
- its distinctive language disappears once compared to current competitors;
- the apparent novelty is merely a theme wrapped around a known puzzle grammar;
- preserving distinctiveness would force a much larger physics/content/UX burden than the factory target;
- its repeated 5–15 second verb is weaker than its pitch sentence.

# 2. Direct analogue checks — original 12 Tier-A candidates

## A1 — G3C06 Museum of Moving Labels

### Current analogue pressure
`Baba Is You` remains the unavoidable comparison: it exposes game rules as manipulable text blocks and lets players assign functions/properties to objects. As of August 2026 it remains extremely well-reviewed and culturally defining for rule-manipulation puzzles.

Source checked 2026-08-19:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/736260/Baba_Is_You/

### Distinction test
Museum of Moving Labels survives only if labels are **physical property certificates attached one-at-a-time to concrete objects**, not sentence grammar.

Good example:
- remove `FRAGILE` plaque from a glass vase;
- snap it onto a steel door;
- vase can now be dropped while the door shatters under impact.

Bad evolution:
- combine `DOOR IS FRAGILE`, `VASE IS OPEN`, conditionals, conjunctions, negation and nested grammar.

That bad evolution simply becomes a less elegant Baba-like language system.

### Decision
**SURVIVES / HIGH-RISK FINALIST CANDIDATE.**

The physical museum fantasy, one-label transfer verb and immediate material consequence are meaningfully different enough to test. Hard scope law for any later tournament: no sentence construction and no more than a compact property registry.

## A2 — G3C12 Bureau of Lost Weight

### Current analogue pressure
Several successful/current first-person puzzlers already manipulate physical scale, mass or physics state:
- `Sizeable` — shrink/grow objects and environmental elements;
- `Superliminal` — forced-perspective resizing;
- `LIGHTPUNK: Law of Inertia` — current/upcoming physics puzzle manipulation with explicit inertia framing and size/weight-changing devices;
- `THANKS, LIGHT.` — current/upcoming first-person puzzle design where light can grant mass/collision/gravity or remove them.

Sources checked 2026-08-19:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/1333910/Sizeable/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/1049410/Superliminal/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/3692740/LIGHTPUNK_Law_of_Inertia/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/2544740/THANKS_LIGHT/

### Distinction test
The candidate is **not** defensible as `change weight to solve physics puzzles`.

It is defensible only through the conservation transaction:
> Every kilogram must live somewhere. You can make the balloon heavy only by taking mass from another certified object, and the world immediately exposes the collateral consequence.

That creates a resource topology absent from ordinary heavy/light toggles: every helpful change necessarily creates another altered object.

### Dominant exploit attack
`Dump all unwanted mass into one anchored junk object` would trivialize the conservation law.

A future design would need objects whose load-bearing limits, inertia, buoyancy, pressure, floor support or transport role make mass sinks strategically meaningful. If the only fix is arbitrary per-level prohibition, kill the concept.

### Decision
**SURVIVES STRONGLY.**

Mass manipulation is crowded; **conserved transferable mass as the entire economy** still appears differentiated enough to justify tournament testing.

## A3 — G3C15 Borrowed Collision

### Current analogue pressure
Current physics-puzzle space contains trajectory, inertia and motion manipulation:
- `LIGHTPUNK: Law of Inertia` explicitly foregrounds inertia;
- `Path-Reverse` reverses object trajectories;
- `Orbital Trajectory` is a deterministic motion/trajectory planning puzzler.

Sources:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/3692740/LIGHTPUNK_Law_of_Inertia/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/2316070/PathReverse/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4391210/Orbital_Trajectory/

### Distinction test
The searched products manipulate how motion happens. Borrowed Collision proposes treating one **resolved impact impulse as a portable object/resource**:
- crash a falling crate into a bumper;
- capture that impact as one arrow/token;
- later paste the stored impulse onto a stationary cart, door, enemy or yourself;
- the original donor collision has already happened and cannot be reused unless recreated.

The hook is thus not `edit trajectory`; it is `harvest a consequence and spend it elsewhere`.

### Risk
Impulse has direction and magnitude. If the UI exposes vectors/numbers, the game becomes homework. The graybox must communicate stored impact through arrow length, orientation, animation and snapping without arithmetic.

### Decision
**SURVIVES STRONGLY.**

No close current analogue found for capture-and-transfer of collision outcome as the primary verb. It has one of the cleanest one-week falsification tests in the field.

## A4 — G3C18 The Room Remembers

### Current analogue pressure
Temporal echo/recording puzzles are visibly occupied:
- `Echoed` (released July 22, 2026) records the player's movement as echoes used to solve puzzles;
- `Echo Shift` stores up to three 15-second temporal echoes that repeat prior action sequences;
- many older clone/echo puzzles share the same broad grammar.

Sources checked 2026-08-19:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4749380/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4199420/Echo_Shift/

### Distinction test
The original seed says moved objects leave temporary physical after-states. That can be distinguished from replaying clones only if the memory is **spatial residue**, not autonomous action playback.

However, a muted clip of an object plus its previous position is likely to be read as `echo/clone puzzle` unless presentation is exceptionally clear.

### Decision
**DOWNGRADE TO RESERVE.**

Do not spend tournament bandwidth unless later mutation gives history a clearly non-echo function, e.g. accumulated wear/deformation as geometry rather than duplicated past state.

## A5 — G3C25 Rule Relay

### Current analogue pressure
Again, `Baba Is You` establishes a very high bar for rule manipulation. The candidate avoids sentence grammar by allowing one portable rule token to be physically carried between hosts.

Source:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/736260/Baba_Is_You/

### Distinction test
Strong form:
- there is exactly one `BOUNCY` seal in the room;
- player physically carries it;
- attaching it to the floor changes traversal;
- moving it to a crate makes the crate a launch object but removes bounce from the floor;
- carrying the seal itself occupies hands/position/action economy.

Weak form:
- dozens of words, syntactic conditions, rule boards or generic programming logic.

### Decision
**SURVIVES, BUT BELOW MOVING LABELS.**

It is mechanically clean but overlaps A1. The tournament should probably advance only one of A1/A5 unless their graybox loops prove materially different: A1 is property redistribution among world objects; A5 is one scarce rule as a carried traversal resource.

## A6 — G3C30 Single-Pixel Kingdom

### Current analogue pressure
Search finds titles named `One Pixel`, `Pixel`, and similar minimalist pixel games, but their mechanics are conventional platforming/action. No close current result found where one moving pixel temporarily confers solidity/color/behavior to touched structures.

Sources checked:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/1541410/One_Pixel/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/2057300/Pixel/

### Distinction test
The visual austerity is not itself a hook. The pixel must be a **moving state carrier**:
- touching ghost geometry makes it solid for a short trail window;
- passing through an enemy transfers its color/behavior to the pixel;
- the pixel then gives that state to the next object it touches;
- movement routes are therefore state routes.

If it turns into `tiny dot avoids hazards`, reject immediately.

### Decision
**SURVIVES STRONGLY.**

Very cheap to prototype, highly portfolio-distinct, and no close mechanic found. Existential risk is whether the stripped presentation feels intentional and pleasurable instead of unfinished.

## A7 — G3C34 Object Parliament

### Current analogue pressure
`Gerrymap` demonstrates that voting/political logic can sustain a compact puzzle game, but its verb is drawing voting districts from census data. No close current result surfaced for adjacency-based object voting where spatial seating itself changes material laws.

Source checked:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4120690/Gerrymap/

### Distinction test
The candidate should avoid real-world legislative simulation. The fantasy is a physical toy parliament:
- chairs/objects vote based on immediate neighbors and visible traits;
- moving one object changes a local coalition;
- passed motions directly alter the room (`gravity down`, `doors public`, `red objects taxable`, etc.);
- the player solves by arranging constituencies rather than choosing dialogue.

### Risk
This can become graph optimization with funny hats. The vote must resolve quickly and visibly, and laws must transform the playable space rather than only a score panel.

### Decision
**SURVIVES.**

Strong systemic concept with low asset burden, but its first 30 seconds are harder to sell than mass/impulse/property transfer.

## A10 — G3C01 Debt Garden

### Current analogue pressure
Gardening puzzles and debt/farm strategy both exist currently:
- `PuzzlePlants` (2026) is a compact gardening logic game around plant-neighbor/environment needs;
- `Bean & Debt` (Q3 2026) explicitly combines farming with escalating debt and resource tradeoffs.

Sources checked:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4742430/PuzzlePlants/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4515610/Bean__Debt/

### Distinction test
The candidate cannot be sold as `garden while paying debt`. That wording is now directly contaminated.

The surviving mechanic is **borrowing a plant's future state into the present**:
- pull tomorrow's fruit into today;
- the plant visibly becomes a future-depleted version for subsequent cycles;
- borrowing growth from several plants compounds ecological obligations;
- repayment may require returning biomass/time/growth capacity, not coins.

The word `debt` should probably leave the eventual title/marketing if the concept survives.

### Decision
**SURVIVES AS A MUTATED TEMPORAL-BOTANY SYSTEM, NOT AS DEBT/FARM MANAGEMENT.**

Good systemic potential, but the first trailer must show time-state borrowing directly or it will be mistaken for another farming resource game.

## A11 — G3C10 Pressure Cooked Planet

### Current analogue pressure
Targeted search did not surface a close primary-mechanic analogue centered on rotating one tiny planet to route heat, shadow and pressure sectors across coupled habitats/machines.

The broader risks are familiar: orbital/planet puzzle games exist, and environmental-variable puzzles can become disguised sliders.

### Distinction test
The planet must be the only major manipulated object. The repeated action is physical rotation/tilt causing several fields to move together:
- sun-facing side heats;
- shadow freezes/condenses;
- rotation changes pressure/wind circulation through visible channels;
- habitats/machines respond spatially.

No numeric meteorological dashboard should be required.

### Decision
**SURVIVES STRONGLY.**

One-object-world design is cheap, tactile and visually coherent. Existential risk is whether three coupled fields remain intuitive rather than turning into spherical spreadsheet logic.

## A12 — G3C29 Fusebox Parkour

### Current analogue pressure
`ReWire` (2025) is a grid-based circuit puzzle. Electricity and switch-platforming are common, but the targeted search did not find a close current game whose primary loop is **rewiring traversal geometry while actively moving through it**.

Source checked:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/2114100/ReWire/

### Distinction test
The action must happen inside motion:
- character wall-runs toward a dead panel;
- player reroutes one circuit while slowed but not paused;
- powered wall becomes solid as they arrive;
- stealing power from that wall disables the platform behind them;
- routing decisions and movement execution are one continuous verb.

If the player stops at safe terminals and solves static circuits before platforming, this is just two familiar games stapled together.

### Decision
**SURVIVES STRONGLY.**

High portfolio diversity and demo strength. Main risks: control complexity, readability at speed, and the possibility that optimal players simply pause/rewire in safety.

# 4. Run-2 expansion — 12 underrepresented concepts

These are new seeds, not canon. They deliberately broaden beyond property-transfer puzzles.

## G3C41 — **Receipt Duel**
Territory: tactile economy / score attack.

Repeated verb: physically tear one receipt line item and staple it onto another customer's bill while a tiny shop simulation immediately rebalances queues, satisfaction and margin.

Opportunity: economy manipulation is spatial/tactile instead of spreadsheet pricing.

Kill risk: becomes bookkeeping comedy rather than fast systemic play.

## G3C42 — **Blind Spot Courier**
Territory: asymmetric information / direct action.

Repeated verb: sprint a courier through a compact room while switching which surveillance layer you are allowed to see; what you hide from yourself is also hidden from certain enemies.

Opportunity: information denial is an active traversal resource.

Kill risk: opacity/frustration; may resemble stealth-camera games.

## G3C43 — **Punch Clock Orchestra**
Territory: score attack / rhythm without strict beat matching.

Repeated verb: punch autonomous workers in/out of one machine loop; their overlapping work cycles create production plus music, and the player scores coherent chains without needing music-game timing accuracy.

Opportunity: logistics expressed as audible polyrhythm.

Kill risk: collapses into idle optimization or Factorio-lite.

## G3C44 — **Turnless Chess Fire**
Territory: turnless tactics.

Repeated verb: slide one unit/cover tile while every enemy continuously telegraphs its next deterministic attack; moving changes attack clocks rather than ending a turn.

Opportunity: Into-the-Breach-like prediction without turn boundaries.

Kill risk: readability and pause dependence; could just be real-time tactics with forecast lines.

## G3C45 — **Desktop Gravity Well**
Territory: desktop/interface-native.

Repeated verb: place one gravity well on the desktop; tiny game objects orbit around the **edges of actual windows**, making window arrangement part of level geometry.

Opportunity: desktop integration materially changes play, unlike idle companions.

Kill risk: OS/security/multi-monitor fragility and poor Steam Deck fit.

## G3C46 — **One-Hand Smuggler**
Territory: direct action / constrained embodiment.

Repeated verb: you control one hand carrying contraband through a moving scanner maze; grabbing one thing means releasing another and changing hand shape/orientation.

Opportunity: one-object physical action with expressive mastery and low environment scope.

Kill risk: physics-grab frustration and novelty fatigue.

## G3C47 — **Rumor Engine**
Territory: non-physics simulation / asymmetric information.

Repeated verb: whisper one short icon-token rumor to one agent; the rumor mutates deterministically as it passes through personality filters, changing crowd behavior.

Opportunity: social simulation with tiny transparent grammar instead of dialogue trees.

Kill risk: indirect waiting and explanation burden.

## G3C48 — **Auction of One Thing**
Territory: tactile economy / adversarial single-player.

Repeated verb: alter one visible property of a single artifact before each bidder reacts; the same object is reinterpreted by distinct deterministic bidder rules.

Opportunity: one-object economy, readable character systems, tiny asset footprint.

Kill risk: content depends too heavily on authored bidder personalities/text.

## G3C49 — **Wallclock Heist**
Territory: turnless tactics / temporal action.

Repeated verb: physically drag one second of time from one object to another—freeze a camera for one second by accelerating a door or guard elsewhere.

Opportunity: conserved time as action economy; visually strong simultaneous tradeoff.

Kill risk: time-transfer can become abstract and state-heavy; possibly too close to property-conservation cluster.

## G3C50 — **Crash Blossom**
Territory: score attack / direct action.

Repeated verb: deliberately collide one controllable seed-pod with moving surfaces; each impact sprouts a temporary platform at the collision normal, and chains create traversal.

Opportunity: collision is both movement failure and level construction.

Kill risk: resembles projectile/platform creation if impact feel is weak.

## G3C51 — **Signal Liar**
Territory: asymmetric information / interface-native.

Repeated verb: falsify exactly one sensor reading in a live machine; autonomous subsystems act on the lie while the physical world remains truthful.

Opportunity: exploit differences between sensed and actual state without text programming.

Kill risk: screen-heavy presentation and indirect plan/watch structure.

## G3C52 — **Anchor Market**
Territory: tactile economy / physics-light action.

Repeated verb: buy/sell *anchoring rights* between moving objects; an anchored object becomes world-fixed, but the anchor token must come from something else that immediately starts drifting.

Opportunity: conserved stability as a visually obvious economy.

Kill risk: may be a simpler/weaker cousin of Bureau of Lost Weight.

# 7. Pairwise redundancy cuts

The factory should not carry every version of the same transferable-property insight into Phase 2.

## Semantic-rule pair: G3C06 vs G3C25
Advance **Museum of Moving Labels** as the primary semantic-property candidate. Keep Rule Relay as reserve.

Reason: Moving Labels can express scarcity by one-property-one-label conservation while producing stronger comedy/visual transformation. Rule Relay's carried-token traversal is cleaner but closer to conventional `rule object` puzzle framing.

## Conservation family: G3C12 vs G3C49
Advance **both for now**, because their play rhythms differ:
- Bureau of Lost Weight = slower tactile physics/resource puzzle;
- Wallclock Heist = action/stealth time allocation.

But the Phase-2 tournament should not allow both to reach the final three unless they prove different audiences and minute-to-minute loops.

## Physical motion transfer: G3C15 vs G3C50
Advance both provisionally:
- Borrowed Collision transfers a stored impact consequence between systems;
- Crash Blossom turns the collision itself into traversal construction.

Borrowed Collision currently has the stronger novelty thesis; Crash Blossom has the stronger immediate action-feel thesis.
