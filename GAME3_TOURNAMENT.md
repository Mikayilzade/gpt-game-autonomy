# 1. G3C30 — SINGLE-PIXEL KINGDOM

## 15-minute demo
0–2: move one pixel; touch a SOLID donor; carried-state glyph appears.
2–4: deposit SOLID into ghost bridge; donor temporarily loses it; cross.
4–6: HOT carrier melts one gate but cannot cross water while hot.
6–8: COLOR state opens matching gate; non-color icon pattern mirrors the state.
8–11: first two-step relay: absorb HOT, transfer to machine, absorb SOLID, restore path.
11–13: first collateral tradeoff: moving a state off an object disables the route behind.
13–15: compact synthesis requiring three transfers in an order chosen by the player, with restart/undo and one optional efficiency mark.

## Representative mature 30-minute session
Three connected 20×12 rooms share six canonical state families. The player routes the pixel through moving carriers, temporarily borrows a state from one room, sends it through a relay object, deposits it in another, then returns a different state to restore the escape path. The session alternates 10–30 second traversal bursts with short state-routing decisions; no inventory screen exists.

## Dominant exploit + smallest counter-rule
Exploit: carry SOLID almost everywhere.
Counter-rule: **receiver compatibility + carrier interference**, not arbitrary level bans. Each state has a small visible interaction class: SOLID enables collision but blocks passage through mesh; HOT melts ice but damages organic receivers; HEAVY permits pressure but prevents lift rails; ALIVE activates biological interfaces but attracts hazards. No state is globally superior.

## 24 situation families / genuine transformations
**T1 — route enable/disable:** 1 ghost bridge, 2 donor floor disappears, 3 one-way restoration, 4 two-room relay, 5 state-gated shortcut, 6 return-path repair.
**T2 — compatibility tradeoff:** 7 HOT vs water, 8 HEAVY vs lift, 9 ALIVE vs predator, 10 COLOR vs camouflage gate, 11 SOLID vs mesh, 12 CONDUCTIVE vs shock field.
**T3 — order/state logistics:** 13 A->B->C relay, 14 temporary donor cooldown, 15 moving donor timing, 16 receiver occupied by wrong state, 17 swap two critical states, 18 preserve one state for exit.
**T4 — motion/mastery:** 19 transfer while chased, 20 minimal-transfer route, 21 moving receiver intercept, 22 state trail as temporary floor, 23 carry state through hazard loop, 24 multi-room no-reset chain.

Genuine transformations: **4**. The 24 situations are not merely skins because route topology, compatibility, ordering and motion execution ask materially different questions.

## Burden 1–5
Art 1 | UI/readability 4 | simulation 2 | content 4 | QA 4.

## Most likely negative review
“Cool minimalist gimmick, but after an hour I’m just carrying the right color/property from A to B.”

## Strongest defensible advantage
The **player avatar itself is the scarce moving state carrier**. Fresh `LeiLei` materially weakens generic property-transfer whitespace, but Single-Pixel remains distinct if transfer cannot happen at range and movement route is inseparable from state transport.

## Prototype kill
Kill if: >50% mature scenarios begin with same state; >25% failures come from state-readability confusion; or testers describe it primarily as property-swapping puzzle rather than movement-through-state logistics.

**Tournament result: FINALIST.** LeiLei lowers whitespace score, but the carrier-route embodiment still earns another round.

# 2. G3C12 — BUREAU OF LOST WEIGHT

## 15-minute demo
0–2: detach 10 kg certificate from crate; crate becomes movable.
2–4: attach mass to pressure platform; door opens.
4–6: balloon receives mass and sinks; introduces buoyancy.
6–8: overloaded shelf breaks, proving mass sinks are not free.
8–11: move 20 kg across three objects to satisfy lift capacity + pressure switch simultaneously.
11–13: first inertia problem uses heavier cart for momentum but lighter cargo for transport.
13–15: synthesis where every useful transfer creates a visible collateral consequence and multiple valid distributions exist.

## Mature 30-minute session
A compact facility has 7–9 objects and 4–6 discrete mass certificates. The player redistributes a conserved total through support, buoyancy, inertia and load-capacity systems, sometimes moving certificates physically with a carrier or transfer station. The central reasoning is not “make object light” but “where must this mass live while I use the consequence elsewhere?”

## Dominant exploit + smallest counter-rule
Exploit: dump mass into one irrelevant anchored object.
Counter-rule: **every certificate host participates in a visible capacity/load graph**. Anchored objects still have support capacity, pressure, deformation or transport consequences. No per-level “cannot receive mass” rule unless physically explained by certificate compatibility.

## 24 situation families
**T1 — accessibility/capacity:** 1 lighten crate, 2 elevator cap, 3 bridge load, 4 carry limit, 5 shelf capacity, 6 floating platform.
**T2 — useful heaviness:** 7 pressure plate, 8 anchor against wind, 9 break weak floor, 10 momentum cart, 11 counterweight, 12 compress spring.
**T3 — conservation topology:** 13 three-way distribution, 14 temporary storage, 15 moving mass sink, 16 locked receiver, 17 chained transfer station, 18 return-mass exit requirement.
**T4 — dynamic consequence:** 19 buoyancy swap, 20 collision momentum, 21 oscillating lift, 22 load-bearing sequence, 23 mass transfer mid-route, 24 minimal-host mastery.

Genuine transformations: **4 strong**; several physics expressions all derive from one conservation economy.

## Burden
Art 2 | UI 3 | simulation 4 | content 4 | QA 5.

## Most likely negative review
“Great idea, but every puzzle eventually becomes parking spare kilograms in whatever object the designer didn’t protect.”

## Strongest advantage
Unlike existing mass-manipulation games, **mass is conserved and transferable, so every benefit necessarily moves a cost somewhere else**.

## Prototype kill
Kill if one sink object is best in >40% mature test scenarios, simplified physics feels arbitrary to >2/5 testers, or ordinary completion requires timing-sensitive free physics.

**Tournament result: FINALIST.** One of the strongest systemic identities, but highest deterministic-physics QA burden among puzzle finalists.

# 3. G3C15 — BORROWED COLLISION

## 15-minute demo
0–2: crate hits bumper; impact becomes a visible arrow token.
2–4: stamp token onto stationary cart; cart launches.
4–6: snap token through a 90° socket; learn direction can be transformed only by physical devices.
6–8: weak vs strong impulse bands introduced without numbers.
8–10: use one impact on yourself for traversal.
10–12: donor collision is consumed; cannot duplicate token.
12–15: first chain where one impact opens route but preserving a second, weaker impact avoids breaking fragile cargo.

## Mature 30-minute session
Several compact rooms share a small impulse inventory. The player engineers donor collisions, harvests quantized outcomes, routes them through direction sockets, spends them on doors/carts/self-launches, and deliberately avoids wasting high-energy impulses on fragile receivers. Short action moments are always bounded by predictable snap directions and bands.

## Dominant exploit + smallest counter-rule
Exploit: manufacture maximum-strength collision repeatedly.
Counter-rule: **source exhaustion + receiver windows**. Donors have a finite reset/state cost, and many receivers reward a specific safe band rather than maximum force. Strong impulses are sometimes harmful because fragility/overshoot is intrinsic, not because “max force forbidden.”

## 24 situation families
**T1 — transport:** 1 launch cart, 2 open impact door, 3 self-jump, 4 knock platform, 5 propel raft, 6 move hanging mass.
**T2 — direction routing:** 7 90° socket, 8 reverse socket, 9 split/rebound chain, 10 choose receiver order, 11 moving socket, 12 transfer between rooms.
**T3 — magnitude choice:** 13 fragile crate, 14 exact band switch, 15 overshoot hazard, 16 combine two weak impulses, 17 reserve strong token, 18 damp through absorber.
**T4 — causal chaining/action:** 19 collision creates donor for next, 20 self-launch harvest, 21 moving receiver timing, 22 minimal-impact mastery, 23 rescue falling body, 24 chain under limited reset.

Genuine transformations: **4** with unusually coherent shared vocabulary.

## Burden
Art 2 | UI 4 | simulation 4 | content 4 | QA 5.

## Most likely negative review
“Neat vector gimmick, but I spend more time guessing arrow strength and angle than solving.”

## Strongest advantage
**A resolved physical consequence becomes a portable resource.** No close searched game owns collision-output harvesting as its primary loop.

## Prototype kill
Kill if players ask for numeric vectors, frame timing changes the captured result, strongest impulse solves >50% mature cases, or precision aiming is required.

**Tournament result: FINALIST — current leader on novelty/compact rule depth.**

# 4. G3C29 — FUSEBOX PARKOUR

## 15-minute demo
0–2: run toward gap; route one power line in slow time; platform materializes.
2–4: previous platform loses power and vanishes behind player.
4–6: wall-run surface and lift compete for same capacity.
6–8: moving load temporarily overloads a branch.
8–10: player chooses safe route or faster power handoff.
10–12: first section with no safe terminal; rewiring must happen during movement.
12–15: 30–45 second continuous chain with one alternate route and immediate retry ghost.

## Mature 30-minute session
Four to six short traversal stages, each 3–6 minutes, combine momentum and a small power graph. The player repeatedly steals capacity from completed geometry and sends it forward, sometimes buffering power in a temporary capacitor. Mastery is continuous flow with fewer safety stops, but baseline remains accessible with strong slowdown.

## Dominant exploit + smallest counter-rule
Exploit: stop safely and solve wiring statically.
Counter-rule: **power follows a moving load / decaying buffer**. Some route-critical capacity exists only while the runner carries or passes a charge window, so stopping is allowed but cannot pre-solve the entire route. This preserves accessibility slowdown while keeping motion causally relevant.

## 24 situation families
**T1 — basic handoff:** 1 floor->wall, 2 wall->lift, 3 lift->bridge, 4 bridge->door, 5 two-choice branch, 6 return path.
**T2 — capacity:** 7 one source two loads, 8 overdraw, 9 temporary capacitor, 10 moving battery, 11 priority load, 12 sacrificial safety light.
**T3 — movement coupling:** 13 wall-run timing, 14 falling platform, 15 moving lift handoff, 16 momentum gate, 17 midair reroute, 18 chase section.
**T4 — expressive route/mastery:** 19 safe vs fast route, 20 no-stop chain, 21 minimal reroutes, 22 reverse route, 23 recovery after missed handoff, 24 branching speedrun line.

Genuine transformations: **4**, though T3/T4 depend heavily on feel rather than logic alone.

## Burden
Art 3 | UI 4 | feel/simulation 5 | content 3 | QA 5.

## Most likely negative review
“Constantly opening the wiring overlay kills the parkour flow; with slowdown it’s trivial, without it it’s fiddly.”

## Strongest advantage
Circuit manipulation is not a separate puzzle room: **the electrical topology is the traversal surface and is edited while moving**. Existing circuit/platform games validate components but do not erase the feel risk.

## Prototype kill
Kill if >45% active time is stationary/overlay time, controller produces >2 accidental selections/minute after tutorial, safe-terminal play clears >40% mature stages, or accessibility slowdown removes the need for route planning entirely.

**Tournament result: FINALIST — highest portfolio diversity, highest feel/input risk.**

# 5. G3C10 — PRESSURE COOKED PLANET

## 15-minute demo
0–3: rotate 12-step planet to warm a frozen habitat.
3–5: same rotation shades another habitat; first coupled tradeoff.
5–7: pressure channel and vent appear; heating creates useful pressure.
7–10: storage tank introduces one-cycle lag; player must anticipate rather than nudge.
10–12: phase threshold melts ice and opens water path.
12–15: three-condition equilibrium with multiple valid orientations over a short 3-cycle stability window.

## Mature 30-minute session
One planet remains the sole manipulated object. Four environmental fields and 6–8 devices respond across discrete sectors. The player sets orientation, steps short deterministic cycles, and uses storage/lag to create temporary windows. Mature play is about coupled-state trajectories rather than locating one permanent angle.

## Dominant exploit + smallest counter-rule
Exploit: find stable orientation and wait.
Counter-rule: **bounded lag/storage + incompatible temporal needs**. Mature goals require a short sequence of states, e.g. heat now to charge pressure, rotate to shade before habitat overheats, spend stored pressure after the rotation. No arbitrary “must rotate N times” objectives.

## 24 situation families
**T1 — static coupling:** 1 warm A/cool B, 2 melt ice, 3 keep greenhouse shaded, 4 solar machine, 5 pressure vent, 6 two-zone conflict.
**T2 — lag/storage:** 7 heat reservoir, 8 pressure tank, 9 delayed freeze, 10 charge then rotate, 11 cooldown window, 12 release stored state.
**T3 — network consequence:** 13 melt opens channel, 14 steam drives turbine, 15 pressure closes valve, 16 shade condenses water, 17 habitat changes airflow, 18 one sector feeds another.
**T4 — temporal equilibrium:** 19 three-cycle stability, 20 oscillating safe band, 21 asymmetric habitat sequence, 22 minimal rotations, 23 emergency recovery, 24 multi-stage production without numeric economy.

Genuine transformations: **4**, but presentation risks making them all feel like gauge balancing.

## Burden
Art 3 | UI 5 | simulation 3 | content 4 | QA 4.

## Most likely negative review
“Pretty planet, but I’m just rotating one notch at a time until the gauges are green.”

## Strongest advantage
One tactile global action moves several environmental systems simultaneously; no close targeted current analogue was found.

## Prototype kill
Kill if >30% inputs are hypothesis-free single nudges, stable waiting solves >40% mature cases, or players need numeric dashboards to predict field behavior.

**Tournament result: CUT TO RESERVE.** Strong and buildable, but its boring strategy is too close to the central verb and its 10-second hook is weaker than the four finalists.

# 6. G3C01M — FUTURE-BORROW BOTANY

## 15-minute demo
0–3: pull tomorrow’s fruiting state into present; harvest; visible future ghost becomes depleted.
3–5: advance one cycle; debt manifests in the plant, not a number.
5–7: repay biomass to restore future stage.
7–10: borrow from plant A to rescue plant B; soil/light interaction creates collateral effect.
10–12: one sacrificial-plant strategy visibly damages shared soil capacity.
12–15: 4-cycle puzzle requiring two different species and a chosen repayment order.

## Mature 30-minute session
A 6–10 cell garden exposes a 4–6 cycle horizon. The player borrows growth stages, spends fruit/biomass now, advances deterministic cycles, and repairs future obligations through species interactions, shared soil/light and bounded adjacency. No real-time waiting, coins or farming grind.

## Dominant exploit + smallest counter-rule
Exploit: sacrifice one plant forever.
Counter-rule: **shared ecological substrate**. Repeated future depletion reduces a visible shared soil/light service the rest of the garden needs, while diverse species can restore different substrate properties. Sacrifice remains a legal tactic but cannot be a universal free sink.

## 24 situation families
**T1 — simple temporal loan:** 1 fruit now, 2 growth now, 3 root now, 4 flower now, 5 repay next cycle, 6 choose loan size.
**T2 — cross-plant ecology:** 7 borrow A help B, 8 shade tradeoff, 9 soil depletion, 10 nitrogen/restoration analogue, 11 adjacency benefit, 12 shared water capacity.
**T3 — horizon/order:** 13 two-cycle repayment, 14 deadline harvest, 15 staggered loans, 16 chain repayment, 17 preserve future seed, 18 restore before final cycle.
**T4 — mastery/robustness:** 19 no permanent depletion, 20 minimal borrow, 21 alternate species solution, 22 delayed harvest route, 23 rescue damaged plot, 24 multi-goal seasonal synthesis.

Genuine transformations: **4**, though T1/T3 can drift toward spreadsheet planning if the future ghosts are not excellent.

## Burden
Art 3 | UI 5 | simulation 3 | content 4 | QA 4.

## Most likely negative review
“Cute idea, but it’s basically a resource spreadsheet with plants and a four-turn preview.”

## Strongest advantage
The debt is **a borrowed future physical state**, not money or time currency. Plant-growth competitors do not currently own this exact temporal verb.

## Prototype kill
Kill if players discuss yields/numbers more than visible future plant states, one sacrificial plant supports >40% mature solutions, or readable horizon must exceed 6 cycles.

**Tournament result: CUT TO RESERVE.** Strong systems concept, but explanation burden and temporal-UI risk are higher than finalists while trailer immediacy is lower.

# 7. G3C06 — MUSEUM OF MOVING LABELS

## Fresh market collision
`LeiLei` is a direct current/upcoming competitor: its Steam description explicitly says the player steals properties from objects and injects/transfers them into others, with traits such as Push, Slide, Kill, Key, Mud, Water and Win. That is not merely Baba-like rule manipulation; it occupies the same core **property transfer between concrete objects** verb that allowed Museum to survive Phase 1.

The museum plaque presentation remains charming, but presentation/theme is no longer enough to justify carrying this candidate into a final duel.

## 15-minute demo if preserved historically
Peel FRAGILE -> door shatters; transfer HEAVY -> pressure switch; move CONDUCTIVE -> machine path; first harmful-label parking problem; 4-label synthesis.

## Exploit
Park harmful labels on irrelevant objects / use universally powerful labels.

## 24-situation proof
The property matrix can trivially generate >24 cases, but after LeiLei this no longer answers the market-distinction question. A different museum skin plus scarcity does not create enough distance.

## Burden
Art 2 | UI 3 | simulation 3 | content 5 | QA 5.

## Most likely negative review
“Baba/LeiLei-style property swapping in a museum theme.”

## Strongest remaining advantage
Physical plaque scarcity and collateral donor loss are cleaner than sentence grammar, but not enough against an upcoming title already marketing direct property theft/transfer.

## Prototype kill
**Market kill triggered before prototype.** Only revive as a future concept if mutated so the repeated verb is no longer property transfer.

**Tournament result: ELIMINATED.**

# 9. Finalist reduction

## F1 — BORROWED COLLISION
Current leader on novel verb + compact systemic depth. Main existential issue: can impulse be understood without vector math and resolved deterministically without twitch precision?

## F2 — SINGLE-PIXEL KINGDOM
Best scope/identity ratio and cheapest falsification test. Fresh LeiLei discovery creates adjacent property-transfer pressure, so final round must prove that movement as a physical carrier is the actual game, not a cosmetic input method for trait swapping.

## F3 — BUREAU OF LOST WEIGHT
Best conservation economy and strongest instantly funny before/after clip. Must prove mass-sink exploit and deterministic physics do not consume the whole design.

## F4 — FUSEBOX PARKOUR
Highest action/portfolio upside and strongest skill-expression route. Must prove wiring and movement are one continuous verb rather than a circuit puzzle repeatedly interrupting platforming.

### Reserves
1. **Pressure Cooked Planet** — first reserve; excellent one-object scope, but nudge/wait strategy too close to central interaction.
2. **Future-Borrow Botany** — second reserve; good temporal system, weaker clip + temporal UI burden.

### Eliminated this round
- **Museum of Moving Labels** — current direct property-transfer collision with `LeiLei`; no longer enough whitespace.
