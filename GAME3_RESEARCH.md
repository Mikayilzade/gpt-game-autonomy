# 2. Current market signals — August 2026

## 2.1 Discovery is the primary macro-risk
June 2026 Steam Next Fest contained more than 4,000 playable demos according to GameSpot. Independent datasets place the total even higher depending on counting method. The practical conclusion is stable: participation itself does not create discovery.

Sources checked 2026-08-19:
- https://www.gamespot.com/articles/steam-next-fest-june-2026-25-of-the-best-demos-you-can-play-right-now/
- https://www.sakujodata.com/reports/steam-next-fest-june-2026

SakujoData's post-fest dataset reports that roughly the top 4% of tracked games captured about 70% of modeled wishlist gains and that demo players correlated with wishlist gains more strongly than Twitch viewership.

**Design implication:** Game #003 should optimize for a playable, understandable demo and a hook that survives extreme catalog competition. Trailer novelty without playable conversion is insufficient.

## 2.2 Supply pressure continues to rise
Two 2026 research papers using large Steam datasets describe extreme attention concentration and continued release-volume growth. Exact future market forecasts are uncertain, but the direction strongly argues against generic low-cost clones.

Sources:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25010
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07825

**Design implication:** cheap production is valuable only if paired with unusually strong identity. AI/automation reducing production cost makes generic concepts less, not more, defensible.

## 2.3 Simulation has demand but job-sim surface is crowded
Steam's current upcoming/recent listings visibly contain many repair, shop, job and cozy management simulators. Current Next Fest analysis also suggests Simulation can over-index commercially, but this does not imply every job-sim is attractive.

Sources:
- https://store.steampowered.com/explore/upcoming/
- https://www.sakujodata.com/reports/steam-next-fest-june-2026

**Design implication:** simulation remains useful as a systemic substrate, but avoid `ordinary occupation + repetitive tool motions + upgrades` unless the verb itself is genuinely unusual.

## 2.4 Novelty can come from one physically legible verb
Current editorial coverage continues to reward highly specific, visually obvious actions, including absurd bodily interactions, movement transformations and interface-native fantasies. This is consistent with the factory principle that a game should ideally explain itself in one sentence/GIF.

2026 reference:
- https://www.theverge.com/games/894511/gdc-2026-best-indie-games

**Design implication:** prefer concepts where the action itself creates the clip, rather than lore/theme needing to explain why a familiar genre is different.

## 2.5 Strategy/adventure oversupply warns against genre label alone
The June 2026 Next Fest dataset cited above reports Strategy and Adventure as heavily represented relative to their modeled wishlist share, while Simulation/RPG over-indexed.

This is not a permanent genre law. It is a warning that `indie strategy game` or `narrative adventure` without a unique interaction thesis is weak positioning.

## 2.6 Demo quality is part of product design
With thousands of simultaneous demos, first-session friction, PC performance, controller support and immediate comprehension become discovery variables, not polish-only concerns.

Reference:
- https://www.gamespot.com/articles/steam-next-fest-june-2026-25-of-the-best-demos-you-can-play-right-now/

**Design implication:** every finalist must specify a 10-second muted clip and a 5–15 minute demo proof before Product Thesis lock.

# 3. Game #003 opportunity rules

For this cycle, candidates are favored when they have:
1. a repeated 5–15 second verb with immediate feedback;
2. a one-sentence hook and mute-video readability;
3. systemic depth from <=8 core rule families where possible;
4. a small-team path to 5–10+ hours without enormous bespoke content;
5. a cheap graybox that can kill the concept early;
6. full single-player value without server/network dependency;
7. controller viability or a very strong reason not to require it;
8. no reliance on photorealism, voice acting volume or thousands of bespoke animations;
9. a strong demo endpoint within 15–25 minutes;
10. enough conceptual distance from generic job sims, survivor-likes, deckbuilder roguelites, retail sims and pure cozy-idle companions.

Hard warnings:
- `known genre + one cosmetic noun`;
- a premise whose fun appears only after meta progression;
- rules too abstract to show in motion;
- a system whose only depth is more numbers/content;
- a dominant strategy obvious from the pitch;
- procedural generation used to conceal thin interaction;
- multiplayer needed to create stories;
- freeform generative AI dependency as the main content source;
- simulation fidelity that must be physically realistic before it becomes intuitive.

# 5. Initial destructive triage

This is deliberately **not** the final tournament. It eliminates concepts that already carry obvious structural problems and elevates those deserving direct competitor research.

## Tier A — direct competitor checks next run

### A1 — G3C06 Museum of Moving Labels
Strongest semantic hook in this run: move a visible descriptive label and the labeled property transfers. Very low art requirement and potentially deep combinations (`fragile`, `heavy`, `alive`, `locked`, `wet`, etc.).

Must prove:
- property vocabulary can remain small and visual;
- does not become `Baba Is You`-like sentence construction with museum skin;
- label transfer creates tactile object manipulation, not textual programming.

### A2 — G3C12 Bureau of Lost Weight
Extremely readable: move a `10 kg` certificate from statue to balloon and reality obeys while total mass remains conserved. Strong comedy/physics clip potential.

Must prove:
- physics can be deterministic/forgiving enough for puzzles;
- mass-transfer interaction remains richer than `make thing light/heavy`;
- player does not solve everything by putting all weight into one irrelevant anchor.

### A3 — G3C15 Borrowed Collision
Capturing an impulse from one collision and replaying it elsewhere is unusual and has a potentially excellent repeated verb.

Must prove:
- visual language makes `stored impulse` instantly understandable;
- player feels agency rather than performing vector math;
- enough depth exists without dozens of modifiers.

### A4 — G3C18 The Room Remembers
Object history becoming temporary geometry/state has strong puzzle potential and a visually explainable identity.

Must prove:
- memory decay/overlap is legible;
- state space does not explode;
- not just `clone/echo puzzle` under different wording.

### A5 — G3C25 Rule Relay
A single carried rule token avoids the combinatorial sentence-building burden of generic rule-programming while preserving powerful system recombination.

Must prove:
- one rule at a time still creates hour-10 depth;
- physical carrying/transfer matters;
- direct competitors do not already own portable-rule puzzles.

### A6 — G3C30 Single-Pixel Kingdom
Potentially the lowest-asset, highest-identity candidate: one pixel confers temporary properties to touched entities/tiles.

Must prove:
- visual austerity is charming rather than cheap;
- core verb creates satisfying motion;
- enough semantic richness exists without making the pixel a universal magic cursor.

### A7 — G3C34 Object Parliament
Spatially moving agents/objects to alter adjacency-based voting can create legible graph logic with comedy and procedural recombination.

Must prove:
- player action feels physical, not spreadsheet politics;
- outcomes are locally readable;
- no large narrative/dialogue burden.

### A8 — G3C37 Wrong Size Department
Scale conservation is a powerful systemic constraint: increasing one object requires shrinking another. Strong visual before/after and many spatial consequences.

Must prove:
- enough whitespace beyond established size-manipulation games;
- conservation law creates genuinely different decisions;
- collision/camera/physics burden remains bounded.

### A9 — G3C40 Trajectory Locksmith
Capturing and transferring future motion curves can generate striking trailer moments and multi-object causality.

Must prove:
- not a path editor;
- trajectory ownership/transfer is understandable without math UI;
- controller input remains simple.

### A10 — G3C01 Debt Garden
The most promising non-physics systems concept in the batch. Borrow growth/resources from future plant states, causing visible later debt/compounding.

Must prove:
- the temporal debt is embodied visibly rather than becoming resource math;
- gardening is not merely cozy skin;
- first 15 minutes reveal the strategic inversion quickly.

### A11 — G3C10 Pressure Cooked Planet
One rotating world with heat/shadow/pressure sectors can provide a compact, tactile multi-system game with minimal content footprint.

Must prove:
- rotation is more than selecting the right orientation;
- interacting systems generate nontrivial dynamics;
- not a static celestial alignment puzzle.

### A12 — G3C29 Fusebox Parkour
Potential continuous action/puzzle product: movement and rewiring happen inside the same flow.

Must prove:
- rewiring has richer consequences than platform toggles;
- no pause-and-solve interruption;
- controller UI remains fluid.

# 6. Run-1 lessons

1. **Property transfer** appears stronger than generic property editing. Moving one bounded quantity/rule between objects creates a visible conservation/ownership constraint that naturally fights universal solutions.
2. **Conservation laws** are promising for low-content systemic games because every gain creates a visible cost elsewhere. Mass/scale/heat/impulse are especially legible, but each needs competitor checks.
3. **Semantic systems** remain fertile only if text is physically manipulated and consequences are embodied. Avoid sentence-programming clones.
4. **Action + editing** is a useful portfolio direction, but the edit must occur inside movement rather than `pause, configure, execute`.
5. **One-object worlds** (single planet/room/pixel/rule token) may create strong scope compression and screenshot identity.
6. The weakest candidates are those whose unusual noun masks a known interaction: traffic routing, magnet movement, sonar stealth, folding, shadow projection, job simulation.
7. Game #003 should not drift back into the map/authority/civic identity of Game #002 merely because those systems are easy to reason about on paper.

# 7. Phase-1 scoring model for Run 2

Each serious candidate will be scored 1–5 on:
- Hook / muted clip clarity — 15%
- Repeated core pleasure — 15%
- Systemic depth — 12.5%
- Hour-10 repetition resistance — 10%
- Scope fitness — 10%
- Graybox falsifiability — 10%
- Demo strength — 10%
- Tutorial burden — 5%
- Technical predictability — 7.5%
- Competitive whitespace — 5%

Hard-kill overrides:
1. stronger known game already owns substantially the same noun+verb+fantasy;
2. fun depends on unstable physics/simulation realism that cannot be simplified;
3. dominant universal strategy survives the concept's central constraint;
4. muted trailer looks like a common genre despite explanation claiming novelty;
5. 20+ meaningful situations require bespoke one-off mechanics/content;
6. accessibility adaptation deletes the core interaction;
7. first meaningful independent decision arrives too late for a 15–25 minute demo.
