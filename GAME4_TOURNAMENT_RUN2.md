# 2. Soundproof Smuggler — ACTIVE ENCOUNTER FALSIFICATION

## 2.1 180-second mature encounter timeline

Encounter graph: 8 rooms, 10 passage edges, one absorber rail with 4 legal edge-slots, two guards, one moving cart source, footsteps strength 2, thrown object strength 3, glass strength 4. Guard A threshold 2; Guard B threshold 3. Patrol cycles are deliberately short and partially player-driven.

- **0–12s:** player enters node P. Preview shows footsteps would reach A through east edge but not B. Player moves immediately; no waiting.
- **12–24s:** absorber shifted from E2 to E5 while walking along its rail. This suppresses cart noise toward A but leaves it audible to B.
- **24–39s:** player deliberately kicks cart. B investigates node B2; A remains on route. This is active selective-audibility setup, not passive patrol observation.
- **39–55s:** player crosses two rooms during B's self-created investigation window. One 3-second doorway hold is required for spatial timing.
- **55–72s:** glass objective becomes reachable. Preview shows breaking now reaches both guards. Player rotates absorber to E7, changing equal-cost route so A still hears but B does not.
- **72–88s:** break glass; A diverts. Player immediately backtracks one edge and uses A's diversion to pass central room.
- **88–105s:** moving cart reopens an acoustic route. Player cannot simply keep E7 blocked because footsteps then reach returning B through alternate edge.
- **105–124s:** absorber moved to E3; player throws object toward side room, intentionally making B hear it. Two guards now investigate different nodes.
- **124–145s:** player traverses objective corridor. A 5-second safety hold occurs because both spatial and acoustic routes briefly conflict.
- **145–163s:** player moves absorber once more while cart source travels; this prevents cart from recalling A into the escape path.
- **163–180s:** exit traversal and final loud action are safe because one guard is intentionally hearing the wrong route.

Explicit passive hold/wait: **8 seconds / 180 = 4.4%**. Even if traversal hesitation doubles this, the encounter remains far below the 20–25% existential ceiling.

## 2.2 Does the graph feel spatial?

Required presentation contract:
- propagation is drawn on actual doors/corridors, never as a detached node-map;
- preview begins at the physical source and animates through world passages;
- absorber is a world object on rails, not a UI toggle;
- guard hearing state is displayed over the guard and on the reached room boundary;
- no global graph overlay is required for normal play; optional accessibility preview may temporarily dim non-acoustic geometry.

This preserves spatial stealth rather than turning the game into graph software.

## 2.3 Dominance attack

Nearest-door blocking fails in the mature timeline because alternate routes and deliberate lures make `silence everything` strategically inferior. A valid mature encounter must contain at least one action where a target guard **must hear** the event while another must not.

**Run-2 verdict: SURVIVE / seed 1.** Waiting risk is no longer the strongest paper objection. Remaining existential issue is whether physical graph edits feel satisfying minute-to-minute rather than intellectually neat.

# 3. Umbrella Engine — 12 MATURE STATES / LOOKUP ATTACK

Frozen actuator states only: CLOSED, NARROW, WIDE, INVERTED. Existing classes only: wind, falling/rolling cargo, shelter-sensitive agents, passages, overhead hazards.

| # | Situation | Best local state | Why not pure lookup? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | lateral wind + empty corridor | WIDE | maximize translation |
| 2 | lateral wind + carried cargo | NARROW | retain cargo while accepting slower travel |
| 3 | narrow doorway + cargo | CLOSED | clears width but sheds cargo: may be strategically wrong |
| 4 | falling cargo + no wind | INVERTED | catch |
| 5 | falling cargo + shelter agent | WIDE/INVERTED conflict | catch vs shelter |
| 6 | lateral wind + shelter agent | NARROW/WIDE conflict | movement vs coverage |
| 7 | overhead hazard + lateral wind | CLOSED/NARROW conflict | exposure vs control |
| 8 | rolling cargo + crosswind | WIDE | deflect, but may displace player |
| 9 | narrow passage + shelter agent | NARROW | partial shelter/clearance tradeoff |
|10 | caught cargo + overhead hazard | NARROW | retain while reducing exposure |
|11 | opposing wind zone + cargo target | CLOSED then WIDE | sequencing matters |
|12 | shelter agent + falling cargo + doorway | no single dominant state | route/order is the puzzle |

State occupancy estimate across a deliberately varied mature route: CLOSED 20%, NARROW 31%, WIDE 30%, INVERTED 19%. No >60% state dominance.

However, the falsification exposes a deeper weakness: **8/12 states have an obvious locally correct response once the object/environment class is recognized.** The interesting cases are the multi-demand conflicts, meaning mature depth depends on authored overlap density. This is not fatal for a compact action-puzzle, but it raises content-design burden and makes hour-10 defense weaker than the top three.

Fresh precedent pressure also confirms umbrella affordances (glide/wind/protection/platform/catch) are individually familiar. The coherent four-state combination is ownable, but discovery cannot rely on `umbrella does many things` alone.

**Run-2 verdict: ELIMINATE from top set / preserve reserve.** Excellent demo and controller fit; insufficient evidence that four-state recognition avoids lookup repetition across 5–8 hours without increasingly bespoke compound encounters.

# 4. Seam Thief — SIX NON-PORTAL-EQUIVALENT PROOFS

A proof counts only if replacing the seam with a conventional entrance/exit portal would fail to preserve the puzzle's central consequence.

### Proof S1 — Continuous shared support
A long beam rests across two intervals that become one continuous support edge only after stitching. The beam's contact manifold spans the joined boundary. A portal could transfer a crossing body but does not naturally create continuous structural support along a shared edge.

### Proof S2 — Sweeper continuation + player reuse
A sweeper bar contacts/crosses the seam along its length and continues in mapped orientation while the player simultaneously uses the opposite side as traversable floor. The same adjacency affects hazard geometry and locomotion.

### Proof S3 — Reversed tangent ordering
Two objects approach different tangent positions on the source interval. Reversed endpoint mapping swaps their spatial order at the destination, which changes which pressure pads they reach. The reasoning is interval mapping, not entrance/exit placement.

### Proof S4 — Collateral enemy adjacency
Stitching a safe floor edge to an exit-side wall gives the player access but also makes an enemy patrol's boundary immediately continuous with the player's region. The edit changes a whole neighborhood relationship, not a private tunnel.

### Proof S5 — Edge occupancy denial
A moving body partially occupies a candidate interval, temporarily making seam commit illegal. Player must first manipulate body position, then stitch. Portal logic usually treats aperture occupancy after placement rather than topology-edit legality before commit.

### Proof S6 — One seam, three relationship changes
A single stitch (a) creates player route, (b) makes a roller enter a new gravity-relative surface orientation, and (c) completes support under a separate long body. The solution is valuable because one adjacency edit has simultaneous consequences.

## 4.1 Commit / crossing ordering

For tournament purposes freeze this deterministic ordering:
1. simulation reaches fixed tick boundary;
2. collect seam-edit command;
3. validate both intervals against occupancy/static/authored tags using pre-edit state;
4. if invalid, reject with reason and do not mutate world;
5. if valid, install adjacency transform atomically;
6. recompute contact graph for bodies touching either interval without advancing time;
7. resolve newly valid support/contact constraints; any state requiring penetration > epsilon invalidates commit and rolls back atomically;
8. advance dynamics;
9. crossings during that dynamics step map tangent coordinate and local velocity basis exactly once; mapped body receives a seam-crossing token for the remainder of the tick to prevent immediate recursive remap;
10. clear token at next fixed tick.

One active seam pair means recursive seam loops are excluded at current scope.

## 4.2 Portal-perception risk

Paper proof is now strong: all six families depend on adjacency/contact semantics broader than teleporting traversal. But visual perception remains empirical. The graybox kill threshold (>35% `portal` descriptions) stays mandatory.

**Run-2 verdict: SURVIVE / seed 2.** Highest systemic puzzle ceiling, with medium-high QA risk but now a clearer deterministic contract.

# 5. Command Wake — THREE-MINUTE OVERLAP SIMULATION

Agents A/B/C have deterministic graph routes. Commands: TURN_NEXT, HOLD_ONE_NODE, FOLLOW_WAKE. A wake segment carries owner, command, creation tick, expiry tick and sequence ID.

## 5.1 Priority / expiry contract

- crossing is detected only on leader center crossing a wake segment between fixed ticks;
- if several wake segments are crossed in one tick, resolve by earliest geometric crossing fraction, then lower sequence ID;
- each resolved crossing overwrites that agent's pending command only if the previous command has not yet been consumed; latest intentional crossing therefore wins for that agent;
- pending command expires after one owner routine cycle if unused;
- wake geometry itself persists 3.0 seconds baseline, then fades over 0.5 seconds; faded wakes cannot trigger;
- overlapping wakes never merge; each keeps owner pattern + directional glyph;
- max three simultaneously triggerable wake lanes inside a 2m readability radius; content validator rejects denser authored nodes.

## 5.2 180-second stress trace

**0–30s:** leader crosses A TURN_NEXT, then B HOLD. A changes next junction; B pauses one node. Two wakes overlap briefly but owner glyphs remain separate.

**30–60s:** A's new route crosses C's path. Leader crosses C FOLLOW_WAKE, then re-crosses A before A consumes its prior command; A's pending command is intentionally overwritten. This proves overwrite semantics but creates a visible cognitive spike.

**60–90s:** three wakes coexist near central junction. Content density cap forces one to be non-triggerable/fading before leader enters the 2m zone. Player has at most two meaningful choices at once.

**90–120s:** leader crosses B and C in the same fixed tick during a fast diagonal. Geometric crossing fraction resolves B first, C second; since they target different agents, both commands apply. UI shows two ordered 0.35s flashes without pausing.

**120–150s:** C command expires unused after its routine cycle; expiry is telegraphed by shrinking next-node ghost. Leader deliberately ignores it and uses A wake to open route.

**150–180s:** final gate requires A TURN_NEXT + B HOLD while leader avoids C wake. Three-agent state is legible only because commands remain one-cycle and predicted next nodes are ghosted.

## 5.3 Readability measurement model

Paper estimate under the frozen cap:
- simultaneous meaningful wake choices: median 1.7, max 3;
- pending commands visible at once: median 1.4, max 3;
- unavoidable mental stop points in trace: 3 events totaling ~11 seconds / 180 = **6.1%**;
- ambiguous same-tick resolution: deterministic, but likely not human-predictable without the geometric order preview; therefore the game should prevent same-tick multi-crossing from being required for optimal solutions.

Critical design constraint discovered: **four-agent maximum is a technical ceiling, not a target encounter density.** Normal mature encounters should use 2–3 commanded agents; a fourth can exist only with spatial separation.

**Run-2 verdict: SURVIVE / seed 3.** Strong action/tactics identity. Readability risk remains higher than Soundproof/Seam, but the overlap model survives on paper if density is validated aggressively.

# 6. Debris Sculptor — TEN FRACTURE DECISIONS / RECIPE-COLLAPSE ATTACK

Each decision must make both source consequence and harvested affordance matter.

1. Wall A: diagonal fracture yields WEDGE but opens low hazard sightline; horizontal yields STEP while preserving cover.
2. Pillar B: top fracture yields BLOCKER but lowers bridge support; base fracture yields ROLLER but collapses upper route.
3. Ramp C: tip fracture yields small WEDGE while retaining ramp; center fracture yields STEP but destroys ramp continuity.
4. Barrier D: vertical fracture yields two narrow BLOCKERS but opens wide player passage; diagonal yields one WEDGE and narrow passage.
5. Arch E: keystone fracture yields STEP but destabilizes gate; side fracture yields BLOCKER while gate remains usable.
6. Chute F: lower fracture yields ROLLER and changes source chute angle; upper yields WEDGE while preserving feed path.
7. Bridge G: end fracture yields STEP with bridge still crossable one-way; middle fracture yields two BLOCKERS but severs bridge.
8. Housing H: corner fracture yields WEDGE and exposes hazard timing; top fracture yields STEP while preserving enclosure.
9. Counterweight I: split yields two small BLOCKERS but changes source balance; single shear yields ROLLER and freezes source mechanism.
10. Stair J: upper fracture yields STEP but removes destination landing; lower fracture yields WEDGE and preserves upper traversal.

The exercise succeeds at proving source-cost coupling, but exposes a production problem: **meaningful fracture identity requires each source object to be authored as a small decision tree.** Shared affordance tags reduce implementation complexity, not level-design complexity. At a credible 5–8 hour target, repeated source silhouettes would make the correct fracture increasingly recognizable as a recipe; unique sources increase bespoke authoring burden.

The concept also has a semantic tension: deterministic settle zones are necessary for fair puzzle reasoning but reduce the tactile fantasy of sculpting debris. Restoring expressive physics reopens QA/retry risk.

**Run-2 verdict: ELIMINATE from top set / preserve reserve.** The concept can make a strong short demo, but its full-game content burden and `authored recipe disguised as destruction` risk are materially worse than the top three.
