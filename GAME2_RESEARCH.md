## 1. Research rules for Game #002

1. Start from current evidence, not from the Organism Cargo shortlist.
2. Extract durable player desires and interaction patterns rather than cloning current hits.
3. Prefer concepts whose hook can be understood in one sentence and demonstrated in a short clip.
4. Prefer reusable systemic depth over large bespoke content libraries.
5. Treat multiplayer as an optional multiplier with a high implementation/QA cost, not a default.
6. Treat any rapidly cloning microgenre as a warning unless the candidate changes the underlying interaction model.
7. A weird premise is useful only when the underlying action remains satisfying after the joke wears off.
8. A candidate must have a plausible primitive prototype that can prove or kill its core quickly.

## 2. Current market / discovery signals — 2026-08-18

### 2.1 Steam demo competition is rising faster than attention
GameDiscoverCo's June 2026 Next Fest analysis tracked 4,382 demos, about 66% more than June 2025, while follower gains for top-percentile games were lower year over year. Exact wishlist conversion remains estimated, but the direction is clear: merely entering a festival is not discovery.

Source:
- https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/who-won-june-2026s-steam-next-fest

GameSpot independently described June Next Fest as containing more than 4,000 demos.

Source:
- https://www.gamespot.com/articles/steam-next-fest-june-2026-25-of-the-best-demos-you-can-play-right-now/

**Factory implication:** Game #002 needs a hook that survives extreme thumbnail/trailer competition and should be demoable without needing an hour of explanation.

### 2.2 Structural oversupply is now a design constraint
A 2026 paper using a large Steam metadata snapshot describes extreme concentration of player attention and frames low-cost production as increasing marketplace oversupply. The exact causal claims are research hypotheses rather than platform facts, but the concentration problem is consistent with observed discovery pressure.

Source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25010

**Factory implication:** low production cost is not itself an advantage when competitors can also produce cheaply. Distinctiveness, clarity and retention must be designed in.

### 2.3 Weird specificity can still cut through
PC Gamer highlighted *Horse Magnifier: The Full Horse* in August 2026: an absurdly specific premise built around magnifying/distorting horses, but backed by a real puzzle structure and prior itch prototype history.

Source:
- https://www.pcgamer.com/games/puzzle/this-weeks-most-compelling-new-steam-game-is-horse-magnifier-which-is-about-magnifying-horses/

**Factory implication:** a memorable noun+verb combination can be a marketing asset. Do not sand concepts into generic “professional” descriptions.

### 2.4 Tiny-team viral multiplayer remains powerful but dangerous to imitate
Coverage of 2026 breakout *Meccha Chameleon* describes a two-person, low-price multiplayer concept whose camouflage/hide-and-seek interactions generated highly shareable clips. Treat the sales numbers as press-reported rather than audited.

Source:
- https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/the-viral-hit-of-2026-has-sold-15-million-copies-in-a-month-on-steam-costs-usd5-and-was-made-by-2-people

**Factory implication:** borrow legible, story-generating interactions. Do not assume networking is worth the scope unless the final concept fundamentally depends on people surprising each other.

### 2.5 Sorting/retail task sims are visibly saturated
PC Gamer counted 14 retail simulators released in one week in July 2026 and separately documented the rapid August 2026 expansion of shelf-sorting/tidying games after a breakout hit.

Sources:
- https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/steam-week-in-review-a-torrent-of-janky-retail-sims-continues-to-flood-steam-and-theres-no-end-in-sight/
- https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/steam-week-in-review-games-about-sorting-1000s-of-mundane-objects-onto-shelves-is-the-new-craze-sweeping-pc-gaming/

**Factory implication:** “satisfying work” remains valid, but plain retail, shelf sorting, ordinary packing and generic cleaning now have a high burden of proof.

### 2.6 Compact command/programming puzzles are viable but already occupied territory
The 2026 *Problem Space* demo controls a space probe through command sequences and optimization objectives. It demonstrates that terminal-like interaction remains legible to an audience while also warning against assuming “command console” is inherently novel.

Source:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4772820/Problem_Space_Demo/

**Factory implication:** if Game #002 uses programming/rule composition, it needs an embodied or unusual consequence model rather than a generic terminal skin.

### 2.7 One deeply explored mechanic plus modular production remains a durable small-team lesson
The postmortem for *The Turing Test* explicitly describes the design goal as one singular puzzle mechanic explored deeply and explains the cost value of modular environment production.

Source:
- https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/postmortem-building-i-the-turing-test-i-around-a-secret-mechanic

**Factory implication:** a single strong mechanic can carry substantial content if the state space expands through recombination instead of bespoke art.

### 2.8 Retro/comfort appeal is visible, but nostalgia cannot substitute for a new game
The Guardian's August 2026 games column describes players revisiting older “comfort” games and renewed interest in familiar design language.

Source:
- https://www.theguardian.com/games/2026/aug/12/why-were-going-back-to-the-games-of-our-youth

**Factory implication:** readable retro interaction grammar or visual constraints can lower production burden and improve identity, but Game #002 should not rely on imitation of a childhood genre alone.

## 3. Opportunity territories to explore

The first pass suggests six broad territories worth testing without locking any one of them:

1. **Rule manipulation with visible physical consequences** — change a small rule, then watch a system behave differently.
2. **Light / shadow / sound as gameplay state** — highly legible audiovisual feedback with potentially small asset libraries.
3. **Security / prediction / adversarial simulation** — plan a system, commit, observe an opposing process, then revise.
4. **Tiny programmable ecosystems** — compact entities whose rule combinations produce emergent behavior without a huge world.
5. **Transformative craft/process games** — the process itself is the puzzle; not menu recipe selection.
6. **Specific absurd job fantasies** — memorable roles with a real mechanical core rather than ordinary simulator chores.

Avoid privileging “compact workplace” simply because Game #001 succeeded as a design exercise in that territory.

## 4. Provisional scoring framework

Each concept is scored 1–5 on:
- **H Hook clarity (20%)**
- **D Systemic depth (20%)**
- **S Scope fitness (20%)**
- **M Market distinctiveness (15%)**
- **A Asset efficiency (10%)**
- **T Technical feasibility (10%)**
- **C Clipability (5%)**

Weighted score:
`0.20H + 0.20D + 0.20S + 0.15M + 0.10A + 0.10T + 0.05C`

Scores are filters only. Emotional pull, tutorial burden, repetition and prototype feel are not captured adequately here.

## 6. First-pass observations

### Strongest raw hooks
- **G2C01 Shadow Printer** — instantly demonstrable transformation from meaningless cutouts to functional shadow.
- **G2C19 False Map Department** — drawing a map changes the world; very strong sentence/GIF hook.
- **G2C20 Creature Costume Inspector** — unusual noun+job combination, low environment burden, clear discovery loop.
- **G2C05 Echo Sculptor** — sound-routing is intuitive and can create strong audiovisual feedback.
- **G2C09 Weather Locksmith** — compact process fantasy with unusual variables and visible cause/effect.

### Strongest scope/depth combinations
- **G2C07 Impossible Elevator** and **G2C12 Tiny Security Camera** look unusually finishable but need stronger differentiation from ordinary optimization/puzzle games.
- **G2C06 Fish Compiler** and **G2C13 Machine-Language Terrarium** can create depth from small rule vocabularies, but programming-game tutorial burden is a major risk.
- **G2C03 Reverse Heist Lab** can generate stories from adversarial simulation without requiring real multiplayer; thief behavior must remain deterministic/explainable enough to avoid feeling cheated.

### High-risk but high-ceiling concepts
- **G2C04 Rule Museum**, **G2C10 Paradox Moving Company**, **G2C14 Memory Locksmith**, and **G2C16 One-Push Salvage** may explode technically through state-space or physics. Keep only if the next pass can reduce their simulation grammar sharply.

### Saturation/derivative cautions
- Avoid making G2C15 an ordinary repair/cleaning/restoration sim.
- Avoid making G2C18 look like another recipe/crafting game.
- Avoid making G2C24 into a simple Papers, Please-style inspection reskin.
- Avoid presenting G2C03/G2C12 as generic tower-defense/security placement.
