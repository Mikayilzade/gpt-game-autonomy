# G8C06 Elevator Etiquette — KILL

**15s trailer:** doors open to three residents; player boards a wheelchair user and courier, refuses a large dog because one rider has a deterministic dog constraint; serves floor 7 first; doors reopen and the previously blocked resident can now board.

**Early case:** two riders, one cannot share with bulky cargo; choose stop order.

**Mature blueprints:** (1) nurse priority + stroller capacity + rider who refuses crowded cabins; (2) delivery cart temporarily blocks boarding and changes who can reach a timed appointment; (3) paired residents must overlap for one floor but a third passenger refuses the pair.

**Dominant-policy attack:** always serve nearest destination / first-come-first-served. Counterexample exists via compatibility and priority windows, but it requires multiplying passenger exception rules.

**Minute 5 -> hour 3:** from floor ordering to compatibility scheduling. The deeper game becomes a constraint table attached to NPCs rather than a richer physical primitive.

**Readability:** cabin/floors are excellent, but mature constraints need icons above people and request lists; risk of mobile-optimization silhouette is real.

**25–35 content model:** passenger archetype combinations. This is cheap technically but content quality depends on inventing many exceptions.

**Market boundary:** current elevator games already own efficient passenger movement; 2026 Sky Lobby makes the overlap newly acute. Social incompatibility is a boundary, but the pitch still looks like elevator traffic optimization before explanation.

**Demo:** one shift introducing incompatibility. Strong surface, insufficient mechanical distance.

**Kill criterion:** if removing character fiction/icons leaves a standard pickup/drop-off optimization problem, kill. **Triggered.**

**Portfolio:** clean from #001–#007 mechanically, but not strong enough externally.

**Verdict: KILL.** High Phase-1 score was misleading; current market collision plus exception-table depth destroys the advantage.

# G8C02 Locksmith's Margin — SURVIVE

**15s trailer:** blank enters vice -> player files one visible ridge -> inserts it into transparent training lock -> three pins set, fourth leaves an impression -> player chooses not to deepen that cut, flips to another lock, and the same imperfect key opens it.

**Early case:** one blank, one simple lock, impression marks show exactly which cut is too high; irreversible filing teaches undercut vs overcut.

**Mature blueprints:** (1) three locks share partial pin families; preserve one almost-key as diagnostic while cutting a second; (2) master-pin lock where a deeper cut opens A but permanently destroys compatibility with later B; (3) worn lock produces a bounded tolerance band, so player must choose a robust compromise rather than exact-copy every mark.

**Dominant-policy attack:** cut every blank specifically for current lock. Counterexample: limited blanks plus later lock-family compatibility makes specialization consume the only route to the set; however blank scarcity must not become the whole puzzle.

**Minute 5 -> hour 3:** read impression -> make exact correction becomes reason about compatibility classes, destructive information, tolerance intervals and when *not* to improve a key.

**Readability:** physical key profile, colored/animated pin stack, impression marks and a finite cut grid can expose all authoritative state without numeric spreadsheets.

**25–35 content model:** data-driven lock cylinders: 4–7 pin columns, discrete depth sets, bounded master pins, wear/tolerance flags, blank family, allowed test sequence. Content can recombine these rather than invent new tools.

**Market boundary:** lockpicking is common as a minigame, but this concept is not real-time lockpicking or burglary: the persistent destructively edited key is the strategic object, and partial compatibility across multiple authored locks is the system.

**Demo:** 5 locks / 3 blanks: exact first success, first irreversible mistake, first deliberate imperfect key, then a two-lock compatibility finale.

**Prototype kill criterion:** if players solve by monotonically filing toward each current impression, or cannot predict why a cut preserves/destroys compatibility, kill.

**Portfolio:** destructive physical editing is new; ensure it never becomes conserved-budget allocation (#005) or representation-causes-reality (#002/#007).

**Verdict: SURVIVE.** Strongest tactile object and clearest irreversible decision structure in the field.

# G8C04 Window Garden — SURVIVE

**15s trailer:** morning sun stripe crosses facade -> player angles one shutter and prunes a branch -> forecast ghost shows tomorrow's growth -> time advances -> vine bends onto trellis, shades a heat-sensitive plant and opens a pollinator route.

**Early case:** redirect a deterministic light band so one vine reaches a support instead of a window vent.

**Mature blueprints:** (1) grow temporary shade for seedling while preserving light for a later flowering window; (2) prune one branch so tomorrow's deterministic fork climbs a support and leaves vent clearance; (3) two species where one creates pollinator access but its mature canopy will later shade the second unless shutter timing changes.

**Dominant-policy attack:** maximize light/growth everywhere. Counterexample: excess growth blocks vents/supports and creates future shade; pruning can be progress.

**Minute 5 -> hour 3:** chase sunlight -> sculpt future geometry under delayed interactions and competing exposure needs.

**Readability:** survives only with discrete authored growth nodes and an exact next-cycle preview. No fuzzy plant physics may be authoritative.

**25–35 content model:** facade cells + sun lanes + shutters/trellises + plant graph templates. 6–8 plant behavior families can recombine across authored dioramas.

**Market boundary:** plant-growth puzzle precedents exist, and current gardening products are plentiful; boundary is architectural light/shade causality plus forecastable pruning, not collecting/growing crops or freeform decoration.

**Demo:** one facade over four days; light redirect, first prune, delayed shade reveal, final two-species dependency.

**Prototype kill criterion:** if a player cannot predict next-cycle geometry from the preview, or optimal play reduces to maximize light, kill.

**Portfolio:** delayed physical growth is distinct from remembered-form transformation (#007); no room-topology rewiring (#006).

**Verdict: SURVIVE.** High visual/trailer strength, but implementation risk remains material.

# G8C01 Maintenance Window — SURVIVE

**15s trailer:** conveyor hums -> machine panel turns amber with a visible 18-second safe service arc -> player stops pump A, physically pulls filter, flow reroutes through bypass -> temperature rises elsewhere -> filter snaps back, A restarts just before the downstream machine hits red.

**Early case:** service one filter; stopping it pauses one dependent line but nothing can cascade yet.

**Mature blueprints:** (1) two machines share a bypass whose heat ceiling means service order matters; (2) deliberately stop a healthy compressor to create the only safe window for a failing valve; (3) service task has two stages separated by a required live cycle, forcing temporary restoration while another component ages.

**Dominant-policy attack:** repair highest-wear component whenever available. Counterexample: dependency windows make a lower-wear intervention necessary first; emergency-only play causes an unavoidable cascade.

**Minute 5 -> hour 3:** follow amber prompt -> intentionally create and chain safe outages while anticipating local knock-on state.

**Readability:** danger is dashboard creep. Survives only if machine bodies, pipes, moving material, heat glow and local service panels carry state; a detached network graph cannot be primary play.

**25–35 content model:** compact authored cells built from ~6 machine roles, 4 service task families, bypass links and declared cycle phases. Situations arise from dependency/timing composition, not unique breakdown minigames.

**Market boundary:** repair simulators exist, but most emphasize diagnosis/part replacement/business fantasy. Boundary here is *maintenance timing as intervention into a live deterministic dependency system*, with physical service action and deliberate planned outage.

**Demo:** 15–20 minute shift: safe single service -> shared bypass -> planned outage -> compact cascade recovery.

**Prototype kill criterion:** if optimal play is pause-and-read meters, or hands-on service can be replaced by clicking a maintenance button without losing reasoning, kill.

**Portfolio:** avoid conserved-capacity network silhouette (#005); time/dependency state, not scarce flow allocation, must remain central.

**Verdict: SURVIVE.** Deepest occupational-system candidate if physical readability holds.

# G8C07 Firebreak Foreman — SURVIVE

**15s trailer:** wind arrow visibly shifts in 12 seconds -> player bulldozes one narrow strip and opens a warehouse fire door -> advancing flame reaches fuel shed -> deliberate sacrificed shed burns, but the cleared lane bends the front around the protected pump station.

**Early case:** remove one fence/brush strip to interrupt a simple deterministic spread path.

**Mature blueprints:** (1) sacrifice low-value structure to consume fuel before declared wind shift; (2) smoke closes worker access, so clear escape/access before treating the apparent hottest edge; (3) opening a structure changes ventilation/spread while soaking delays one material just long enough for a second break.

**Dominant-policy attack:** always attack nearest/hottest flame or clear maximum contiguous line. Counterexample: future wind/material order makes a remote break and intentional sacrifice superior.

**Minute 5 -> hour 3:** block current spread -> sculpt future front while preserving worker access and choosing losses.

**Readability:** continuous-looking fire is dangerous. Authority must be discrete cells/edges with explicit next-spread preview, material state and declared wind epochs; VFX may interpolate but never decide outcomes.

**25–35 content model:** authored compact sites from material cells, barriers, access edges, wind epochs, 4 intervention verbs and protected/sacrificial objectives.

**Market boundary:** wildfire games exist, including direct firefighting/co-op. Boundary is not action firefighting: it is deterministic pre-emptive landscape/structure intervention and chosen sacrifice on compact sites.

**Demo:** 4 micro-sites: break line, material order, access/smoke, final wind-shift sacrifice.

**Prototype kill criterion:** if players attribute outcomes to hidden/random fire physics, or VFX cannot communicate discrete authority, kill immediately.

**Portfolio:** no carried consequence (#003), no topology rewiring (#006); opening/clearing affects spread rules locally rather than rewriting adjacency as the core fantasy.

**Verdict: SURVIVE.** Excellent trailer causality, but highest simulation/readability risk among survivors.

# G8C10 Greenhouse Night Shift — SURVIVE

**15s trailer:** moths wake at dusk -> player opens mesh gate and vent -> moth follows a visible scent/light lane to flower A -> gate closes before pest enters -> humidity falls, flower B opens, bee route becomes valid and autonomous pollination completes.

**Early case:** open one gate at the right plant-state window so one pollinator reaches one flower.

**Mature blueprints:** (1) two pollinator species have different active windows; venting helps one flower but closes another; (2) isolate a pest corridor while preserving a pollination route using timing rather than spatial rewiring; (3) cross-pollination order matters because first visit changes the target's state and later agent preference.

**Dominant-policy attack:** keep all routes open / maximize ventilation. Counterexample: pests, humidity and flowering windows make openness actively harmful.

**Minute 5 -> hour 3:** open path for insect -> orchestrate autonomous agents whose valid actions depend on ecological state and order.

**Readability:** paths must be fixed physical greenhouse aisles/mesh doors; player never redraws connectivity. Agent intent needs visible next-target cues and finite state icons.

**25–35 content model:** fixed greenhouse modules, 3–4 pollinator/pest agent families, flower-state templates, vents/gates and schedule epochs. Risk: biological exception count can grow.

**Market boundary:** gardening/greenhouse games are common; boundary is night-shift orchestration of autonomous pollinators under deterministic ecological windows, not crop economy or idle growth.

**Demo:** dusk-to-dawn sequence with moth, vent/humidity conflict, pest isolation and two-agent finale.

**Prototype kill criterion:** if route opening feels like topology puzzle (#006), or agents need hidden AI/random preference to create depth, kill.

**Portfolio:** this is the closest contamination risk: fixed routes and time/state orchestration are mandatory; no graph editing.

**Verdict: SURVIVE, narrowly.** Strong systemic potential, but must beat Maintenance Window and Window Garden on exception burden in Run 2.
