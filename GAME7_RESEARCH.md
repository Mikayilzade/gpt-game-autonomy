## 1. Purpose
Generate a broad Game #007 field from a clean slate, using Games #001–#006 only as exclusion/portfolio history. This run intentionally searches beyond another spatial/topology puzzle.

## 2. Fresh 2026 market/context observations
Fresh Steam/current-release sampling on 2026-08-30 shows very heavy visible supply in:
- roguelike deckbuilders / card battlers;
- incremental / idle hybrids;
- horror and survival-horror;
- multiplayer / online co-op;
- survival/crafting;
- automation/building/resource management.

Steam's broad popular-tag surface still strongly supports single-player, simulation, strategy, puzzle, relaxing and stylized games, so a premium small-team systemic game remains commercially plausible, but Game #007 should not rely on `another deckbuilder`, `another anomaly horror`, `another cozy organizer`, or `another automation factory` as its differentiator.

Current-release evidence also reinforces two useful product lessons:
1. a very legible repeatable verb and short shareable clip can matter more than feature count;
2. low apparent production cost does not excuse unclear art/presentation or generic AI-looking output — visual authorship/readability remains part of trust.

This is market context, not a requirement to chase current viral genres.

## 4. Scoring lens
Each concept is screened on:
- **H** Hook legibility — can it be understood in one sentence / short clip?
- **D** Depth — can one compact ruleset recombine for hours?
- **S** Scope fit — plausible for a small implementation track?
- **V** Visual/gameplay readability — can cause/effect be shown clearly?
- **X** Differentiation — from market and existing factory portfolio?

Scores are directional 1–5, not false precision. Total /25.

## 5. Broad candidate field — 36 distinct concepts

### G7C01 — Decommission
**Hook:** Shut a live facility down permanently, one subsystem at a time, while every removal changes what can still be safely powered down next.
H5 D5 S5 V4 X5 = **24** — **SURVIVE**.
Why: strong irreversible planning fantasy, compact graph/state depth without detached graph-editor presentation.

### G7C02 — Last Known Shape
**Hook:** Objects keep only the last shape in which you directly observed them; leave, alter the viewing conditions, and that remembered shape becomes physically authoritative.
H5 D4 S4 V5 X5 = **23** — **SURVIVE**.
Risk: must not become vague quantum-magic trial-and-error.

### G7C03 — Command Lag
**Hook:** Every machine obeys your command after a visible delay, so you solve spaces by programming overlapping future consequences instead of reacting in real time.
H5 D5 S5 V4 X4 = **23** — **SURVIVE**.
Risk: avoid turning into generic programming UI or reflex timing.

### G7C04 — Offscreen Shift
**Hook:** Certain machines and workers act only while they are outside your camera view; framing the scene becomes a deterministic scheduling tool.
H5 D4 S4 V5 X5 = **23** — **SURVIVE**.
Risk: camera gimmick must support strategy rather than tedious peekaboo.

### G7C05 — Spare Parts Choir
**Hook:** Repair one machine by physically transplanting a functional module from another machine, knowingly breaking or changing the donor.
H5 D5 S4 V5 X4 = **23** — **SURVIVE**.
Difference from Borrowed Collision: persistent structural module exchange, not harvesting portable transient force; still requires tournament scrutiny for portfolio proximity.

### G7C06 — Museum of Almosts
**Hook:** Reconstruct an object from several mutually incompatible witness descriptions; the winning artifact is the one configuration that makes all statements simultaneously true under perspective/context rules.
H4 D4 S5 V4 X5 = **22** — **SURVIVE**.
Risk: content authoring burden and language/localization dependence.

### G7C07 — Queue Collapse
**Hook:** You may reorder only one customer/job in a live service queue at a time; each completion changes the priority rules for everyone still waiting.
H4 D5 S5 V4 X4 = **22** — **SURVIVE**.
Potential framing: airport, repair bay, clinic-like fictional service without real medical claims, magical office, robotic depot.

### G7C08 — Chain of Custody
**Hook:** Important objects gain or lose permissions depending on exactly who handled them and in what order; solve logistics by preserving a valid custody history.
H4 D5 S5 V4 X5 = **23** — **SURVIVE**.
Risk: needs highly legible physical provenance, not spreadsheet bureaucracy.

### G7C09 — Understudy Controls
**Hook:** Reassign a physical control from one machine to another; the new machine gains that control function and the original machine immediately loses it.
H5 D5 S4 V5 X4 = **23** — **SURVIVE**.
Risk: property-transfer resemblance to Game #003; tournament must prove the core experience is control topology, not `steal effect and spend elsewhere`.

### G7C10 — Recurrence Department
**Hook:** Every room repeats the action you performed there exactly one turn later, so moving through the building creates a deterministic wave of delayed echoes.
H5 D5 S4 V4 X5 = **23** — **SURVIVE**.
Risk: requires excellent timeline visualization.

### G7C11 — Margin of Error
Tune machines whose sensors have explicit tolerance windows; improving accuracy in one band worsens another. **20 — HOLD.** Good systems puzzle, weak fantasy.

### G7C12 — One Good Fuse
A facility has one reusable protection fuse; decide which circuit is allowed to fail safely each step. **19 — REJECT.** Too close to conserved-budget reasoning from Tension Budget.

### G7C13 — Negative Space Foundry
Remove a void/cutout from one solid and physically insert that exact absence into another object. **21 — HOLD.** Great GIF, but spatial manipulation risks too much adjacency/shape portfolio concentration.

### G7C14 — Pallet Ghost
Warehouse puzzle where you never move crates directly; you move the single guaranteed empty footprint and crates deterministically collapse into it. **20 — HOLD.** Elegant but Sokoban-adjacent and visually modest.

### G7C15 — Borrowed Weather
Move a local weather condition between tiny diorama zones. **17 — REJECT.** Reads as Game #003 property-transfer reskin.

### G7C16 — Thermal Memory
Machines remember their last stable temperature and behave based on it after power loss. **19 — HOLD.** Potential depth, poor immediate hook.

### G7C17 — Exit Interview
Interview departing synthetic workers whose answers alter which procedural rule they are classified under. **17 — REJECT.** Content/dialogue burden dominates mechanics.

### G7C18 — Dead Letter Office
Sort impossible mail using address, physical evidence and recipient behavior. **19 — HOLD.** Charming but dangerously content-heavy and Papers-Please-adjacent.

### G7C19 — Priority Inversion
Physical workers obey visible operating-system-like priority inheritance rules. **18 — REJECT.** Mechanically deep but abstract/technical framing burdens onboarding.

### G7C20 — Shelf Life
Inventory objects age only when removed from storage, so traversal and use consume their lifetime. **18 — REJECT.** Too easily becomes a resource timer.

### G7C21 — Counterfeit Gravity
Only one object at a time is legally `heavy`; transfer that status to navigate a facility. **18 — REJECT.** Property-token transfer and generic physics-puzzle risk.

### G7C22 — Light Lease
A limited set of surfaces may own illumination; moving a light contract changes which shadows are solid. **19 — HOLD.** Strong visual, but shadow-platformer space is crowded and likely implementation/art sensitive.

### G7C23 — The Wrong Manual
Operate machines using several manuals written for neighboring model variants; infer the actual rule set through safe tests. **20 — HOLD.** Strong deduction, but risks False Map Department representation-vs-reality overlap.

### G7C24 — Calibration Day
Every device exposes only whether a reading is above/below its current calibration threshold; move thresholds to infer hidden state. **19 — HOLD.** Clean logic, weak emotional fantasy.

### G7C25 — Proxy Damage
Route deterministic machine faults through safety relays so different components take the consequence. **18 — REJECT.** Too close to budget/routing abstractions and could become spreadsheet logic.

### G7C26 — Split Shift
Two workers share one action schedule: whenever one acts, the other inherits the unused half of that action next turn. **18 — REJECT.** Hard to explain and likely puzzle-state bookkeeping heavy.

### G7C27 — One-Sided Contract
Granting a permission to one actor automatically revokes its paired permission from another. **20 — HOLD.** Mechanically promising, theming presently too abstract.

### G7C28 — Assembly by Absence
Build devices only by removing pre-existing parts from standardized blocks; every useful component creates a discarded negative remainder that must still fit disposal constraints. **21 — HOLD.** Interesting production puzzle, moderate UI burden.

### G7C29 — Second Hand
Mechanical clocks are machines; repositioning one hand changes what connected mechanisms consider `before` and `after`. **18 — REJECT.** Likely opaque temporal metaphysics.

### G7C30 — Clean Break
You can permanently sever one dependency at a time in a living machine; severed pieces continue autonomously under their last instruction. **21 — HOLD.** Strong, but overlaps Decommission and topology/disconnection themes; Decommission is cleaner.

### G7C31 — Floor Manager
A building's elevators obey service classes instead of floor buttons; change class memberships to make riders and cargo co-resolve routes. **18 — REJECT.** Too much routing/logistics familiarity.

### G7C32 — Unfinished Business
When a worker abandons a task, the partial progress/state transfers to the next worker assigned. **17 — REJECT.** Management sim framing hides the hook and risks grind.

### G7C33 — Blackout Etiquette
Devices reveal different legal actions depending on whether neighboring devices are powered. **18 — REJECT.** Generic circuit puzzle without a sufficiently ownable verb.

### G7C34 — Weight of Words
Spoken labels change how physical objects are categorized by machinery. **19 — HOLD.** Strong semantic puzzle possibility; localization and content burden high.

### G7C35 — Single Source of Truth
Several control panels show conflicting state, but only the most recently physically serviced panel becomes authoritative for the machinery it describes. **19 — REJECT.** Too near False Map Department's representation/authority territory.

### G7C36 — Spare Key Ecology
Keys are living tools that adapt permanently to the last lock they open, changing which future locks they fit. **20 — HOLD.** Cute systemic hook, but can collapse into colored-key inventory optimization.

## 7. Mandatory Phase-2 attack questions
Every survivor must be tested against all of these, not just scored by vibes:

### Portfolio collision
- Does G7C05 or G7C09 secretly reproduce Borrowed Collision's `take something from A, spend it at B` satisfaction?
- Does G7C01 or G7C08 drift into cargo/routing work already represented by Organism Cargo?
- Does any concept become another topology/disconnection puzzle after implementation detail is added?

### Minute-to-minute play
- What exactly does the player do every 10–30 seconds?
- Is the repeated verb satisfying after discovery, or only clever once?

### Hour-4 depth
- Can the concept generate qualitatively different reasoning families without adding many bespoke mechanics?
- What is the likely dominant strategy?

### Demo / store legibility
- Can a 10–20 second clip show cause, choice and consequence without narration?
- Can a screenshot communicate state without spreadsheet UI?

### Scope / technical risk
- Is deterministic authority feasible?
- Does the concept require expensive AI, physics, animation, dialogue, bespoke art or hundreds of authored exceptions?

### Accessibility / input
- Can core play work without reflex timing, precision pointer movement, color-only state or audio-only clues?
