## C01 — NIGHT SHIFT SWITCHBOARD — PASS — 42/50
**Microcase.** Calls A (payroll correction), B (security badge), C (delivery) wait. Operators Mira handles Payroll but may transfer only callers whose identity has been confirmed; Leon handles Security and confirms identity whenever discussing a badge; Noor handles Reception and can connect a confirmed caller to Payroll. A initially cannot reach Mira. Player routes B→Leon, which causes Leon to publish the day's verified-employee token to Noor; A→Noor now becomes confirmed and Noor→Mira resolves A. C can go directly to Noor. Resolution is fully explained by operator cards and the visible knowledge/permission changes.

**Case #5 escalation.** Two calls refer to the same project under different public aliases. Handling either teaches one operator the alias equivalence, but transferring that knowledge consumes their one permitted internal consultation. Player must decide which call creates knowledge, which operator becomes the bridge, and preserve the consultation for a later authorization chain. This adds shared-fact provenance and scarce communication, not merely more calls.

**Scores:** 5/5/5/5/5/4/4/4/5/5 = **47?** Arithmetic correction under strict scoring: production 3, input 4, commercial 4 => **45/50**. Final score **45**.
**Kill argument:** truthful state could become flags and transfer matrices; animations may disguise a spreadsheet rather than remove it.
**Defense:** state changes occur through concrete conversations, and call-routing provides an immediate physical metaphor for preparing another call.
**Kill probes:** no enumeration, hidden arbitrary rule, prior-state collision or clone; UI-density risk is controllable if operator cards expose only currently relevant facts. **PASS.**

## C02 — SECOND-HAND MUSEUM — KILL — 35/50

Visitor Ada trusts maker evidence; Bo distrusts signed claims after seeing a replica warning. Player orders warning→signed cup→factory cup so Bo reads the signature skeptically while Ada still authenticates the factory cup. Deterministic interpretation ledger explains each conclusion.

**Case #5 escalation.** A label can redefine a category only for rooms visited afterward; two visitor routes cross, requiring a shared exhibit to seed different conclusions based on prior context.
**Scores:** 4/3/4/3/4/3/4/3/5/2 = **35**.
**Kill argument:** semantic interpretation needs many authored definitions and exceptions; fairness rapidly depends on language nuance.
**Defense:** order-dependent meaning is distinctive and visually packageable as exhibitions.
**Kill probes:** **OPAQUE-RULE / production inversion triggered.** Making enough semantics explicit to be fair turns the screen into explanation text; hiding them makes outcomes arbitrary. **KILL.**

## C03 — PLATFORM 4½ — KILL — 34/50
**Microcase.** Two cohorts must transfer from train A to B/C. Commuters prefer familiar stairs unless crowd >3; tourists follow visible coffee signs before choosing a platform. Player can move one kiosk sign and retime B by one minute. Moving the sign draws tourists away, keeping the familiar stair below threshold so commuters reach B; tourists then reach C. Choice traces are deterministic.

**Case #5 escalation.** Boarding one service changes platform crowding before another cohort chooses, creating endogenous preference changes and a deliberate sacrificial connection.
**Scores:** 4/4/4/4/4/4/2/3/3/2 = **34**.
**Kill argument:** truthful causality requires timetable, cohort, crowd and path UI plus animated station simulation; it reads as a management game but lacks management breadth.
**Defense:** manipulating human habits instead of rails is a strong twist.
**Kill probes:** **PRODUCTION INVERSION triggered**; communicating cohort path decisions costs disproportionate simulation/UI effort. **KILL.**

## C04 — THE QUIET AUCTION — PASS — 40/50
**Microcase.** Player has $12 and needs Lot C. Rival Iris bids 6 on any botanical item, but 9 if she already owns its matching frame; Pavel bids 5 on clocks and +2 when he lost the previous lot. Lots A botanical frame, B clock, C botanical print resolve in order. Player can spend $1 to beat Iris on A, causing Iris not to gain frame; intentionally lets Pavel win B; then C clears below Iris's boosted threshold and player can win within budget. All rival rules are given as hypotheses supported by history; resolution reveals exact bids afterward.

**Case #5 escalation.** Bundle bids and rival substitute preferences create opportunity-cost inference: winning one lot can suppress a rival's bid elsewhere while the player's own tie-break position depends on conserved cash.
**Scores:** 4/5/5/4/5/5/5/4/4/4 = **45**; uncertainty penalty for inference onboarding gives final comparative **43/50**.
**Kill argument:** arithmetic and rival-policy inference can feel like finance homework or guessing.
**Defense:** deliberate losing/price manipulation creates expressive plans; deterministic post-round reveal makes causality inspectable.
**Kill probes:** no mandatory trigger if policy hypothesis space is always bounded and evidence-complete before commitment. **PASS.**

**Defense:** active experiment design is deeper than passive deduction and supports systemic case generation.
**Kill probes:** spreadsheet risk exists but is not mandatory if prototypes are manipulable objects with tiny behavior traces. **PASS.**

## C07 — COMMON KNOWLEDGE — PASS — 38/50

Player has one public notice and one acknowledgement. Public notice heard by both establishes that both heard it; Ana's public acknowledgement makes Bo know Ana knows, while public observability closes the reciprocal layer. Ledger visualizes K(A,D), K(B,D), K(A,K(B,D)), etc., with natural-language summaries.

**Case #5 escalation.** One participant cannot hear the announcement but can see acknowledgements; player constructs common knowledge through a public signal whose receipt itself is observable, introducing channel observability rather than deeper arbitrary nesting.
**Scores:** 3/4/5/5/4/5/4/2/5/2 = **39**.
**Kill argument:** epistemic nesting is intrinsically academic; interface may need symbolic ledgers.

**Kill probes:** spreadsheet/opacity risk does not yet trigger if nesting is capped at two and represented spatially. **PASS, but Round-B teachability gate is severe.**

## C11 — HOUSE RULES — PASS — 41/50

## C13 — MINUTE HAND — PASS — 39/50

**Case #5 escalation.** Two clocks share a power circuit: one fault's stall resets when the other chimes. Player must choose observation intervals that distinguish independent drift from coupled reset, adding causal intervention rather than another arithmetic fault.

**Kill probes:** one-trick collapse not yet triggered because coupling changes inference topology; Round B must prove three later families beyond fault-list growth. **PASS.**
