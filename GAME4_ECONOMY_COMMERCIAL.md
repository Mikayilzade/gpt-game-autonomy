# GAME #004 — ECONOMY / RETENTION / COMMERCIAL MODEL

Last updated: 2026-08-21
Factory run: **11**
Phase: **7 — Economy / Retention / Commercial Model**
Working name: **HUSHLINE** (provisional; likely replacement candidate, not trademark-cleared)
Phase status: **COMPLETE / LOCKED FOR DOWNSTREAM DESIGN**
Production implementation: **NOT STARTED**

This phase defines how the locked 34-main-encounter + 8-remix premium game is packaged, priced, demonstrated and replayed. It does **not** add gameplay systems, currencies, grind, live-service retention or narrative obligations.

---

## 1. Commercial thesis

Game #004 is a **compact premium single-player systemic puzzle/infiltration game**, sold on one immediately legible physical idea:

> Move one visible soundproof barrier through a facility so the right listener hears an action and the wrong listener does not.

Commercial success depends less on raw hour count than on:
- the hook being visible in a 5–10 second clip;
- a demo that proves selective audibility rather than generic stealth;
- deterministic, understandable failures and fast retries;
- enough authored systemic content to make 5–8 hours feel dense rather than stretched;
- strong replay/mastery value without grind;
- a title/capsule that does not misclassify the game as horror, microphone stealth or a sound-only accessibility product.

The product should not compete on content-volume claims against large stealth games. It should compete on **clarity, novelty, density and finish**.

## 2. Fresh 2026 market / platform evidence

Market and Steam platform checks performed 2026-08-21.

### 2.1 Comparable premium pricing signals
Current Steam prices checked:
- **Patrick's Parabox** — $19.99; premium systemic puzzle game with a highly specific mechanic and demo.
- **The Roottrees are Dead** — $19.99; focused premium deduction/puzzle product.
- **Tactical Breach Wizards** — $19.99; much larger tactics/narrative product, useful as an upper-value comparison rather than direct scope peer.
- **Blue Prince** — $29.99; substantially broader/deeper premium puzzle/adventure product and therefore above the intended Game #004 tier.

These examples show that unusual single-player puzzle/systemic games can sustain premium pricing; the correct comparison is not `hours per dollar` alone. However Game #004's planned 5–8 hour first clear and modest narrative/asset scope argue against a $24.99–$29.99 launch target unless implementation evidence materially expands perceived value.

### 2.2 Steam pricing guidance
Steam's current pricing documentation advises considering completion length, replayability, comparable games and future promotion strategy rather than simply undercutting competitors. Steam supports explicit USD tiers including $14.99, $15.99, $16.99, $17.99, $18.99 and $19.99 plus regional pricing tools.

### 2.3 Demo guidance
Steam's current demo documentation explicitly positions demos as useful when players need to *touch the game to understand it*, which strongly matches this product. Steam demos are separate App IDs. Steam can link demo users back to the full game, supports shared depots, and can share Cloud storage with the full app to reduce upgrade friction. Valve recommends disabling achievements in the demo and granting them later in the full game if appropriate.

### 2.4 Discounting guidance
Steam currently suggests launch discounts around **10–15%** when used. Launch discounts run **7–14 days** and must be configured before release. After release, discount cooldown rules apply; a launch discount is the main exception allowing a discount at release. Discounts of 20%+ can trigger wishlist emails, which is useful later but should not drive launch pricing.

## 3. Launch price model

### 3.1 Realistic USD base-price range
**$14.99–$19.99** is the locked commercial test range for the current scope.

### 3.2 Current preferred test anchor
**$17.99 USD** is the provisional pricing anchor for planning, not a frozen final price.

Why $17.99 currently fits:
- 34 main encounters + 8 optional remixes is materially more than a tiny experimental puzzle game;
- 5–8 hour first clear supports premium positioning if encounter density/finish are high;
- complete controller support, no-audio parity, accessibility, deterministic replay and polished visual explanation increase product value beyond bare puzzle count;
- $17.99 sits below the common $19.99 premium-puzzle tier while avoiding a `small $9.99 curiosity` signal;
- it leaves room for a normal 10% launch discount without teaching players that the product is bargain-priced.

### 3.3 Price gates before freeze
Final price must be chosen from real external evidence, preferably a small blind pricing test after a polished vertical slice/store page exists.

Ask test participants after seeing the store capsule, 30–60 seconds of normal gameplay and the demo/value description:
- what price did you expect before seeing a number?
- at $14.99 / $17.99 / $19.99, does the game feel underpriced, fair, or expensive?
- does `34 main encounters + 8 remixes / 5–8h first clear` feel like enough value?

**Commercial gate:** if a meaningful majority that likes the concept still perceives $17.99 as too high after the demo, prefer $14.99–$16.99 rather than padding scope. If players commonly expect $19.99 and content polish supports it, $19.99 remains valid.

Do not increase gameplay scope merely to justify a higher sticker price.

## 4. Value proposition of the 1.0 package

Locked 1.0 content value:
- **34 main encounters**;
- **8 optional remix/mastery encounters**;
- nominal **5–8 hour first clear**;
- fast replay times after understanding;
- 12 reusable reasoning families, with selective audibility recurring through different route/listener/source configurations;
- complete campaign with no paid chapter required for resolution;
- optional mastery layer without power grind.

Store-facing communication should emphasize quality/density rather than literally leading with `34 levels`.

Preferred value language:
- compact handcrafted infiltration puzzles;
- every facility is built around readable acoustic cause-and-effect;
- later encounters combine the same small rules into multi-step selective-hearing problems;
- optional remixes/mastery targets reward cleaner, faster and more deliberate solutions.

Avoid claims such as `hundreds of hours`, `endless replayability`, `immersive sim`, or `full stealth sandbox`.

## 5. Demo commercial package

### 5.1 Demo objective
The demo exists to answer one commercial question:

> Does the player understand and enjoy physically controlling **who hears what** enough to wishlist/buy the full game?

It is not a generic tutorial sampler and must not end before the product's differentiator appears.

### 5.2 Structure
Target: **4 curated encounters / ~18–24 minutes first play**, with a 20-minute nominal target.

**D01 — Physical Screen** (~3–4 min)
- one listener;
- first direct barrier move;
- simple visible route;
- teaches world-first propagation and instant preview.

**D02 — The Other Way Around** (~4–5 min)
- alternate acoustic route;
- nearest-door blocking fails;
- second meaningful barrier move.

**D03 — Make Them Move** (~5–6 min)
- deliberate distraction;
- player intentionally causes investigation and actively crosses the created opening;
- demonstrates `heard` is not `failed`.

**D04 — Selective Audibility** (~6–8 min)
- two listeners;
- one action should reach A but not B;
- barrier must be repositioned during the encounter rather than parked once;
- ends immediately after a clean selective-hearing + active traversal payoff.

The demo should stop **after the thesis is proven**, not after introducing every system.

### 5.3 What stays out of the demo
Do not expose the full late-game vocabulary. Normally exclude:
- moving environmental source family;
- three-listener exceptional content;
- the densest tied-route layouts;
- extraction inversion at mature complexity;
- final multi-event retarget combinations;
- most optional mastery remixes.

One door mutation may be included only if required for pacing and clarity; the demo does not need to checklist every mechanic.

### 5.4 End state / call to action
After D04:
- show a short earned recap of the final acoustic split in world space;
- present `Wishlist / Full Game` CTA via Steam overlay/store link;
- communicate the full-game promise concisely: **34 handcrafted main encounters + optional mastery remixes; increasingly complex selective-audibility facilities**;
- show 2–3 short spoiler-safe clips from later rule combinations, not late solutions;
- allow immediate replay of D01–D04 or return to menu.

No artificial cliffhanger, timer or nag loop.

### 5.5 Demo save transfer policy
**Preferred:** share settings/accessibility configuration and demo-completion metadata with the full game when technically practical.

If D01–D04 are content-identical to early full-game encounters at release, the full game may offer:
- `Continue after demo`, and
- `Replay from beginning`.

If the demo is a curated/remixed branch rather than identical campaign content, do **not** fake campaign completion. Transfer settings and a `demo_completed` flag only; begin the full campaign normally with optional accelerated onboarding where safe.

Use Steam Cloud sharing after full-game release when practical. Disable Steam achievements in the demo; full-game achievements may be granted later from transferred valid completion state where appropriate.

## 6. Progression and retention — no economy required

There is **no currency economy** in 1.0.

No coins, XP, crafting materials, upgrade points, daily rewards, energy, battle pass or login streaks are needed.

Retention comes from four intrinsic layers:

1. **Campaign understanding** — new encounter combinations reveal deeper consequences of already-known rules.
2. **Mastery predicates** — solve more cleanly, quickly or with fewer barrier edits.
3. **Optional remixes** — recombine locked content grammar at higher density.
4. **Readable improvement** — players become faster at seeing useful audibility routes and creating investigation windows.

The game should make `I understand this building better now` the progression feeling.

### 6.1 Unlock structure
Default recommendation:
- campaign encounters unlock mostly linearly within small bands;
- after completing a band's gate encounter, allow 2–3 next encounters where sequencing is not pedagogically strict;
- optional remixes unlock from related main-encounter completion/mastery, never from currency spending;
- no star threshold blocks the main ending.

This gives player autonomy without letting late systemic encounters appear before required acoustic language is learned.

### 6.2 Difficulty modes
Do not create Easy/Normal/Hard by changing hidden hearing math.

Use an **Assist configuration** that preserves the same acoustic truth:
- slower whole-simulation speed;
- longer direct-detection forgiveness where applicable;
- stronger preview persistence;
- larger interaction/snap zones;
- additional checkpointing;
- high contrast / explicit numeric acoustic bands if desired.

Main campaign completion remains valid with assists. Achievements must not shame or exclude assist use except for explicitly labeled optional mastery feats that depend on a fixed timing constraint.

## 7. Mastery and replay incentives

The eight optional remix/mastery encounters are the main authored replay layer.

Reusable mastery targets already locked mechanically/content-wise:
- no restart;
- time target;
- maximum barrier edits;
- required selective hearing outcome;
- maximum passive-wait percentage;
- fast-move usage target.

Commercial/replay rule:
- never require all mastery targets for main completion;
- avoid a universal 3-star-per-level grind;
- expose 1–2 meaningful mastery ideas per encounter only when they reveal a different route/skill;
- do not reward mastery with stronger barriers, quieter footsteps or easier thresholds.

Preferred reward types:
- profile/campaign completion marks;
- optional challenge/remix unlocks;
- cosmetic UI/case-file presentation accents only if extremely cheap and non-distracting;
- achievement progress.

No loot box, randomized cosmetic economy or paid cosmetic store.

## 8. Achievement plan

Target **18–24 Steam achievements** for 1.0, enough to recognize campaign and systemic mastery without trivial notification spam.

Suggested families:
- 4–5 campaign-band milestones;
- first deliberate successful lure;
- first true selective-audibility split;
- route where a strength-4 action leaks through a barrier but still yields the intended listener outcome;
- solve a tied-route encounter without a failed attempt;
- finish an encounter with zero passive waiting above the validator threshold;
- low-barrier-edit challenge;
- FAST_MOVE mastery challenge;
- complete all 8 remixes;
- full campaign completion;
- optional expert completion/mastery aggregate.

Avoid achievements for repeated grind counts such as `move barrier 1,000 times` or `wait 10 hours`.

Demo achievements: **disabled**, following current Steam guidance.

## 10. Launch posture

### 10.1 Preferred launch discount
If using a launch discount: **10% for 7–14 days** is the preferred baseline.

At a provisional $17.99 base price, that communicates a normal introductory offer without implying weak value. A 15% launch discount is acceptable if market testing or event timing supports it.

Do not default to 20%+ at launch merely to trigger wishlist notifications. Preserve price integrity first.

### 10.2 Post-launch discounts
Guiding posture:
- avoid rapid deep discounts immediately after release;
- use Steam seasonal/themed opportunities once eligible;
- first meaningful post-launch sale should normally be modest unless launch performance or market conditions justify otherwise;
- deeper discounts can grow gradually with age rather than training launch buyers to wait.

Exact timing remains release-strategy work because Steam cooldown/event calendars are time-sensitive.

### 10.3 Early Access
**Not recommended as baseline.**

The game is authored, deterministic and demo-falsifiable; Early Access would create pressure to add content/features publicly while the hook can instead be validated through prototype, playtests and a free demo. Use Early Access only if implementation later uncovers a genuine need for prolonged public systems validation, not as a marketing substitute.

## 11. DLC / expansion boundary

1.0 must be a complete premium game.

Allowed later expansion shape only after 1.0 proves demand:
- a substantial new authored encounter pack using the same acoustic grammar;
- possibly one clearly separated new facility/theme;
- new reasoning combinations primarily from existing mechanics.

A paid expansion must provide enough authored content to feel like a real package, not sell individual puzzles one by one.

Do **not** reserve a core mechanic, ending, barrier function, accessibility option or campaign chapter for paid DLC.

Do not design speculative DLC mechanics during 1.0 production. New systems require a future design amendment and cannot contaminate current scope.

Free post-launch patches may reasonably add:
- accessibility improvements;
- balance/readability fixes;
- a small challenge/remix if cheap;
- quality-of-life improvements.

## 12. Monetization exclusions / ethical boundaries

Game #004 1.0 explicitly forbids:
- paid power or acoustic upgrades;
- consumable retries/hints;
- premium barriers;
- loot boxes / gambling / randomized paid rewards;
- energy/lives systems;
- daily-login rewards;
- battle passes;
- ad monetization;
- real-money currencies;
- artificial wait timers;
- paid progression skips;
- manipulative streak/FOMO systems;
- deliberately frustrating checkpoints sold back as convenience.

The commercial model is **buy the complete game once**.

## 13. Title and capsule risk

`HUSHLINE` remains provisional and should now be treated as **high-risk for final use**, not merely uncleared.

A fresh non-exhaustive web search on 2026-08-21 found:
- **Hushline: Chat Story**, an active App Store interactive-story app;
- `hushline.net`, an active audio-generation service;
- a small 2026 browser experiment titled **Hush line**.

This does not constitute trademark/legal clearance, but it is enough to prevent the factory from emotionally locking the name.

**Commercial gate:** before Phase 11/final migration, either:
1. complete a real title-clearance process appropriate to target territories/classes; or
2. replace `HUSHLINE` with a stronger low-conflict title and then clear that.

Do not build narrative lore around the name until this is resolved.

### Capsule communication gate
A successful capsule/trailer must not look like generic dark stealth.

At minimum, store media should communicate one of these without explanation:
- a large physical acoustic barrier visibly sealing one passage while sound routes around another;
- two listeners receiving visibly different hearing outcomes from one action;
- barrier reposition → route flip → deliberate listener investigation → player crossing.

If viewers describe the game only as `stealth`, `sound horror`, `microphone game` or `stay quiet`, the commercial identity is not communicating.

## 14. Commercial risk gates

These remain empirical and must be measured before release/final commercial freeze.

### C7-01 — Price sensitivity
After polished demo/store media, the preferred $17.99 anchor must not materially suppress stated purchase intent versus $14.99–$16.99 among target players. Final base price remains adjustable inside $14.99–$19.99.

### C7-02 — Perceived value
Target players who enjoy the demo should generally regard 34 main encounters + 8 remixes / 5–8 hour first clear as a complete premium package, not a prologue.

### C7-03 — Demo conversion comprehension
After D04, players must be able to explain: **sometimes one listener should hear you while another should not**.

### C7-04 — Demo appetite
A meaningful majority of qualified demo completers should report wanting more combinations rather than feeling they already saw the whole system.

### C7-05 — Capsule legibility
Unprimed viewers should identify barrier movement + different hearing outcomes, not generic stealth/horror, from the first store media set.

### C7-06 — Title clearance
`HUSHLINE` or its replacement must pass actual naming/trademark/store-search review before final title lock.

### C7-07 — Audio-game confusion
Store media/text must not lead players to believe headphones, microphone input or good hearing are mechanically required. No-audio parity should be communicated positively without making the product look like a settings demonstration.

### C7-08 — Content-value honesty
Do not advertise theoretical replay hours based on retries/mastery. First-clear and handcrafted encounter scope should remain honestly described.

### C7-09 — Discount integrity
Do not use a deep launch discount to compensate for weak capsule/demo conversion. Fix the product communication first.

### C7-10 — Retention integrity
If analytics later show drop-off, do not add grind, streaks, currencies or artificial locks. Fix pacing, readability, encounter ordering or content quality.
