# GAME #016 — PHASE 1 OPPORTUNITY DISCOVERY

Date: 2026-09-03
Status: Phase 1 complete; no winner selected.

## 1. Clean-slate boundary
Game #016 inherits no canon from #001–#015. Exclude their defining identities: living-cargo ecology cascades; bureaucracy/reality editing; property transfer; audio-hidden geometry; conserved tension networks; topology rewiring; observation/persistent transformed forms; destructive key-vector editing; folding/imposition; moving-luggage label permutation; cyclic-program deletion; topology-of-empty-space placement; temporal tamper witnesses; multi-light shadow classification; workpieces-as-masks / persistent spray exposure histories.

Additional default kills for #016: generic survivor/roguelite/deckbuilder reskins; online co-op dependence; precision platforming/twitch timing; freeform physics where failure is hard to explain; enormous narrative/world/content burden; automation/factory clone without a sharply legible new verb; finite-set brute force disguised as deduction.

## 2. September-2026 market evidence and implications
Fresh research shows an exceptionally crowded Steam discovery environment. June Next Fest coverage reported thousands of demos (one contemporary report counted 8,682) and strong competition for attention. Curated coverage repeatedly surfaced instantly communicable interaction hooks, unusual physical fantasies, cozy tactile assembly, observation/exploration, co-op spectacle, horror and Souls/extraction hybrids. Implication: #016 needs a clip-readable verb/state change rather than genre-description marketing.

Recent September coverage includes deliberately absurd, visually legible premises such as Worming from Home and head-arrangement puzzling in MrFreezy. June coverage highlighted Screenbound's 2D/3D relationship, TOEM 2's photography/observation, Woodo's tactile diorama assembly, and mechanically explicit simulation fantasies. Implication: small-team games can compete through a distinctive manipulable object/system rather than content volume.

Automation/management retains commercial appetite, but it is also attractive to developers and increasingly populated; use only if the new interaction is materially different. Dark-fantasy co-op, Soulslike/extraction, survivor-like and generic roguelite/deckbuilder intersections remain obvious saturation hazards for this factory.

Pricing analyses available in 2026 continue to place substantial indie puzzle/strategy products around the low-premium $9.99+ band while warning that median indie outcomes are poor. Treat those figures as directional rather than authoritative. Design consequence: bounded production ratio, strong demo proof and a premium-sized value proposition matter more than chasing a fashionable genre.

## 4. Retained field — 12 candidates

### A. Margin of Error
**10-second hook:** Build a tiny mechanism using rulers that are visibly wrong in different ways.
**Core:** measurements are intervals/tolerances, not exact values; choose references, cuts and mating strategy so assemblies work for every allowed error.
**Depth source:** propagation/cancellation of uncertainty and choosing common reference surfaces.
**Demo:** three increasingly deceptive bench builds ending in a satisfying fit-test reveal.
**Failure hypothesis:** may feel like engineering homework unless manipulation and reveal are extremely tactile.

### B. Second-Hand Forecast
**Hook:** A desk of mechanical weather instruments lies to itself; physically isolate interactions and make tomorrow's forecast.
**Core:** barometer, hygrometer, thermometer-like fictional devices have visible cross-couplings; reposition baffles/vents/linkages to distinguish environment from instrument interference.
**Depth:** causal diagnosis from coupled instruments, then configuration under constraints.
**Demo:** one storm forecast with 4–5 devices.
**Failure hypothesis:** forecasting fiction may obscure exact rules or demand text.

### C. The Spare Room
**Hook:** Furnish one room that must still work after the walls slide into a second disclosed shape.
**Core:** discrete furniture footprints + access/use zones; one arrangement must satisfy two floorplans.
**Depth:** objects act as constraints across paired spatial states; doors, reach zones and furniture orientation recombine.
**Demo:** three compact before/after rooms.
**Failure hypothesis:** can collapse into ordinary packing/CSP if objects lack interesting use relationships.

### D. One-Way Workshop
**Hook:** Every cut gives you the part you wanted — and an offcut you are required to use as a later tool/jig.
**Core:** choose cuts from discrete stock; resulting offcuts become spacers, gauges, supports or templates required downstream.
**Depth:** dual-purpose planning; current subtraction creates future capability rather than waste.
**Demo:** build a small wooden mechanism in 4–6 cuts.
**Failure hypothesis:** cut-space may explode or become brute-force unless action vocabulary stays very small.

### E. Unpacking Order
**Hook:** Pack a case so its contents can later be extracted in a demanded order without moving anything else.
**Core:** discrete rigid objects, allowed extraction vectors, occlusion/blocking graph; final packing is judged by sequential removability.
**Depth:** accessibility changes as objects leave, creating designed dependency structures.
**Demo:** suitcase/toolcase with 5 objects and a surprising requested extraction order.
**Failure hypothesis:** risks becoming generic 3D packing; must foreground extraction proof rather than density.

### F. Quiet Please
**Hook:** Place clockwork appliances so their loud moments never happen together.
**Core:** each device has a short visible repeating cycle; placement changes phase through mechanical drive sockets; solve noise-window constraints.
**Depth:** cycle composition, harmonics, shared drive choices, spatial acoustic zones.
**Demo:** make a tiny apartment pass a nighttime noise inspection.
**Failure hypothesis:** could drift toward #011 cyclic phase relationships; likely portfolio collision on deeper inspection.

### G. Borrowed Floor
**Hook:** Build an arrangement using temporary props, then remove every prop without anything becoming unsupported.
**Core:** place final objects plus removable support jigs; removal is discrete and gravity/support logic is deterministic.
**Depth:** temporary scaffolding creates an assembly-order puzzle; final state alone is insufficient.
**Demo:** assemble a small shop display/structure and pull the last prop.
**Failure hypothesis:** physics expectations may conflict with discrete support rules; construction theme can overscope assets.

### H. Lost Motion
**Hook:** Make a machine work by deliberately adding slop.
**Core:** cams/gears/linkages use discrete backlash intervals; output requirements often need delays/deadzones rather than perfect transmission.
**Depth:** intentional non-response, direction reversal, stacked slack and synchronization.
**Demo:** one absurd tabletop automaton where adding looseness fixes it.
**Failure hypothesis:** communicating backlash state may be difficult; mechanical animation burden moderate.

### I. The Long Drawer
**Hook:** A cabinet where opening one drawer physically changes which other drawers can open.
**Core:** telescoping drawers occupy shared depth lanes; handles/latches/reach points appear at extension states; reach target tools in sequence.
**Depth:** extension is both access and obstruction; nested/side-access variants create stateful spatial planning.
**Demo:** recover three requested tools from a bizarre compact cabinet.
**Failure hypothesis:** finite state may invite blind search unless information and solution-space structure support deduction.

### J. Phase Change
**Hook:** Solve a circuit made of materials that change what they conduct when they get hot or cold.
**Core:** discrete thermal bands; pieces transmit/block heat depending on band; sources and sinks update deterministically in turns.
**Depth:** topology changes because state changes conductivity; temporary thermal states can enable later routing.
**Demo:** thaw/start/cool a compact fictional machine.
**Failure hypothesis:** cellular-simulation readability and update-order ambiguity.

### K. Please Hold
**Hook:** Park telephone calls in physical cable loops; loop length determines when each caller returns.
**Core:** turn-based switchboard; calls cycle through visible delay loops and must reconnect to limited operators/resources at useful turns.
**Depth:** delay as spatial resource, loop sharing, priorities, deliberate misses.
**Demo:** five-call comic shift lasting ~10 turns.
**Failure hypothesis:** can become scheduling spreadsheet; theme/UI must make state tactile and human-readable.

### L. Pressure Proof
**Hook:** Every time you test a soft manufactured part, the test permanently changes it.
**Core:** choose among discrete presses/gauges; deformation is deterministic; satisfy final dimensional/contact requirements while diagnostic tests alter shape.
**Depth:** information-gathering actions are also state-changing production actions; test order matters.
**Demo:** certify three strange gasket/block jobs with a final snap-fit reveal.
**Failure hypothesis:** must avoid #013 witness/history identity and #015 persistent-transform feel; needs a distinct deformation grammar.

## 5. Immediate collision warning
Quiet Please is retained only as a control candidate: its cycle/phase heart appears too close to Game #011 and should face a severe Round-A collision penalty. Pressure Proof similarly needs proof that deformation/testing is not merely another persistent-history puzzle. These are not canon.

## 6. Equal destructive Round-A protocol
Score every retained candidate 0–5 on the same dimensions, with written evidence rather than vibes:
1. 10-second hook legibility;
2. core interaction pleasure before puzzle difficulty;
3. second-order depth after 2 hours;
4. hour-10 freshness path;
5. explainable deterministic failure;
6. anti-brute-force structure / human proof route;
7. tutorial burden;
8. demo strength in <=30 minutes;
9. production ratio (systemic depth per bespoke asset/UI/code burden);
10. controller/Deck viability;
11. market differentiation as of Sep-2026;
12. portfolio distance from #001–#015.

Mandatory destructive probes for each:
- write one trivial tutorial puzzle and one late puzzle using the same verbs;
- identify the likely dominant strategy and whether the rules defeat it;
- estimate meaningful state branching rather than raw combinatorics;
- identify what a player learns between attempts;
- state what would make attempt #20 feel different from attempt #3;
- name the ugliest implementation/UX risk;
- compare against at least two current/relevant analogues when market positioning matters;
- kill immediately for portfolio collision, precision dependence, opaque failure, uncontrolled content burden, or a loop whose only depth is enumerating a finite set.

Round A target: reduce 12 to ~5–7 survivors. Do not choose the final game. Survivors proceed to deeper mechanical proofs and market checks in Round B.

## 7. Phase-1 conclusion
Phase 1 produced 30 seeds and 12 provisional survivors without selecting a winner. The strongest unexplored interaction territories at this stage are uncertainty/tolerance manipulation, future extraction accessibility, consumptive fabrication that creates tools, intentional mechanical slack, paired-space furnishing, and state-changing tests. All remain hypotheses until equal Round-A destruction.
