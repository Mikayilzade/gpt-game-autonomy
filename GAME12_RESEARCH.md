## 1. Fresh 2026 market scan

### Current signals
A focused scan of current/recent Steam puzzle/strategy releases shows two simultaneous truths:

1. **Minimalist grid logic remains extremely crowded and cheap.**
   - `Netoo` (2026-03-03) is a free minimalist logic/grid puzzle built from magnets/rotators.
   - `Metanoia` (2026-02-27) is a $4.99 minimalist Sokoban-like puzzle with 24 levels / 5–7 hours and only two base mechanics.
   - `Caber` (2026-05-04) is a $1.99 abstract logic puzzle.
   - `Untangle` (2026-07-16) is another minimalist line-clearing/untangling puzzle.
   - `AfterMove` (2026-08-12) is $8.99, 190 handcrafted levels, and already claims the strong systemic pitch “move one piece, everything else moves with you.”
   - `Puzzle Cottage` (2026-07-31) combines daily deduction puzzles, streak/calendar structure and controller support.

2. **A small vocabulary can still carry a premium product when the causal law is distinctive.**
   - `Shapez 2` reached 1.0 in 2026 and continues to expand depth through recombination of a limited automation vocabulary rather than bespoke narrative content.
   - `Dawnfolk` demonstrates that a compact tile/city-builder can read as both puzzle and strategy while remaining Steam Deck-friendly.
   - `Rhell: Warped Worlds & Troubled Times` markets combinatorial experimentation (“spell segments” that combine into many solutions), but at a much higher 3D/content burden than this factory should normally accept.
   - `Rivage` uses a time-loop mystery wrapper; useful evidence that loops remain legible marketing language, but Game #012 should avoid temporal-loop territory after Missing Step unless structurally unrelated.

3. **Demos/controller clarity remain valuable.** Current Steam listings continue to surface game demos, Steam Cloud, achievements, controller support and Deck suitability prominently. A 10–30 second clip should explain not merely the theme but the causal law.

4. **The wider indie market is noisy.** September 2026 upcoming Steam listings are dense with survival, co-op, RPG and physics-comedy releases. Competing by production volume or trend imitation is unattractive for this factory. A compact single-player systemic object with a visually self-evident transformation remains the safer lane.

## 2. Portfolio exclusion boundaries

Game #012 must not be a reskin or close mechanical sibling of any frozen design.

Hard exclusions derived from #001–#011:
- no living-cargo/ecology cascade whose primary puzzle is post-commit organism interaction (#001);
- no paperwork/map/reality bureaucracy manipulation (#002);
- no central verb of transferring properties between objects (#003);
- no hidden-geometry-via-audio perception puzzle (#004);
- no conserved load/tension redistribution network (#005);
- no topology rewiring where making adjacency consumes/destroys another adjacency (#006);
- no observation/memory loop where an object retains a transformed remembered form (#007);
- no destructive persistent editing of a key/credential vector against lock predicates (#008);
- no flat-sheet/signature assignment solved through fold/nest/trim transforms (#009);
- no moving-carousel/object permutation by fixed adjacent gantries and service predicates (#010);
- no deleting instructions from cyclic programs to re-phase loops (#011).

Additional structural exclusions for this round:
- generic Sokoban with a cosmetic modifier;
- pure line/pipe connection;
- generic automation/factory routing;
- “program robots” instruction sequencing;
- standard deckbuilder/roguelike shell;
- physics sandbox dependent on emergent rigid-body precision;
- multiplayer/social deduction/co-op as the core value proposition;
- first-person environmental puzzle requiring large 3D asset production;
- narrative mystery whose puzzles are secondary to authored story volume;
- daily/streak system as the main differentiation rather than the mechanical law.

## 3. Scoring rubric

Each concept is scored 1–5 on:
- **H** Hook / 10-second explainability
- **D** Same-vocabulary depth
- **S** Scope safety (5 = compact)
- **V** Visual causal legibility
- **M** Market differentiation
- **T** Deterministic/testable implementation
- **C** Content efficiency
- **Demo** Demo strength

Maximum = 40. Score is directional; Phase 1 does not select the winner.

## 4. Broad concept field — 30 concepts

### 01. Margin of Error
You place one tolerance band on a chain of imperfect mechanical gauges; every downstream reading is derived from the previous accepted reading, so deciding where to permit error changes which later measurements count as valid.
Score H4 D4 S5 V4 M5 T5 C5 Demo4 = **36**.
Risk: must avoid becoming dry arithmetic.
**RETAIN.**

### 02. Last Elevator First
A building has elevators with deterministic stop rules. You may designate exactly one floor as “last served”; that single exception changes each car’s next-stop tie breaking and therefore the whole passenger flow.
Score 4/4/4/4/4/5/4/4 = **33**.
Risk: visually busier than ideal.
**RETAIN.**

### 03. Negative Space Clerk
Place a small number of opaque stamps on a document; objectives concern only the connected shapes of the *unstamped* paper left visible. One placement simultaneously creates and destroys void-regions.
Score 5/4/5/5/4/5/5/5 = **38**.
Risk: must stay distinct from map/paper bureaucracy and Binder's Imposition; theme should not be literal office paperwork if selected.
**RETAIN.**

### 04. Wrong-Side Assembly
Components have one functional face and one inert face. You never rotate a component directly; you choose which assembly joints are made from the back, and each joint flips the entire downstream branch orientation.
Score 5/5/4/5/5/5/5/5 = **39**.
Risk: tree orientation must remain readable.
**RETAIN.**

### 05. Shared Spare
Several machines each need one of a few interchangeable spare parts over a known schedule. Assigning a spare permanently changes its future wear state; the puzzle is not routing but choosing which machine inherits which accumulated wear history.
Score 4/4/4/4/4/5/4/4 = **33**.
Risk: property-history flavor can drift toward Borrowed Collision or Locksmith's Margin.
KILL — portfolio adjacency too high.

### 06. The Quiet Majority
A row of devices votes ON/OFF from local neighborhood states. You may silence exactly N devices; silenced units stop voting but still count as physical neighbors, producing non-obvious majority cascades.
Score 5/5/5/5/5/5/5/5 = **40**.
Risk: must prove varied challenge families instead of cellular-automata spectacle.
**RETAIN.**

### 07. Misprint Foundry
Every press duplicates a shape but exactly one printer plate is mirrored. You choose where the defective plate enters a deterministic production tree; all descendants inherit alternating mirror relationships.
Score 4/4/5/5/4/5/5/5 = **37**.
Risk: could look factory-like; keep as finite derivation tree, not logistics automation.
**RETAIN.**

### 08. Debt of Distance
Moving one token consumes distance from a shared finite pool, but unused distance is inherited by the next token in a fixed order. Solve position goals by intentionally underspending early moves.
Score 4/4/5/4/4/5/5/4 = **35**.
Risk: conserved resource resembles Tension Budget at an abstract level but not network redistribution.
**RETAIN cautiously.**

### 09. Witness Protection
A set of cameras sees deterministic sectors. You may cover lenses, not move cameras; a target is safe only when exactly one camera can witness it, while other targets require 0/2/3 witnesses.
Score 5/4/5/5/4/5/5/5 = **38**.
Risk: familiar visibility-set puzzle; needs second-order interaction.
**RETAIN.**

### 10. Thermal Queue
Objects enter an oven queue; each item heats itself and modifies only the *next* item's heating rate. Reorder a fixed queue to hit final temperature bands.
Score 4/4/5/5/3/5/5/4 = **35**.
Risk: is fundamentally permutation/order puzzle; too close to familiar sequencing.
KILL — insufficient structural novelty.

### 11. One Bad Ruler
You have several rulers that measure by snapping to tick marks. Exactly one ruler has a missing tick; choose which measurement uses it so all subsequent cut positions, measured from previous cuts, satisfy the final silhouette.
Score 5/5/5/5/5/5/5/5 = **40**.
Risk: arithmetic can dominate unless silhouette feedback is immediate.
**RETAIN.**

### 12. Return Address
Parcels pass through deterministic “return if predicate” checkpoints. You can mark a limited set as already-returned; that metadata changes which later checkpoints are eligible, producing route changes without editing the network.
Score 4/4/4/4/4/5/4/4 = **33**.
Risk: label/predicate transport territory overlaps Luggage Carousel Zero.
KILL — portfolio collision.

### 13. Counterweight Jury

### 14. Ink Bleed

### 15. Public Secret

### 16. Broken Compass

### 17. Window Tax

### 18. Priority Leak

### 20. Second-Hand Blueprint

### 21. Air Gap

### 22. Spare Chair

### 23. Echo Invoice

### 24. Dry Dock Draft

### 25. The Unsent Letter

### 26. Cracked Mold

### 27. Overbooked Orbit

### 29. Fallow Pattern
A field has plots with deterministic depletion/recovery: planting crop X changes the future allowed crops of neighboring plots, but the puzzle presents a short finite season schedule. Choose only the *empty* plots each season.

### 30. Courtesy Gap

Risk: must distinguish from carousel permutation by using encounter-order inversions over continuous-looking but discrete event time, not adjacent gantry swaps.

## 5. Phase-1 survivors — 12 candidates

These are not ranked winners; all enter destructive Round A:

1. **Margin of Error** — tolerance placement in chained measurements.
2. **Negative Space Clerk** — solve the topology/shape of what remains unstamped.
3. **Wrong-Side Assembly** — downstream branch flips caused by backside joints.
4. **The Quiet Majority** — silence voters while their bodies remain neighbors.
5. **Misprint Foundry** — route one mirrored defect through a finite derivation tree.
6. **Debt of Distance** — unused movement allowance passes to the next actor.
7. **Witness Protection** — satisfy exact witness-count predicates by covering lenses.
8. **One Bad Ruler** — one missing measurement tick changes downstream cuts.
9. **Ink Bleed** — order-dependent deterministic spreading where earlier wet regions block later spread.
10. **Public Secret** — deterministic local fact propagation under initial redaction.
11. **Spare Chair** — remove seats to force a different deterministic seating equilibrium.
12. **Courtesy Gap** — insert limited yield events that invert encounter order and alter all later encounters.

### Wildcards not in the main 12
- **Priority Leak**: excellent structural novelty but weak visual language; keep only as a reserve if Round A exposes several false positives.
- **Second-Hand Blueprint**: strong hook/depth but graph-routing collapse risk.
- **Dry Dock Draft**: strong fantasy but discrete buoyancy model may feel fake or expand presentation burden.
- **Cracked Mold**: strong visual potential but likely permutation-search dominance.

## 6. Why these 12 survived

The survivor field intentionally spans different causal structures rather than 12 themes over one puzzle:
- chained numeric derivation;
- complement/negative-space geometry;
- tree orientation parity;
- local voting dynamics;
- derivation-tree inheritance;
- sequential resource inheritance;
- visibility-set predicates;
- measurement corruption;
- history-sensitive flood fill;
- information propagation;
- stable allocation/equilibrium;
- encounter-order dynamics.

No candidate is canon yet. Similar-looking candidates must be killed aggressively rather than merged into a mechanic zoo.

## 7. Phase-2 Round A destructive tests

For each of the 12, next run must create **two concrete tiny cases** and attack:
1. Can a player predict cause/effect after one tutorial case?
2. Does the second case create a genuinely new reasoning pattern from the same verb?
3. Can the state be shown without a wall of numbers/arrows/text?
4. Is there a deterministic finite solver/validator with manageable state space?
5. Does brute enumeration dominate before human reasoning becomes interesting?
6. Is the concept still distinct after stripping theme/art?
7. Does it collide structurally with Games #001–#011?
8. Can 30+ quality cases plausibly exist without introducing a new mechanic every few levels?
9. Can a 10–30 second trailer clip show the law and its cascade?
10. Can the interaction remain comfortable on controller/Steam Deck?

Round A should cut **12 -> roughly 5–7**, not protect a fixed number. Any candidate failing structural novelty, content depth or causal readability dies even if its aggregate Phase-1 score was high.
