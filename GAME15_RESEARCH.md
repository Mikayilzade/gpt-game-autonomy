## 1. September 2026 market read

Fresh research was performed before concept generation.

### Discovery pressure
- June 2026 Steam Next Fest was extraordinarily crowded: contemporary reporting counted thousands of demos (one GamesRadar report counted 8,682), making a weak genre label or slow-to-explain premise dangerous.
- Curated Next Fest coverage repeatedly elevated games with a highly communicable interaction/premise rather than merely broad feature volume: Screenbound's 2D/3D crossover, Shroom and Gloom's dual-deck first-person structure, Chained Beasts' literal shared chain, Woodo's assembled dioramas, and strange mundane fantasies such as Worming from Home.
- Dark fantasy, horror, co-op and Souls/extraction language remain highly visible and therefore expensive fields in which to differentiate without production scale. Game #015 should not enter those fields merely because they are popular.
- Puzzle competition at the high end is formidable: Order of the Sinking Star is being presented around 1,000+ handcrafted puzzles, while Blue Prince remains a reference point for large mystery/puzzle discovery. A small project should not compete by puzzle count or sprawling metapuzzle volume.
- Demo progress carry-over is explicitly praised in current Next Fest coverage. A bounded premium design should make its demo feel like the beginning of the real product rather than a disposable tutorial branch.
- Current indie coverage still makes room for compact, bizarre, immediately readable fantasies. This supports seeking a visual/verb hook that can survive a crowded discovery page.

### Commercial implications
1. Prefer a premise understandable before genre taxonomy.
2. Need a screenshot/GIF state change, not only an abstract rules claim.
3. Do not win through 1000-level content promises, giant narrative mystery, co-op virality, horror streamer bait, or roguelite/deckbuilder dressing.
4. A finite premium PC/Steam product with a meaningful same-rules demo remains plausible; exact price is deliberately deferred until a winner exists.
5. Controller/handheld friendliness is a useful default but not a Phase-1 requirement if a candidate earns mouse-first interaction.
6. Systemic recombination should carry depth while authored content supplies pacing and surprise.

## 4. Phase-1 survivors — 12 candidates

No ranking implies selection. Each receives the same Round-A attack next.

### A. WRONG SCALE
**Pitch:** Move and swap measurement legends in a miniature workshop; the world physically resizes to what its nearby scale claims, and the goal is to make several incompatible-looking objects fit and function at once.
**GIF:** slide a `1:10` ruler card from a door to a key; key grows while door snaps small.
**Depth candidate:** shared scale domains, interfaces between differently scaled zones, conservation rules for contained objects.
**Failure hypothesis:** becomes arbitrary magic/property reassignment or trivial scale-key matching.

### B. CARBON COPY
**Pitch:** Build stacks of forms, carbon sheets and blockers so one press of a pen produces different required records on different layers.
**GIF:** draw one stroke, explode stack, reveal different marks per sheet.
**Depth candidate:** propagation depth, masks/backings, stack reorder, pressure classes, multi-stroke interference.
**Failure hypothesis:** collapses into bitmask/set-cover arithmetic with weak embodied reasoning.

### C. BEFORE IT DRIES
**Pitch:** Compose an image with a few wet strokes that can slide, smear or transfer pigment only during their short physical wet window.
**GIF:** move one wet strip through another and both visibly change.
**Depth candidate:** contact order, drying locks, transfer direction, negative-space preservation.
**Failure hypothesis:** timing turns into fiddly execution instead of thinkable state manipulation.

### D. ONE-WAY GLASSHOUSE
**Pitch:** Stack and rotate privacy/polarizing films so people on opposite sides of several windows get intentionally different views.
**GIF:** rotate one film and two observers' visibility states diverge.
**Depth candidate:** side-dependent transmission, layer order, shared panes/viewpoints.
**Failure hypothesis:** real polarization physics is unintuitive or abstraction becomes truth-table matching.

### E. FRESH COAT
**Pitch:** Stack/nest a handful of plain objects, spray only currently exposed faces, then unpack them into a demanded final paint pattern.
**GIF:** spray a pile uniformly, unpack into unexpectedly different face patterns.
**Depth candidate:** occlusion as mask, nesting, orientation, multiple coats, protected faces.
**Failure hypothesis:** degenerates into reverse packing or requires too many arbitrary shapes.

### F. PUNCH LIST
**Pitch:** Inspect a building by placing transparent annotated plan overlays; flip/rotate/register them so every mark points to a real defect and no sound feature is falsely condemned.
**GIF:** slide acetate and four red circles suddenly align to four visible defects.
**Depth candidate:** transforms, shared landmarks, sparse marks, multi-floor correspondences.
**Failure hypothesis:** becomes simple overlay matching with insufficient hour-3 evolution.

### G. PARALLAX BUREAU
**Pitch:** Arrange a few chunky signs/objects once so several fixed inspection cameras each see a different required icon or short reading through perspective overlap.
**GIF:** same scene shown from three cameras spells/depicts three different targets.
**Depth candidate:** occlusion, depth, shared elements, multi-view conflicts, partial glyph reuse.
**Failure hypothesis:** authoring/certification becomes 3D search and interaction feels imprecise.

### H. MISALIGNED
**Pitch:** Deliberately misregister a tiny set of print plates so their overlaps create the requested final symbol while forbidden ink combinations stay absent.
**GIF:** nudge cyan/magenta plates and a new composite image appears.
**Depth candidate:** plate transform, overlap colors, knockout regions, order if physically justified.
**Failure hypothesis:** merely pixel overlay matching or too close to generic image compositing.

### I. SOFT FOCUS
**Pitch:** Build a layered tabletop scene where changing only camera focus reveals different required silhouettes/messages at fixed focus distances.
**GIF:** rack focus and the apparent readable shape changes without scene movement.
**Depth candidate:** depth layers, blur footprint, occlusion, shared fragments across focus planes.
**Failure hypothesis:** renderer-dependent ambiguity and inaccessible visual precision.

### J. CONTACT SHEET
**Pitch:** Build one small still-life; a fixed list of camera crops and rotations must each photograph a different demanded composition without moving the scene between shots.
**GIF:** zoom/crop through one scene to reveal three convincing target images.
**Depth candidate:** shared objects serving multiple crops, occlusion, negative space, camera-frame constraints.
**Failure hypothesis:** solution search becomes tedious placement pixel-hunting rather than deduction.

### K. UNDER PRESSURE
**Pitch:** Stack embossed plates, spacers and soft sheets so one press transfers different relief patterns to different depths of a physical stack.
**GIF:** press stack, peel layers, reveal different imprints.
**Depth candidate:** relief height, attenuation depth, blockers, orientation, reusable plate fragments.
**Failure hypothesis:** carbon-copy sibling with less legibility; may collapse to layer bitmasks.

### L. STENCIL DEBT
**Pitch:** Print a short edition with reusable stencils whose apertures visibly clog after each pass, so every print changes the tool available for the next.
**GIF:** stencil makes a clean first mark, then blocked apertures alter later impressions.
**Depth candidate:** controlled irreversible tool degradation, order, cleaning budget, shared apertures.
**Failure hypothesis:** sequence permutation dominates and the physical premise feels contrived.

`PACKING SLIP` is first alternate: attractive wraparound-label visual but currently judged shallower than the 12 above.

## 5. Equal Round-A attack protocol

Every survivor must be attacked under the same questions before any preference ranking:

1. **10-second legibility:** can a silent clip show input -> surprising consequence -> goal?
2. **First 5 minutes:** can the player learn the central causal rule without text-heavy explanation?
3. **Minute-to-minute agency:** does a move expose useful information/change state, or is play mostly blind permutation?
4. **Hour-3 depth:** name at least four qualitatively different reasoning pressures produced by the same core grammar, not extra minigames.
5. **Anti-bruteforce:** estimate discrete action/state growth and determine whether trial spam dominates human reasoning.
6. **Failure readability:** after a check/failure, can the player explain what constraint failed without solver-oracle UI?
7. **Content factory:** can 20–30 strong cases be authored/validated from a bounded vocabulary without bespoke art for every case?
8. **Certification:** can legality/solution/equivalence/repetition be tested deterministically enough for another implementation session?
9. **Input:** controller/handheld path or a compelling reason to remain pointer-first.
10. **Accessibility:** does the concept survive non-color cues, scalable UI and reduced motion; does it depend on unusually fine vision/hearing/timing?
11. **Market collision:** search exact mechanic/fantasy analogues and kill if the hook is already occupied without a second-order differentiator.
12. **Production ratio:** does its marketing payoff justify rendering, physics, content, UI and QA burden for a bounded solo/small-team build?
13. **Demo cut:** can 15–30 minutes end on a meaningful escalation while using exactly the full game's grammar?
14. **Repetition signature:** define what two cases would feel cognitively identical even if their assets differ.
15. **Portfolio collision:** re-check #001–#014 after mechanics become concrete.

### Round-A kill rule
A candidate dies immediately for a hard portfolio collision, fundamentally unreadable failure, unavoidable precision/timing burden, or depth that demonstrably reduces to blind permutation/set cover/parameter inflation. Softer risks receive explicit validation obligations, not optimism.
