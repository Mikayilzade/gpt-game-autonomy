## 0. Clean-slate boundary
Game #018 inherits no mechanics, fiction, title, content, or product assumptions from Games #001–#017. The frozen portfolio is used only as an exclusion/collision set.

## 1. September 2026 market read
Fresh research was used for current context. Signals are directional, not sales forecasts.

- Discovery is extremely crowded: June 2026 Steam Next Fest reportedly exposed more than 4,000 demos, making immediate legibility and a strong playable demo unusually important.
- 2026 indie coverage remains crowded with cozy simulation, horror, roguelike hybrids, automation/crafting, metroidvania/action and narrative exploration. These surfaces can still work, but a generic entry has weak discovery odds.
- Puzzle games remain commercially/critically visible when they have a strong system identity: Blue Prince carried puzzle-adventure/roguelike momentum from 2025 into 2026; Order of the Sinking Star foregrounds several interacting puzzle systems; The Artisan of Glimmith represents polished handcrafted grid logic. This increases the bar for another abstract grid/Sokoban-like product.
- Hybridization is common: current examples combine word puzzle + roguelike deckbuilding, match-3 + tower defense + roguelike, and automation + town-building/clicker loops. “Genre A + genre B” alone is therefore not a sufficient hook.
- Demos remain strategically important for mechanics that are easier to feel than explain. Game #018 should prefer a concept whose central surprise is demonstrable in 10–30 seconds and genuinely playable inside 20–40 minutes.

Sources consulted 2026-09-06 include recent GamesRadar/PC Gamer 2026 indie coverage, June 2026 Steam Next Fest roundups, current coverage of Order of the Sinking Star, Blue Prince, Titanium Court, Beyond Words, Scriptorium, Terrafactor and community Next Fest discovery discussion.

1. Generic cozy shop/farm/cafe/restoration simulator.
2. Generic survivor-like / bullet heaven.
3. Roguelike deckbuilder whose novelty is mostly theme/cards.
4. Sokoban/grid-pushing with one additional tile rule.
5. Generic factory/automation throughput game.
6. Liminal/PS1 horror whose primary hook is atmosphere.
7. Extraction/co-op survival requiring networking/content scale.
8. Metroidvania/action platformer requiring large animation/combat/world burden.
9. Open-world crafting/survival.
10. Detective game whose depth depends on large authored dialogue/lore volume.
11. Physics sandbox whose fun depends on expensive content/QA rather than a bounded rule system.
12. “Job simulator” whose trailer is satisfying but whose hour-3 play is repetitive manual labor.
13. Pure UI/spreadsheet puzzle without a strong embodied visual metaphor.
14. Huge procedural promise before a compact handcrafted vertical slice proves the mechanic.
15. Multiplayer-first social deduction: server/network/population risk disproportionate to factory goals.

## 4. Broad field — 28 clean-slate concepts
These were generated before preference filtering.

### C01 — LAST LIGHTHOUSE SHIFT
Operate a lighthouse whose rotating beam physically reveals only the reflective navigation marks it currently hits. Ships follow deterministic rules based on the sequence of marks seen; rotate/occlude lenses to encode safe routes through moving hazards. Depth: temporal signaling language. Demo: one beam sweep redirects three ships differently. Failure: could drift toward #004/#014 if light becomes hidden-geometry deduction rather than communication.

### C02 — SECOND-HAND WEATHER
Tiny islands exchange weather fronts as tradable physical parcels: capture rain/wind/fog into bounded containers, release them elsewhere, and satisfy ecological contracts while every release changes neighboring parcels. Depth: conservation + sequencing. Demo: move a storm to grow one island but its departing pressure creates wind for another. Failure: simulation readability.

### C03 — THE ROOM AFTER YOU
You arrange a room for a person who will enter only after you leave. Their deterministic routine reacts to object affordances and leaves a transformed room for the next visitor. Goal is to choreograph a chain of absent interactions. Depth: indirect planning across visitors. Demo: chair placement causes visitor A to move a lamp, enabling B to reach a shelf. Failure: can resemble generic NPC automation if rules are opaque.

### C04 — BORROWED GRAVITY
Small diorama puzzle where each object can temporarily nominate another object as its local “down.” Gravity relationships create rotations/falls without changing geometry or topology. Depth: relational orientation chains. Demo: a cup falls sideways toward a clock while the clock falls toward the floor. Failure: physics QA; must be discrete/deterministic.

### C05 — NIGHT JANITOR OF THE MUSEUM
After closing, exhibits obey their own simple movement/display rules when unseen. You control shutters, lights and viewing windows to ensure the morning arrangement satisfies curatorial constraints. Depth: visibility as permission, not observation/memory. Demo: close one shutter and three exhibits legally reposition. Failure: collision with observation themes unless strictly about action permission.

### C06 — COMMON GROUND
Place a limited set of public amenities between tiny neighborhoods. Residents do not move houses; instead each amenity changes which pairs of neighborhoods share a “reason to meet.” Objectives concern overlap patterns, not land value or economy. Depth: hypergraph/social intersection construction. Demo: one bench creates a three-way overlap while breaking exclusivity. Failure: abstract UI risk.

### C07 — ECHO KITCHEN
A compact kitchen records only the timing pattern of your last preparation cycle; on the next cycle ghost appliances repeat those timings while you cook a different order. Build multi-cycle choreography. Depth: temporal layering, not cyclic-step deletion. Demo: previous-cycle mixer starts itself while current-you uses the oven. Failure: execution stress; should be pause/plan deterministic.

### C08 — HOUSE OF DRAFTS
Air moves through a cutaway house using simple discrete pressure rules. Doors, vents and warm/cold sources create airflow that carries smell, smoke, seeds or sound tokens to target rooms. Depth: flow fields with multiple token properties. Demo: opening one transom reverses a whole-house draft. Failure: can become generic network-flow puzzle.

### C09 — FALSE BOTTOM
Objects in drawers/boxes have two legal storage depths. Closing a container compresses its contents according to visible rules; stacked containers can transfer pressure to hidden compartments. Goal: pack/unpack to expose requested objects in order. Depth: nested compression state. Demo: closing a suitcase raises a false-bottom compartment elsewhere. Failure: spatial rule explanation.

### C10 — WITNESS GARDEN
Plants grow into deterministic shapes based on which neighboring plants were present during each growth stage. You can transplant only between stages; final garden must encode a requested pattern in growth scars. Depth: developmental history as constructive material. Demo: remove a neighbor and the next ring visibly changes. Failure: collision with #007/#013 if scars become forensic evidence rather than construction.

### C11 — THE QUIET AUCTION
You cannot set bids. You arrange lots, lot order and public bundle boundaries; deterministic bidders value combinations differently and reveal demand through bids. Objective is to achieve allocation/fairness constraints, not maximize money. Depth: mechanism design via packaging. Demo: split one bundle and the entire winner pattern changes. Failure: collision with #017 if framed as diagnosing hidden bidder types; keep valuations public and puzzle about allocation.

### C12 — PAPER MOON CONTROL
A stage technician operates layered flat scenery on rails. Actors follow fixed marks, but scenery changes which world-space locations are visually represented as doors, cliffs, windows, etc. Solve performances by swapping semantic backdrops at cues. Depth: same physical path, changing scene meaning. Demo: actor walks through “door” because the backdrop changes at the beat. Failure: content/animation burden.

### C13 — ONE MORE CHAIR
A dinner table has strict local etiquette rules: each guest reacts only to adjacent place settings, dishes and empty seats. You cannot move seated guests, only insert/remove a tiny number of chairs/settings between courses. Depth: local-rule propagation across rounds. Demo: one empty chair changes passing direction and dish ownership around table. Failure: could become abstract adjacency puzzle; ensure embodied comedy.

### C14 — FLOODLINE ARCHIVE
You run a tiny archive during a slowly rising flood. Documents themselves are waterproof, but shelves/boxes change buoyancy and become bridges, anchors or floating carriers. Plan retrieval using water level as deterministic phase progression. Depth: changing affordances across a known environmental schedule. Demo: a shelf stops being storage and becomes a ferry. Failure: production physics; discretize levels/positions.

### C15 — SIGNAL CLOTH
Stretch a finite cloth over pegs. Printed symbols on the cloth become aligned/overlapped only under certain tension-free folds/stretches; aligned symbols define commands for tiny machines underneath. Depth: deformation as programming interface. Demo: pull one corner and three printed fragments form a valid icon. Failure: collision with #009/fabric geometry; reject if fold/nest dominates.

### C16 — THE DOOR IS BUSY
A building has too few doors for all room-to-room activities. Doors can be reassigned between wall sockets only at discrete breaks; occupants have public schedules and route rules. Design door placement over time so all activities remain possible. Depth: temporal connectivity without topology-rewire puzzle emphasis. Demo: lunch break moves the only door from kitchen to clinic. Failure: strong collision with #006; likely kill.

### C17 — LOST PROPERTY ORCHESTRA
Found objects emit simple rhythmic patterns when placed near certain material surfaces. Arrange mundane objects on a desk to satisfy musical timing constraints and trigger mechanical devices. Depth: rhythm as deterministic logic, not performance. Demo: keys + mug + radiator form a repeating actuator sequence. Failure: audio dependence/accessibility; visual rhythm must be complete.

### C18 — SOFT DEADLINE
A tiny office processes tasks whose deadlines are not clocks but conditions (“before red file is opened,” “after two approvals,” “while desk B is empty”). You choose task execution order to satisfy relational deadlines. Depth: partial-order scheduling with visual office metaphor. Demo: one action simultaneously makes three conditions true/false. Failure: bureaucracy collision with #002; likely kill unless fiction changes.

### C19 — TIDE TABLE FOR TWO
Two shorelines share one deterministic tide but have differently shaped terrain. You place only a few temporary objects before each tide step; as water rises/falls, the same object changes role as bridge, dam, float or weight. Depth: reversible environmental phase affordances. Demo: falling tide strands a raft exactly where it becomes a bridge. Failure: resembles generic water physics unless discrete and crisp.

### C20 — WINDOW DRESSER
Arrange products behind layered shop windows. Pedestrians have public sightline preferences; objective is to make different products visible from different sidewalk positions without moving the products after opening. Depth: occlusion composition. Demo: one rotating display reveals three different silhouettes from three viewpoints. Failure: shadow/visibility collision with #014; keep direct occlusion, not light projection/classification.

### C21 — UNFINISHED SENTENCES
A machine executes instruction cards whose verbs are fixed but objects are omitted. Physical tokens passing under each card fill the missing noun at execution time. Route tokens so the same short program performs different operations over time. Depth: dataflow through incomplete commands. Demo: “MOVE ___ TO B” successively moves key, box, then itself. Failure: could resemble programming puzzle market saturation; hook is strong if physicalized.

### C22 — KEEP THE CHANGE
A market puzzle where every purchase must be paid exactly, but coins have public side effects when they change hands: some split, fuse, flip denomination or become coupons. You choose transaction order, not prices. Depth: transactional state transformation. Demo: paying 7 creates the exact 5 needed for the next shop. Failure: can become dry arithmetic; transformations must be visual/simple.

### C23 — AFTERIMAGE DELIVERY
Courier moves through a tiny city where each street retains one temporary “afterimage” of the last vehicle direction; certain gates/vehicles respond to those arrows. Plan routes that write infrastructure for later routes. Depth: route as state-writing. Demo: first delivery paints directional traces that open second delivery’s path. Failure: could become generic path puzzle; needs non-grid embodied presentation.

### C24 — THE WEIGHT OF NAMES
Warehouse objects have public physical weight, but labels add handling rules rather than changing properties. You may relabel only a few objects; cranes/floors deterministically obey the label protocol. Solve by changing institutional treatment of unchanged objects. Depth: semantics vs physics. Demo: same heavy crate becomes “fragile” and takes a different conveyor protocol. Failure: label-manipulation collision with #010/#002; likely kill.

### C25 — GREENHOUSE AT MIDNIGHT
Plants emit/consume heat, moisture and light in discrete nightly pulses. You rotate a small greenhouse carousel between pulses so plants condition one another’s microclimates. Depth: cyclic ecological choreography with spatial neighborhoods. Demo: cactus dries a tray that then cools orchids next pulse. Failure: can look like cozy gardening sim; must foreground exact puzzle loop.

### C26 — HANDOVER
You operate a workbench shared by three workers on staggered shifts. Each worker can perform only a tiny public verb set and leaves tools/materials exactly where their shift ends. Plan handovers so later workers exploit earlier incomplete work. Depth: capability partition + stateful relay. Demo: worker A cannot finish the object but deliberately leaves it clamped upside-down so B can use a different tool. Failure: collision with #016 if fabrication/cutting dominates; choose non-fabrication tasks if retained.

### C27 — LOCAL TIME
A diorama town has several clocks, each defining time only for objects physically inside its small zone. Move clocks, not objects, to make public schedules line up: bread rises, bridge opens, flowers close, train departs. Depth: spatially local temporal state. Demo: slide “8:00” zone over bakery while station remains at 7:55. Failure: paradox complexity; rules must forbid retrocausality and use discrete states.

### C28 — MISSING MIDDLE
Every machine has visible input and output, but its middle operation is selected by the physical spacer inserted between them. You have few reusable spacers and must infer/compose transformations through examples, then route objects through a workshop. Depth: compositional transformation grammar. Demo: same input/output shell behaves differently when a triangular spacer is inserted. Failure: if inference becomes hidden-rule guessing; operations must become fully public after introduction.

## 6. Round-A retained field — 12 equal-format briefs

### A1 — LOCAL TIME (C27)
Hook: move zones of local time around a tiny town so processes that share space but not time can line up.
Core interaction: reposition a few clock-zones between discrete beats; objects inside adopt that zone's current public time-state.
Depth source: overlapping schedules, zone size, process durations, handoffs between differently timed areas, no retrocausality.
Demo moment: bakery reaches 8:00 while the adjacent station stays at 7:55, then the time-zone slides onto the bridge control.
Primary failure hypothesis: rule complexity creates paradox questions faster than puzzles.

### A2 — UNFINISHED SENTENCES (C21)
Hook: instruction cards are missing their nouns; the object physically passing under a blank completes the command.
Core interaction: route tokens through a short fixed program so each blank binds to a different object at execution.
Depth source: dataflow, aliasing, self-reference bounded by strict deterministic rules, reusable command grammar.
Demo moment: one printed instruction performs three visibly different operations because three objects occupy its blank at different beats.
Primary failure hypothesis: becomes a programmer-only abstraction or generic Zach-like automation.

### A3 — THE ROOM AFTER YOU (C03)
Hook: arrange a room, leave, and watch a chain of visitors transform it for one another without ever meeting you.
Core interaction: pre-place/move a few affordance-rich objects, then resolve deterministic visitor routines.
Depth source: indirect action chains, persistent room state, visitors with small public priority lists.
Demo moment: your chair placement causes visitor A to move a lamp, which makes visitor B notice a shelf and leave the required object for C.
Primary failure hypothesis: rules feel arbitrary unless visitor behavior is extremely legible.

### A4 — KEEP THE CHANGE (C22)
Hook: solve a shopping trip where money mutates whenever it changes hands.
Core interaction: choose transaction order and exact tender from a tiny wallet; public coin rules transform returned currency.
Depth source: transaction sequencing, split/fuse/flip transformations, coupons, exact-payment constraints.
Demo moment: an awkward first purchase intentionally creates the denomination needed for two later shops.
Primary failure hypothesis: perceived as arithmetic homework instead of playful systemic puzzle.

### A5 — BORROWED GRAVITY (C04)
Hook: objects choose other objects as “down.”
Core interaction: assign a bounded set of gravity-parent relationships, then resolve deterministic discrete falls/rotations.
Depth source: relational orientation chains, support order, moving reference objects, limited reassignment.
Demo moment: a cup falls toward a clock while the clock falls toward the floor, producing a two-stage route.
Primary failure hypothesis: implementation/visual edge cases overwhelm the elegant rule.

### A6 — TIDE TABLE FOR TWO (C19)
Hook: plan with a tide that turns the same object into a different tool at each water level.
Core interaction: place/reposition a few objects only at safe planning windows, then advance deterministic tide stages.
Depth source: phase-dependent affordances, two terrain profiles sharing one tide, cross-shore sequencing.
Demo moment: a raft floats, strands, then becomes a bridge exactly where the next falling tide needs it.
Primary failure hypothesis: water-state semantics become bespoke exceptions rather than a small grammar.

### A7 — SECOND-HAND WEATHER (C02)
Hook: weather is a finite thing you can capture, carry and release between tiny islands.
Core interaction: bottle bounded rain/wind/fog parcels and release them into public island states.
Depth source: conservation, side effects, ecological timing, container scarcity, neighboring pressure relationships.
Demo moment: stealing rain from one island creates the wind needed to pollinate another before the rain is released on a third.
Primary failure hypothesis: simulation feels like resource shuffling without enough spatial drama.

### A8 — ONE MORE CHAIR (C13)
Hook: change a dinner by changing the empty seats, not the guests.
Core interaction: between courses insert/remove a few chairs/place settings; local etiquette rules deterministically propagate dishes/actions.
Depth source: adjacency, empty-seat buffers, passing direction, course-to-course persistent table state.
Demo moment: one empty chair flips who receives a dish around half the table.
Primary failure hypothesis: too close to abstract circular logic puzzle after novelty fades.

### A9 — AFTERIMAGE DELIVERY (C23)
Hook: every route writes arrows into the streets for the next route.
Core interaction: plan one courier path, resolve it, then exploit its temporary directional afterimages with later couriers/gates.
Depth source: route-as-infrastructure, decay timing, multiple deliveries, conflicting traces.
Demo moment: first van takes a deliberately longer path solely to write the route that makes the second delivery possible.
Primary failure hypothesis: route planning lacks enough second-order depth without adding clutter.

### A10 — HOUSE OF DRAFTS (C08)
Hook: open one vent and make a whole cutaway house breathe differently.
Core interaction: toggle a few doors/vents/heat sources, resolve discrete airflow, carry typed tokens to targets.
Depth source: pressure hierarchy, reversible flows, token-specific effects, sequential room states.
Demo moment: opening a transom reverses two corridors and carries smoke away while pulling a seed toward a greenhouse.
Primary failure hypothesis: generic network-flow puzzle hidden under house art.

### A11 — FALSE BOTTOM (C09)
Hook: closing containers changes the storage geometry inside them.
Core interaction: pack items at one of two legal depths, close/open nested containers, exploit deterministic compression/pressure transfers.
Depth source: nested state, closure order, false compartments, retrieval sequence.
Demo moment: closing a suitcase pushes one object down and raises a hidden tray in a different nested compartment.
Primary failure hypothesis: spatial rules are hard to read in a trailer and may require bespoke cases.

### A12 — COMMON GROUND (C06)
Hook: build social overlap, not a city: one amenity changes which neighborhoods have a reason to meet.
Core interaction: place a few public amenities whose public attraction sets create pair/triple intersections.
Depth source: hypergraph construction, exclusivity vs overlap, limited placements, multi-objective social patterns.
Demo moment: one tiny playground creates the requested three-way meeting while accidentally destroying a required exclusive pair.
Primary failure hypothesis: concept is intellectually distinct but visually too abstract to sell.
